HEDGING PROTECTION FROM BLACK-SWAN MOVES

Hedging = buying insurance on your position. 95% of retail option buyers don't hedge because it reduces max profit. That's exactly why blown-up accounts hit −80% drawdowns overnight. A ₹500 hedge can save a ₹10,000 loss on an event day.
WHY YOU MUST LEARN TO HEDGE
CAPPED LOSS
KNOW YOUR MAX LOSS
A hedged trade has a pre-defined maximum loss. You know exactly what you can lose, no matter what the index does. No overnight panic, no gap shocks.
LOWER MARGIN
UP TO 70% LESS
A hedged short position (e.g., Bull Call Spread instead of a naked short Call) requires up to 70% less margin. Brings F&O strategies within retail capital limits.
SLEEP AT NIGHT
OVERNIGHT SAFETY
Overnight gaps can evaporate premium. A hedge capping risk means you can hold positions overnight without waking to a 50% loss on gap-down.
EVENT PROTECTION
RBI / BUDGET / FED
RBI meetings, budget, Fed, election results — unhedged options can triple or halve in minutes. Hedge before every scheduled event without exception.
IV CRUSH DEFENCE
VEGA NEUTRAL-ISH
When you sell a further-OTM option against a long one, IV crush hurts both — but net impact is much smaller. Critical protection before results / budget / RBI.
THE TRADEOFF
CAPPED PROFIT
Hedging reduces your max profit. Accept it. "Limited profit + known risk" beats "unlimited profit + blown account" every single time over a career.
THE 4 HEDGES EVERY OPTION BUYER SHOULD KNOW
These 4 structures cover 95% of retail hedging needs. Start with Bull/Bear Call/Put Spreads — they are the most beginner-friendly and used daily on Nifty.
1 BULL CALL SPREAD (LIMITED RISK, LIMITED REWARD)
BULLISH VIEW · BEGINNER FRIENDLY
What it is: Buy an ATM/slightly-ITM Call + Sell a further-OTM Call (same expiry).

When to use: you think the index will move up moderately (not explosively). You want a directional bet with a hard cap on loss.

Example — Nifty at 24,400:
• Buy 24,400 CE @ ₹120
• Sell 24,600 CE @ ₹60
• Net debit = ₹60 × 65 lot = ₹3,900 max loss
• Max profit = (200 − 60) × 65 = ₹9,100
• Breakeven at 24,460 spot

Greek effect: partially theta-neutral — the sold Call's theta offsets your bought Call's theta. You bleed far less overnight than a naked long CE.
2 BEAR PUT SPREAD (LIMITED RISK, LIMITED REWARD)
BEARISH VIEW · BEGINNER FRIENDLY
What it is: Buy an ATM/slightly-ITM Put + Sell a further-OTM Put (same expiry).

When to use: you think the index will move down moderately. Same logic as Bull Call Spread, mirrored.

Example — Nifty at 24,400:
• Buy 24,400 PE @ ₹110
• Sell 24,200 PE @ ₹55
• Net debit = ₹55 × 65 = ₹3,575 max loss
• Max profit = (200 − 55) × 65 = ₹9,425
• Breakeven at 24,345 spot

Use this before RBI meetings, results day, or any expected bearish event — your loss is capped at the net debit, regardless of how volatile the session turns out.
3 PROTECTIVE PUT (PORTFOLIO INSURANCE)
FOR EQUITY INVESTORS
What it is: you already own stocks / an ETF. Buy an OTM Put on Nifty (or the stock itself) as insurance.

When to use: you hold a portfolio and want downside protection through scheduled risk events (elections, Fed, budget, earnings). You keep all the upside; a small Put premium caps your downside.

Example — portfolio worth ₹5L, Nifty at 24,400:
• Buy 1 lot of 24,000 PE monthly @ ₹80 (= ₹5,200 cost)
• That's ~1% of your portfolio for a whole month of insurance.
• If Nifty crashes to 23,500, the Put pays back ₹32,500 → cushions the drawdown dramatically.
• If Nifty stays up or rises, the ₹5,200 is the cost of peace of mind — identical to car insurance.

Think of it as fire insurance on your house — you hope you never need it, but the one time you do, it changes everything.
4 IRON CONDOR (NEUTRAL / RANGE VIEW)
INTERMEDIATE · SELLING PREMIUM
What it is: four-leg strategy. Sell an OTM Call + Buy a further-OTM Call + Sell an OTM Put + Buy a further-OTM Put. All same expiry.

When to use: you think the index will stay range-bound until expiry (low IV, no upcoming events, sideways chart).

Example — Nifty at 24,400, 7-day expiry:
• Sell 24,600 CE @ ₹60 · Buy 24,800 CE @ ₹25
• Sell 24,200 PE @ ₹55 · Buy 24,000 PE @ ₹22
• Net credit = ₹68 × 65 = ₹4,420 max profit
• Max loss = (200 − 68) × 65 = ₹8,580
• Profit zone: Nifty stays between 24,132 and 24,668

Why it's a hedge: the bought Calls/Puts cap your loss. Without them (= "short strangle") your loss is unlimited — the classic account-blower.

Pre-Budget / Pre-RBI / Pre-Fed Bull/Bear Call/Put Spread or Iron Condor IV is inflated. Naked options lose on IV crush even if direction is right. Spreads neutralise most of the crush.
Overnight option position Turn naked CE/PE into a spread by selling further OTM Gaps can halve premium. A spread caps the loss at strike-width minus credit.
Large size (> 2% of capital) Always use a spread, never naked If losing ₹10K+ would hurt emotionally, you MUST hedge. Spreads lock in the max loss.

COST OF HEDGING — IS IT WORTH IT?
RETAIL VIEW
"Hedging cuts my profit. I'd rather risk it — sometimes I'll make 3×."

Reality: 2–3 big losses wipe out months of small wins. The SEBI Sep-2024 study found 93% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY24 — this is how those accounts get there.
PROFESSIONAL VIEW
"Consistency matters more than max profit. I happily cap my upside to cap my downside. My career depends on not blowing up."

This is why pros survive 20+ years.
SIMPLE MATH
Account: ₹2L.
• 10 unhedged wins @ ₹3K = ₹30K
• 1 unhedged loss of ₹50K = wipes 5 winners
• Hedged version: 10 wins @ ₹1.5K = ₹15K, max loss capped at ₹8K.
You survive the bad day.
HOW TO EXECUTE A HEDGED TRADE

EXECUTION — ZERODHA KITE / DHAN / UPSTOX

  • Use BASKET orders (F6 on Kite) — all legs are sent together as a single order. Prevents partial fills and "leg risk".
  • Order sequence matters: on multi-leg spreads, buy the long leg first to reserve margin, then sell the short leg.
  • Check net debit/credit BEFORE placing — the broker shows combined P&L for the whole basket.
  • GTT (Good-Till-Triggered) for exits — set target and SL for the entire basket in advance.
  • Sensibull / Opstra strategy builders construct hedges visually and fire them with one click — beginner-friendly.
  • Never leg out for "just a little more profit" — removing the hedge leg by leg is the classic account-killer.
COMMON HEDGING MISTAKES
HEDGE TOO WIDE
Buying a 24,400 CE and selling 25,000 CE = almost a naked CE. The hedge must be close enough (1–2 strikes wide) to actually cap loss.
HEDGE TOO TIGHT
Selling at 24,450 when you bought 24,400 = max profit is just ₹50 × 65 = tiny. Sweet spot is roughly 1–2% of spot, or 1–2 strikes wide.
REMOVING HEDGE EARLY
"Let me close the short leg — profit is coming." You just unhedged at the worst time. Discipline: the hedge stays until the whole position exits.
DIFFERENT EXPIRIES
Calendar spreads are advanced. Keep all legs in the same expiry unless you explicitly want to trade volatility term structure.
HEDGING AFTER THE EVENT
Hedging after the spike/crash = stable door, bolted horse. Put the hedge on before the event, never after.
HEDGE WIDTH > YOUR SL
If your SL triggers at ₹1,000 loss but the hedge only kicks in at ₹5,000, the hedge is pointless. Hedge width must align with your risk plan.
HEDGING CHECKLIST

BEFORE EVERY F&O TRADE — ASK YOURSELF

  • Is my position > 2% of account capital? (If yes, hedge is mandatory.)
  • Is there a scheduled event in the next 3 days? (If yes, hedge is mandatory.)
  • Am I holding overnight? (If yes, hedge is mandatory.)
  • Is IV elevated (> 90th percentile)? (If yes, hedge or skip the trade.)
  • Do I know the exact max loss of my position to the rupee? (If no, DO NOT trade.)
  • Is my hedge 1–2 strikes wide, same expiry, placed together via basket order?
Rule of thumb: if you don't know your max loss down to the rupee before pressing "Place Order", you're not trading — you're gambling. Hedging is the single discipline that separates professional option traders from retail.
