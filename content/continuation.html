CONTINUATION PATTERNS 12 PATTERNS

Continuation vs Reversal — the critical distinction:
A reversal pattern says "the trend is about to change". A continuation pattern says "the trend is taking a breath, then continuing in the same direction". Mistaking one for the other is the #1 cause of stop-losses hitting.

How to tell them apart:
Continuation patterns appear inside a trending move (higher highs / lower lows intact)
Reversal patterns appear at trend exhaustion (after an extended move, usually with fading volume)
If in doubt, check the higher timeframe — if the larger trend is still intact, treat the current pattern as continuation.

Why they matter for option buyers: Continuation patterns are where you ADD to winning positions or re-enter after a profit-booking leg. They also signal "trend has energy left" — the best R:R trades of the day.

BEGINNER PRIORITY — MASTER THESE FIRST

  • Rising / Falling Three Methods — the textbook continuation pattern. Trend candle → small counter-trend pullback (3 candles) → trend continues. Ideal for options — enter on the breakout candle of the pullback range.
  • Flag / Pennant on candles — mini-consolidation after a sharp move. The tighter the pullback, the stronger the continuation.
  • Separating Lines — gap in the trend direction followed by same-direction candle. Clear, easy to identify.
Rule of thumb: The pullback inside a continuation pattern should NOT exceed 50% of the previous trending move. If it does, the pattern is compromised — the trend is likely ending, not pausing.
