TRAP & FAILURE PATTERNS 6 PATTERNS

What is a "trap"? A trap is when price BREAKS a level (looks like a real breakout) → retail traders rush in → and then price COMES BACK and traps them on the wrong side. The retail traders panic-sell, and that panic-selling fuels a big move in the OPPOSITE direction.

Why traps happen: The big players (operators) need a lot of buyers to enter at a certain price so that they can SELL into them. So they push price ABOVE a key level, lure retail buyers in, then crash the price. The retail traders are now "trapped" with losing positions.

How to confirm a trap: Look for a candle that CLOSES BACK INSIDE the broken range with high volume. That close-back is your signal that the breakout was fake.

Why these are gold-mine trades: When a trap is confirmed, FLIPPING YOUR DIRECTION is one of the highest reward-to-risk trades available to a retail trader — because all the trapped traders are forced to exit, fueling the move.
BULL TRAP (FALSE BREAKOUT)
BEARISH REVERSAL
Price breaks above resistance → Call Option buyers jump in → price falls back below the resistance within 1 to 2 candles. Those Call Option buyers are the "trapped" longs. The stop-loss hunt has been completed — a big move down follows.
To confirm: a candle closes back BELOW resistance with strong volume.
→ Flip to BUY PUT on the close-back
BEAR TRAP (FALSE BREAKDOWN)
BULLISH REVERSAL
Price breaks below support → Put Option buyers jump in → price rallies back above the support within 1 to 2 candles. Those Put Option buyers are the "trapped" shorts. A short squeeze follows — a violent up-move.
To confirm: a candle closes back ABOVE support with strong volume.
→ Flip to BUY CALL on the close-back
LIQUIDITY SWEEP / STOP HUNT
SMART MONEY SIGNAL
Price briefly spikes past a well-known swing high or swing low (where retail traders' stop losses are clustered), then reverses sharply. Big players fill their orders against the trapped retail traders. Look for a single-candle wick through the level, followed by a V-shape recovery.
→ Take a reverse-direction entry
FAILED BREAKOUT (THROWBACK)
REVERSAL SIGNAL
Genuine breakout happens, but price drifts back through the breakout level and closes inside the range. Differs from bull trap only in speed — slower but same bearish outcome.
Action: Stop following the breakout story. Flip bias.
→ FADE the breakout
FAKE BREAKDOWN
REVERSAL SIGNAL
Price breaks support on weak volume → drifts back above → strong up-candle closes above broken level. Bears who shorted the breakdown now cover.
Action: Flip bias after close-back confirms.
→ FADE the breakdown
RANGE EXPANSION TRAP
CHOP WARNING
Price repeatedly pokes just outside a range and then snaps back inside. Both Call Option and Put Option breakout traders get stopped out. Do NOT trade breakouts in this state — trade the range boundaries instead, until you see a full-candle close outside.
→ AVOID — chop mode

HOW TO IDENTIFY A TRAP (CHECKLIST)

  • Volume first: Genuine breakouts have expanding volume. Fake ones have declining or just average volume — suspect a trap.
  • Speed: Trap reversals happen within 1 to 3 candles. If price does not hold the break within 3 candles on your signal timeframe, treat the breakout as failed.
  • Close versus wick: A wick above resistance that closes below it = trap. A body that closes above resistance and holds = a genuine breakout.
  • Context: Breakouts that go into round numbers, into option Open Interest walls, or into prior swing highs / lows are most likely to be liquidity grabs (traps).
  • Confirmation candle: Wait for the close-back candle to complete. Enter on its break to avoid being the next trapped trader.
WHY TRAPS HAPPEN — THE PSYCHOLOGY
The simple truth: Traps exist because large orders need liquidity. Institutions cannot buy or sell huge quantities without someone on the other side. That "someone" is the retail trader chasing the obvious breakout.

The anatomy of a trap — step by step:
STEP 1 · THE OBVIOUS LEVEL
Resistance or support gets tested 2–3 times. Every retail trader, every YouTuber, every WhatsApp group is now watching the same level. Stop-losses pile up just above resistance (for shorts) / just below support (for longs).
STEP 2 · THE BAIT
A small push breaks the level. The breakout is now "live" on every screen. Retail option buyers rush in with Call Options (or Put Options for a breakdown). The trailing stops of existing shorts get triggered, adding fuel to the move.
STEP 3 · THE REVERSAL
With no real buyers left above, price has no fuel. Institutional algos start fading the move. Price closes back inside the range — the "close-back" candle. This is the trap confirmation.
STEP 4 · THE UNWIND
Trapped retail traders see their Call Option / Put Option premium collapse. They exit at a loss. Their exit orders add more selling pressure, accelerating the reversal. The "real" move is now in the opposite direction — often 2 to 3 times bigger than the fake break was.
HOW TO PROFIT FROM TRAPS — FLIP THE BIAS
The trap trade is one of the highest risk-to-reward setups available to retail option buyers. Once you learn to spot the close-back, you can flip from being the trapped victim to being the trader who gets paid when retail panics out.

THE TRAP-FLIP ENTRY CHECKLIST (6 STEPS)

  • 1 · Identify an obvious level — Previous Day High or Low, a round number (24,000 / 24,500), the Volume Weighted Average Price line, the edge of the Central Pivot Range, or an option Open Interest wall.
  • 2 · Watch for the break — price pokes past the level and may even make a new high or new low. Volume is weak or only average.
  • 3 · Wait for the close-back candle — the candle MUST close back inside the range. A wick alone is not enough.
  • 4 · Confirm with a second candle — the next candle should NOT reclaim the broken level. If it does, skip the trade — it might be a pullback rather than a trap.
  • 5 · Enter in the opposite direction — Bull Trap → Buy a Put Option. Bear Trap → Buy a Call Option. Enter on the next candle's open after the close-back candle.
  • 6 · Stop Loss is tight — above the trap high (for a Put Option trade) or below the trap low (for a Call Option trade). If price goes back to test the break, you are wrong — exit immediately. The risk-to-reward is often 1 to 3 or even better.
When to take this trade — The Bull Trap fade at the Previous Day High (highest risk-to-reward in trap trading)
Setup
Nifty has rallied near the Previous Day High in the morning session. Volume has been shrinking on each push higher (a tell-tale sign of weak buying). Price pokes 10 to 30 points above the Previous Day High, but only with a wick.
Entry
Buy an At The Money Put Option ONLY when a 5-minute candle closes BACK BELOW the Previous Day High, AND the next candle does NOT reclaim Previous Day High (this confirms the trap). Volume on the close-back candle should be at least 1.5 times the recent average.
Stop Loss
10 points above the trap's wick high. On the option premium, set a hard stop at 30 percent loss. If price reclaims Previous Day High, you are wrong — exit immediately.
Target
The first target is the Volume Weighted Average Price line (book 50 percent there). The second target is the Previous Day Low or the Day Open. Trail the rest with a 20-point stop on Nifty.
Skip if
The break of Previous Day High happens with strong volume (more than 2 times the average) — that is most likely a real breakout, not a trap, OR it is between 12:00 PM and 1:30 PM (lunch hour fakeouts are mostly noise, not setups), OR it is the day before expiry (Theta makes Put Option buying very risky).
HOW NOT TO BE THE VICTIM — DEFENSE RULES

5 RULES TO AVOID GETTING TRAPPED

  • 1 · Never buy the wick break. If price pokes above resistance but the candle is still open, DO NOT enter. Wait for the candle to close above. 90 percent of retail losses come from buying the spike before the close.
  • 2 · Volume is non-negotiable. A breakout without expanding volume is suspect by default. No volume = no conviction = a likely trap.
  • 3 · Avoid the first 5 minutes. Opening-range fakeouts are the number 1 trap zone. Wait until 9:30 AM or later for the first clear break.
  • 4 · Expiry day traps are brutal. On expiry Tuesday or Thursday, the move near the Max Pain level is often a trap designed to expire option buyers worthless. See the EXPIRY DAY tab for details.
  • 5 · If you are already in a trade — respect the close-back. If your Call Option position is tested by a close-back below support within 1 to 2 candles of your entry, exit immediately. Don't "hope" for a recovery — hope is not a strategy.
Mental model: Think of traps as "the market paying you to not be greedy". The trader who chases the breakout loses. The trader who waits 2–3 candles and enters on confirmation makes money either way — either the breakout is real (and they catch the second leg safely) or it's a trap (and they flip and catch the reversal). Patience is the single highest-paid skill in option trading.
FALSE BREAKOUT vs FAILED BREAKOUT — THE CANONICAL DEFINITION
These two terms are used interchangeably in most Indian blogs — they are NOT the same. Kirkpatrick & Dahlquist (the academic standard) distinguish them precisely, and each carries a different trade plan. Learn the distinction once and you will never misread a breakout again.
FALSE BREAKOUT
QUICK FADE (1–5 BARS)
Definition: Price breaks the level, but returns back through the breakout price within a few bars — the move dies at birth.

Signature:
· Break & close back inside within 1–5 candles
· Volume on the break is weak or average
· Price stays within the prior range afterwards

Outcome: Range-bound — no fresh trend, just trap + fade.
→ FADE the break, target opposite range edge
FAILED BREAKOUT (KIRKPATRICK)
STOP & REVERSE
Definition: Breakout happens, fails, AND then price breaks the opposite side of the pattern. The structure fully inverts.

Signature:
· Break → close back inside → price travels across range → breaks opposite boundary
· Usually 5–20 bars from first break to second
· Volume expands on the opposite break

Outcome: HIGHEST-EV reversal in technical analysis. Kirkpatrick's data: ~68% follow-through, 2× the measured target of normal breakouts.
→ BIG trade: ride the inverted trend
SUCCESSFUL BREAKOUT (FOR COMPARISON)
HEALTHY CONTINUATION
Definition: Break with volume, brief return to test the level from the OTHER side, then trend continues.

Signature:
· Wide-range break candle with volume
· Price holds above/below the broken level
· Throwback (1–8 bars) touches level & bounces
· Breakout target reached within typical time window

Outcome: Textbook follow-through. Second entry on throwback is often safer than chasing the break.
→ Enter on throwback, not the break

DECISION TREE — WHICH OUTCOME IS IT?

  • Break happens — now wait & observe the next 3–5 candles. Do not enter yet.
  • Price closes back inside within 1–5 bars? → FALSE BREAKOUT. Fade it: buy opposite option, target the other range edge. SL beyond the trap high/low.
  • Price closes back inside, then breaks the OPPOSITE boundary within 20 bars? → FAILED BREAKOUT. This is the A+ trade. Enter on the opposite-side break with volume. Target = 1.5–2× the pattern height.
  • Price holds past the breakout level and throws back without closing inside? → SUCCESSFUL BREAKOUT. Enter on the throwback bounce, ride the trend.
  • Practical tip: On Indian index charts, false breakouts cluster at 09:15–09:30 (opening volatility) and 14:30–15:15 (closing hunt). Failed breakouts often crystallize after lunch (13:00–14:00) on low-conviction days. Time-of-day matters.
FAILED BREAKOUT — THE STOP-AND-REVERSE PLAYBOOK
STEP 1 · ENTRY
Enter on the candle that closes beyond the opposite boundary with volume > 1.3× 20-bar average. Do NOT anticipate — wait for the close.
STEP 2 · STOP
SL placed just beyond the failed-break extreme (the original false high/low). If price can reclaim that level, your reversal thesis is wrong — exit.
STEP 3 · TARGET
T1 = pattern height projected from the opposite-side break. T2 = 1.5× height (Kirkpatrick's average follow-through). Book 50% at T1, trail the rest.
Why failed breakouts pay so well: Two groups are caught offside simultaneously — the original breakout buyers AND the traders who faded the false break expecting range continuation. Both groups must exit in the same direction when the opposite boundary breaks. That forced flow is what powers the outsized move.
