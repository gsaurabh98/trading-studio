RISK MANAGEMENT CRITICAL

What is Risk Management? Risk management is just a fancy term for ONE simple idea: decide how much money you can afford to lose BEFORE you place the trade.

It is like wearing a helmet on a bike. You hope you never crash. But the helmet means even if you DO crash, you walk away alive. Without risk management, even ONE bad trade can wipe out months of profits.

Why this page is the most important page on the entire site: 93 out of every 100 retail traders LOSE money. The #1 reason is NOT bad strategy — it is putting too much money on a single trade and getting wiped out. The 4 rules below stop that from happening.
POSITION SIZING — THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE OF TRADING
RULE 1 · THE 1 to 2 PERCENT RULE
1-2%
PER TRADE
Never risk more than 1 to 2 percent of your TOTAL capital on a single trade.

Real example: If your trading capital is ₹1,00,000, the MOST you can afford to lose on a single trade is ₹1,000 to ₹2,000.

Why? So that even after 5-10 losing trades in a row (which WILL happen), your account is still alive and you can keep trading.
RULE 2 · DAILY LOSS LIMIT (THE STOP-FOR-THE-DAY RULE)
3-5%
PER DAY
Decide a STOP-FOR-THE-DAY loss limit of 3 to 5 percent of your capital. If you hit it, you STOP TRADING for that day. No exceptions.

Real example: If your capital is ₹1,00,000, then your daily stop = a loss of ₹3,000 to ₹5,000. Once you hit it, close the laptop. Walk away.

Why? Bad days happen. Forcing more trades on a bad day to "recover" is how accounts die in a single afternoon.
RULE 3 · NEVER "AVERAGE DOWN"
NEVER
DEADLY MISTAKE
What is "averaging down"? Buying MORE of a losing option to lower your average cost.

Why it is deadly with options: Options have time decay. If your trade is wrong, buying more does NOT save you — it just doubles your loss. Stocks can recover over years. Options can become zero in a few hours.

The right move: If a trade is losing, EXIT. Then look at the chart fresh and decide if a new trade makes sense.
RULE 4 · LIMIT YOURSELF TO 2-3 TRADES PER DAY
2-3
QUALITY OVER QUANTITY
Take only 2 to 3 high-quality trades per day. No more.

Why? More trades = more mistakes = more brokerage costs = more losses. Most beginner traders click BUY out of boredom, not because of a good signal.

The mindset shift: A real trader is a SNIPER, not a machine gun. Wait for A-plus setups only. If you do not see one today, do not trade today. The market will be open tomorrow too.
RISK-TO-REWARD RATIO
MINIMUM 1 TO 2 RISK-TO-REWARD

Only take trades where the potential reward is at least 2 times the risk. Even with a 50 percent win rate, a 1 to 2 risk-to-reward ratio keeps you profitable over time.

When to take this trade — Position-sized properly so you survive 7 losses in a row
Setup
You have a clean entry signal (a candle pattern at support / resistance with volume confirmation). You have not yet hit your daily loss limit. You are taking your first or second trade of the day. The risk-to-reward of the trade is at least 1 to 2 (your target is at least twice as far away as your stop loss).
Entry
Calculate your position size like this: maximum risk per trade = 1 percent of your total capital (or 2 percent maximum). For an account of ₹1,00,000, that is ₹1,000 to ₹2,000 of risk. Then size your position so that hitting your stop loss results in exactly that rupee loss — not more.
Stop Loss
Pre-defined BEFORE entry, based on the chart structure (below the swing low for a Call Option trade, above the swing high for a Put Option trade). NEVER move the stop wider after entry — that breaks the entire risk math.
Target
Book 50 percent of the position at the first target (1 to 1 risk-to-reward). Trail the remainder with the Volume Weighted Average Price line, or with a 20-point Nifty stop. Hold for the second target (at 1 to 2 or 1 to 3 risk-to-reward) only if the trend is still intact.
Skip if
You have already taken 3 trades today (over-trading kills edge), OR your daily loss limit (3 to 5 percent of capital) has been hit, OR the risk-to-reward of the setup is below 1 to 2 (the maths does not work over time, even with a high win rate).
DRAWDOWN & RECOVERY MATH — WHY 2% MATTERS
THE RECOVERY TRAP
Lose 10% → need +11.1% to recover
Lose 20% → need +25% to recover
Lose 30% → need +43% to recover
Lose 50% → need +100% to recover
Lose 70% → need +233% to recover
Losses and gains are NOT symmetric. A 50% drawdown requires doubling your account to get back to break-even. Protect downside first.
CONSECUTIVE LOSSES PROBABILITY
At 50% win rate (realistic):
• 5 losses in a row: 3.1% chance
• 7 losses in a row: 0.8% chance
• 10 losses in a row: 0.1% chance
Over 200 trades, you WILL hit:
• A 7-loss streak (expected ~1.5×)
• Possibly a 9-loss streak
Your risk-per-trade MUST survive a 10-loss streak without emotional ruin. At 2% per trade = 18% max drawdown. At 5% = 40% drawdown = career-ending.
RISK OF RUIN — 2 PERCENT VS 5 PERCENT
At a 50 percent win rate and 1 to 2 risk-to-reward ratio:
• Risk 1 percent per trade → about 0 percent risk of ruin
• Risk 2 percent per trade → about 0 percent risk of ruin
• Risk 5 percent per trade → about 4 percent risk of ruin
• Risk 10 percent per trade → about 36 percent risk of ruin
• Risk 20 percent per trade → about 77 percent risk of ruin
"Risk of ruin" means the probability of losing 50 percent of your capital before doubling it. If you risk 20 percent per trade, 3 out of 4 traders blow up regardless of their skill.
COMPOUNDING AT 2%/DAY
₹1,00,000 capital, 2% daily gain:
• 5 days (1 week): ₹1,10,408
• 20 days (1 month): ₹1,48,595
• 250 days (1 year): ₹1,40,91,574
The math on paper — impossible in practice due to drawdowns, taxes, slippage, costs. But even 0.3% compounded daily = 110% in a year. Small, consistent gains win.
