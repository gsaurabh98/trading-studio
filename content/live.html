What do these numbers mean? (read once)
  • OI (Open Interest) — live count of option contracts that haven’t been squared off. The bigger the OI at a strike, the more money is parked there.
  • PCR (Put / Call Ratio) — total Put OI ÷ total Call OI. Above 1.2 = bullish lean (writers are happy to sell puts because they expect support). Below 0.8 = bearish lean.
  • Max Pain — the strike at which option-writers (the big players) lose the least money at expiry. Price tends to gravitate toward this level on expiry day.
  • OI Bias — how the put-vs-call book is tilted. Positive = put-heavy (writers expect a floor → bullish). Negative = call-heavy (writers expect a ceiling → bearish).
  • Wall — the single strike with the largest OI on its side. Acts as a magnet / barrier because writers will defend their position.
  • Growing OI on a wall = new writers piling in (wall getting stronger). Shrinking = writers covering / running (wall is about to break).
Tall green bars = strikes where put-writers are committed. These act as support / floor.
Tall red bars = strikes where call-writers are committed. These act as resistance / ceiling.
Dashed blue line = current spot. Whichever side has the bigger OI stack is where price will struggle to go.
