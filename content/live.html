    PER-TIMEFRAME BREAKDOWN

    Every indicator the intraday recommendation was built from — trend, momentum, volatility, volume, structure and patterns at each timeframe, with that timeframe's signed contribution to the final score.

    How to read this analyzer

    A quick reference for the recommendation, the per-timeframe cards, and the trade plan.

    1. The recommendation in one line

    • BUY CE — bullish confluence across the lower timeframes with no safety veto. Buy a call option.
    • BUY PE — bearish confluence across the lower timeframes with no safety veto. Buy a put option.
    • WAIT — either no clear edge (mixed signals / chop) or a side scored but a safety filter blocked it. The SETUP banner above the plan card always names the specific reason.

    2. What each of the 4 timeframes does

    • 1 HOUR — big-trend filter. Blocks counter-trend trades (e.g. BUY CE when the macro trend is BEAR).
    • 30 MIN — confirm. Has to agree with 1H direction before any setup is allowed; rejects 1H whipsaws.
    • 15 MIN — context. Confirms the intraday direction; carries the heaviest weight.
    • 5 MIN — setup + trigger. The "trade-grade" frame — this is where pattern + S/R + momentum align AND where the bar that fires the trade closes.

    3. The 3 headline numbers per TF card

    • TREND — direction inferred from EMA stack + price location. ▲ BULL good for CE, ▼ BEAR good for PE, NEUTRAL = wait. VWAP n/a on 1H is normal — VWAP is session-anchored.
    • RSI (14) — momentum 0-100. 55-70 bullish · 30-45 bearish · > 75 or < 25 over-extended (reversal risk — wait).
    • ADX (14) — trend strength. > 25 = strong move worth trading · < 20 = chop, premiums will decay against you.

    4. The contribution score (per-card panel)

    Each timeframe casts a signed score from the indicators that fired on its bars. +positive = bullish, −negative = bearish. The engine totals all four TFs and picks the side that clears +4 minimum and meaningfully beats the other side. Mixed totals or both sides low → WAIT.

    The ANALYSIS COVERAGE strip above the plan shows the two totals (Bullish X pts vs Y pts) and the final recommendation in one line.

    5. The 6 plan cells

    • 1. BUY THIS STRIKE — recommended ATM strike (or your override from the strike dropdown). Tightest bid-ask spread, ~0.5 delta.
    • 2. BUY AT THIS PRICE — live premium per share. Click BUY on the trade ticket below to fill at market.
    • 3. STOP-LOSS — sell-stop on the premium, anchored to the nearest support. Broker auto-exits if it fills.
    • 4. TARGET T1 — first realistic exit. Nearest resistance, highest hit-probability.
    • 5. TARGET T2 — main target. Next resistance, where most setups stall out.
    • 6. EMERGENCY EXIT — spot trip-line. If Nifty breaks this level, sell at market regardless of the premium stop.

    6. Why a trade gets BLOCKED

    The engine occasionally scores a clear side but vetoes it for safety. The SETUP banner above the plan card always names the specific reason. The most common ones:

    • 1H macro misalignment — wanted BUY CE but the 1-hour trend is BEAR (or vice versa). Counter-trend intraday fails ~65% of the time.
    • Structure room (R:R) — reward to the nearest major level is < risk. Math says you lose money over time even at a 60% win-rate after brokerage.
    • Market not open — pre-09:15 or post-15:30 IST, or a weekend. No live data to plan against.
    • Session phase — inside the last 5-15 min "NO_NEW" window. Time decay outpaces directional edge.
    • Dead volatility — 5m ATR < ~0.05% of price. Premiums decay faster than direction pays.
    • RSI cascade exhaustion — momentum is already stretched across the lower TFs. Reversal risk too high.

    7. Strike override — ITM vs ATM vs OTM

    The spot levels (Nifty target ₹X) never change regardless of which strike you pick — S/R is structural. What changes is the premium projection for each level, because each strike has a different delta:

    • ITM (delta ~0.7-0.8) — premium moves ~75p per 100p Nifty. Tighter SL/targets in ₹, bigger absolute swing.
    • ATM (delta ~0.5) — balanced. The engine's default recommendation.
    • OTM (delta ~0.2-0.3) — premium moves ~25p per 100p Nifty. Cheaper to lose, less to gain per move.

    R:R stays roughly the same across strikes — setup quality is identical, you're just choosing how much skin to put in per lot.

    Option Chain Snapshot

    What do these numbers mean? (read this once)
    • Open Interest — the live count of option contracts that have NOT yet been squared off. The bigger the Open Interest at a strike, the more money is parked at that strike.
    • Put-Call Ratio — total Put Option Open Interest divided by total Call Option Open Interest. Above 1.2 = a bullish lean (writers are happy to sell Put Options because they expect support to hold). Below 0.8 = a bearish lean.
    • Max Pain — the strike at which option-writers (the big players) lose the least money at expiry. Price tends to gravitate toward this level on expiry day.
    • Open Interest Bias — how the Put-vs-Call book is tilted. Positive = Put-heavy (writers expect a floor → bullish). Negative = Call-heavy (writers expect a ceiling → bearish).
    • Wall — the single strike with the largest Open Interest on its side. It acts like a magnet or a barrier because the writers will defend their position.
    • Growing Open Interest on a wall = new writers piling in (the wall is getting stronger). Shrinking Open Interest = writers covering / running away (the wall is about to break).

    Option Chain

    Tall green bars = strikes where Put Option writers are committed. These act as support / a floor for price.
    Tall red bars = strikes where Call Option writers are committed. These act as resistance / a ceiling for price.
    Dashed blue line = the current spot price. Whichever side has the bigger Open Interest stack is the side where price will struggle to go.

The chart above is showing live Nifty data and you can see a clear setup forming — for example, a hammer candle at the Previous Day Low with volume above average, or an Opening Range Breakout above the 9:30 AM high with a wall just below.
    Entry
    Pick the At The Money strike from the strike picker (the strike closest to the spot price). Click BUY Call Option (if bullish) or BUY Put Option (if bearish), with 1 lot only. Note the entry premium, time, and your reason for the trade. This is a SIMULATED trade — no real money is involved.
    Stop Loss
    Mentally set a stop loss at 30 to 35 percent below the entry premium. The simulator does NOT auto-stop; you must manually click EXIT when the spot price breaks the opposite side of the structural level.
    Target
    Aim for at least a 1 to 2 risk-to-reward (so if your stop loss is 35 percent, the target is at least 70 percent gain on premium). Click EXIT at the target. Open the Trade History to review the win rate, the average profit and loss, and the best and worst trades. Continue paper trading until you have at least 30 closed trades AND a win rate above 50 percent before moving to live trading.
