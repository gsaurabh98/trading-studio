OPTION GREEKS THE FORCES THAT MOVE PREMIUM

What are "the Greeks"? The Greeks are 4 simple numbers that explain how the option premium moves. They are called "Greeks" because they are named after Greek letters (Delta, Theta, Vega, Gamma).

Why beginners lose money: Most retail traders look at only ONE Greek — Delta (the direction). They forget that THREE other forces are working AGAINST them:
· Theta — the option loses value every day, like an ice cube melting.
· Vega — the option loses value when fear in the market drops.
· Gamma — the option becomes more sensitive close to expiry day (small moves cause big premium swings).

Understand these 4 numbers, and you stop losing money to forces you cannot even see.
THINK OF THE 4 GREEKS LIKE THIS — A SIMPLE CAR ANALOGY
Imagine your option premium is a CAR moving on a road. The 4 Greeks are different forces affecting that car:
· Delta = the SPEED of the car (how fast premium moves with the index).
· Theta = a slow PUNCTURE in the tyre (the car loses speed every day, even when standing still).
· Vega = the WEATHER (when fear / volatility changes, the road conditions change).
· Gamma = the ACCELERATION (how fast the speed itself can change — sudden braking or sudden burst).
THE 4 GREEKS YOU MUST KNOW — ONE BY ONE
Δ DELTA — THE SPEED
0.5
AT THE MONEY OPTION
Plain English: If Nifty moves ₹1, how much will the option premium move?

· At The Money option: Delta is around 0.5 → if Nifty moves ₹1, the premium moves ₹0.50.
· Deep In The Money option: Delta is around 1 → premium moves rupee-for-rupee with Nifty.
· Deep Out Of The Money option: Delta is only 0.1 → premium barely moves.

Beginner rule: Always buy options with Delta above 0.4. Anything below moves too slowly to be worth your time.
Θ THETA — THE TIME LEAK
-ve
YOUR DAILY ENEMY
Plain English: How much premium does your option LOSE every day, EVEN if Nifty does not move?

Theta is always NEGATIVE for buyers. It is like a slow leak in your tyre — you are losing money every day you hold.

· Day 1 of the week: Theta is small.
· Day before expiry: Theta is HUGE — the premium can drop 30-40 percent in a single afternoon.

Beginner rule: Never hold an At The Money weekly option overnight on the day before expiry. Theta will destroy it.
ν VEGA — THE FEAR FACTOR
IV
WATCH BEFORE EVENTS
Plain English: How much premium does your option lose or gain when fear (volatility) in the market changes?

Think of Vega as the "fear premium". When traders are scared, they pay MORE for options. When fear drops, the premium drops too — even if Nifty moves in your favour.

Real example: Before the Union Budget, the India Volatility Index might be 25 (high fear). After the Budget speech is done, fear drops to 15. Your option premium also drops sharply — this is called "Implied Volatility crush" (or IV crush).

Beginner rule: Never buy options the day BEFORE a big event. The IV crush after the event will hurt you.
Γ GAMMA — THE ACCELERATION
Δ rate
EXPIRY DAY DRAMA
Plain English: How fast does Delta itself change?

If Delta is the speed of the car, Gamma is the acceleration. High Gamma = the speed can change very fast.

· Gamma is HIGHEST when the strike is At The Money AND expiry is close.
· This is why options on expiry day move WILDLY — one moment you are up 100 percent, the next you are down 50 percent.

Beginner rule: Avoid weekly expiry-day options unless you are very experienced. High Gamma cuts both ways — quick wins, but quicker losses.
DELTA: CALL OPTION IS POSITIVE, PUT OPTION IS NEGATIVE
Confused by the minus sign? When you open the option chain on Zerodha, Upstox, or Sensibull, you see Delta as a positive number for Call Options and a NEGATIVE number for Put Options.

The minus sign is NOT a loss indicator. It just means "this option moves in the OPPOSITE direction to Nifty". A Put Option goes UP when Nifty goes DOWN — that is what the minus sign is showing you.

Read the two cards below once and you will never confuse it again.
Δ CALL OPTION DELTA
0 to +1
POSITIVE — MOVES WITH NIFTY
A Call Option goes UP when Nifty goes UP. So its Delta is POSITIVE.

Typical Delta values:
· At The Money Call Option: +0.5 (premium moves ₹0.5 for every ₹1 move in Nifty)
· Deep In The Money Call Option: +0.9 (almost rupee-for-rupee)
· Deep Out Of The Money Call Option: +0.1 (barely moves)

Bonus tip: The Delta number also tells you the rough chance that the option will be profitable at expiry. So an At The Money option (Delta 0.5) has roughly a 50 percent chance, and a deep Out Of The Money option (Delta 0.1) has only a 10 percent chance.
Δ PUT OPTION DELTA
−1 to 0
NEGATIVE — MOVES OPPOSITE TO NIFTY
A Put Option goes UP when Nifty goes DOWN. So its Delta is NEGATIVE (the minus sign just shows the opposite direction).

Typical Delta values:
· At The Money Put Option: -0.5
· Deep In The Money Put Option: -0.9
· Deep Out Of The Money Put Option: -0.1

Ignore the minus sign for the chance calculation. A Put Option with Delta -0.5 also has roughly a 50 percent chance of being profitable at expiry. The size of the number is what matters — the sign just tells you direction.

TAKEAWAYS

  • Same Nifty move, opposite P&L — that's the whole point of the sign. CE row and PE row are mirror images.
  • Magnitude is identical at ATM (0.50 vs −0.50) — both options earn or lose ₹325 per 10 pts. The CE doesn't have more "power" than the PE.
  • Quick rupee shortcut: P&L per lot ≈ Nifty pts × |Delta| × lot size — sign comes from option type × direction of move.
  • Why option-chain screens show the minus: if you sum the deltas of a strangle (long CE + long PE), the deltas partially cancel and you immediately see your net directional exposure. The sign convention is what makes that arithmetic work.
  • Real life: Theta and Vega will eat into both sides daily — see the "Why did my CE lose money even though Nifty went up?" worked example below.
IMPLIED VOLATILITY (VEGA IN PRACTICE)
WHEN IV IS LOW → BUY

When IV is below average, options are cheap. Best time to buy. IV will likely expand → premium increases even before price moves.

IV CRUSH AFTER EVENTS

Before results/budget, IV is high. After event, IV crashes even if price moves. AVOID buying options just before major events.

THETA DECAY BY TIME OF DAY
PREMIUM DECAY CURVE (INTRADAY)

Buy options between 9:15–11:30 AM. Theta decay accelerates after 2:00 PM. After 2:30 PM, option buyers are at severe disadvantage.

PLAIN-ENGLISH ANALOGIES (REMEMBER THESE)
The 4 Greeks in one sentence each — use these mental models every time you trade options. Greek formulas don't matter; the intuition does.
Δ DELTA = THE THERMOMETER
Tells you how reactive the option is to index moves. Delta 0.5 means the option moves 50 paise for every ₹1 the index moves. Also roughly equals the probability the option finishes ITM. Beginners: always buy Delta ≥ 0.4 — anything lower is a lottery ticket.
Θ THETA = THE RENT
You pay rent every day to hold an option. Theta is that rent, in ₹. A -₹8 theta means the option loses ₹8 overnight even if the index doesn't move. Rent compounds — theta accelerates as expiry approaches. The option buyer's permanent enemy.
ν VEGA = THE FUEL PRICE
Think of IV as the fuel price of options. When fuel is expensive (high IV), every option costs more. When fuel crashes (after an event), every option loses value instantly — even if the index moves in your favour. Rule: buy options when fuel is cheap, not expensive.
Γ GAMMA = THE ACCELERATOR
Delta isn't fixed — it changes as the index moves. Gamma is how fast Delta speeds up. Highest on expiry day, for ATM strikes. High Gamma = massive profits when right, massive losses when wrong. Double-edged sword.
"WHY DID MY CE LOSE MONEY EVEN THOUGH NIFTY WENT UP?" — WORKED EXAMPLE
This is the #1 question every new option buyer asks. You were right on direction — the index went UP — yet your CE shows a LOSS. The answer is always in the Greeks. Here's the math broken down day-by-day so you can finally see it with your own eyes.
SETUP
Friday 09:30 AM: Nifty is at 24,000. You buy 24,100 CE (slightly OTM) expiring next Tuesday (Nifty weekly). Premium = ₹80 per unit. Lot size 65 → you pay ₹5,200.
Greeks at entry: Delta 0.35 · Theta -₹12/day · Vega ₹5 per 1% IV change · IV = 14%
You believe Nifty will rally to 24,200 by Tuesday. Your thesis is correct. Let's see what actually happens.
Day Nifty spot What happens Premium P&L per unit
Fri 09:30 24,000 Entry. Paid ₹80. Thesis: Nifty hits 24,200 by Tue. ₹80 —
Fri 15:30 24,010 (+10) Nifty drifts up ₹10. Delta adds (+₹3.5). Theta eats (-₹6 for half day). ₹77 -₹3
Mon 15:30 24,030 (+30) Weekend & Mon decay. Delta adds (+₹7). BUT 3 days of theta come out (-₹30) and IV drops 1% (-₹5). ₹58 -₹22
Tue 11:00 24,100 (+100) Nifty at your target strike! Delta adds (+₹18). BUT expiry theta is brutal (-₹25) and IV crashes (-₹10). ₹41 -₹39
Tue 14:30 24,130 (+130) Nifty up 130 points — your thesis was RIGHT. But expiry-day theta is crushing. 0 DTE IV has crashed. ₹32 -₹48
Tue 15:30 24,130 (expiry) Expires with intrinsic value = (24,130-24,100) = ₹30. ₹30 -₹50

WHAT HAPPENED — THE BRUTAL LESSON

  • Direction was right: Nifty moved +130 points in 4 days. Most retail traders would think "my CE must be profitable".
  • Delta earned you ~₹35 total over the 4 days as the index climbed.
  • Theta cost you ~₹58 over 4 days (accelerating near expiry from -₹12 to -₹25/day).
  • Vega (IV crush) cost you ~₹15 as IV fell from 14% to 11% through the week.
  • Net result: ₹35 - ₹58 - ₹15 = ₹38 LOSS per unit (close to the ₹50 shown above). You were right on direction but Theta + Vega devoured your profit.
The fix: Had you bought 24,000 CE (ATM, Delta 0.52) instead of 24,100 CE (OTM, Delta 0.35) — you would have earned more Delta per point of Nifty move, enough to offset Theta. Or had you traded 2-hour intraday (no overnight theta) instead of holding 4 days, you'd have captured the Delta without the theta bleed. Strike selection and holding period are NOT cosmetic — they are the trade.

BEGINNER RULE OF THUMB — PICK YOUR STRIKE

  • Expiry day (0 Days To Expiry): ONLY At The Money or 1-strike In The Money. Out Of The Money is a trap — Theta kills you in minutes.
  • 1 to 3 days to expiry: At The Money or slightly In The Money. Delta should be 0.45 or more.
  • 4 or more days to expiry: You can use slightly Out Of The Money if you expect a big move, but At The Money stays the safest choice.
  • Never: Buy far Out Of The Money "cheap" options. The maths is stacked against you — the entire reason they are cheap is that they have less than a 20 percent chance of paying out.
When to take this trade — The Greeks-aware option buy (avoid the "right direction, still lost" trap)
Setup
You have a clean directional signal on Nifty (a candle pattern at support / resistance with volume confirmation). India Volatility Index is between 12 and 16 (so Implied Volatility is on the cheaper side, NOT inflated). It is between 9:30 AM and 12:00 PM IST. There are at least 3 days left until expiry.
Entry
Buy the At The Money strike of the current-week expiry. Confirm Delta is at least 0.45 on your broker's option chain. Use a limit order at the mid-price.
Stop Loss
30 percent loss on the option premium — this is a hard stop, no exceptions. So if you bought at ₹100, exit at ₹70.
Target
Book 50 percent of the position when the premium gains 50 percent. Trail the rest with the Volume Weighted Average Price line on Nifty's 5-minute chart. Aim to close the entire position before 2:30 PM IST — do NOT let Theta decay accelerate against you in the final hour.
Skip if
India Volatility Index is above 22 (premiums are too inflated, so an Implied Volatility crush will hurt you), OR there is a major event (Reserve Bank of India meeting, Federal Reserve announcement, quarterly results) within the next 24 hours, OR the option expires tomorrow (Theta will eat you alive overnight).
DTE (DAYS TO EXPIRY) — HOW THETA ACCELERATES
Theta is not linear — it is exponential near expiry. The same ATM option loses ₹5/day at 30 DTE, ₹15/day at 7 DTE, and ₹40/day at 1 DTE. Understanding this curve prevents the classic "I held too long" mistake.

14 DTE Moderate (2–3% premium/day) Medium High Ideal for 2–5 day swing trades. Good balance of Delta response and bleed.
7 DTE (weekly) Fast (4–8% premium/day) High Moderate Intraday buyers' favourite. High Gamma = explosive moves. Close same day.

GREEKS CHEAT-SHEET FOR OPTION BUYERS

  • DELTA: Pick options with Delta greater than 0.4 (At The Money or slightly In The Money) for responsive moves. Deep Out Of The Money has very low Delta and near-zero hope.
  • THETA: Your daily rent. Pay attention after 2 PM on expiry day — the premium can be cut in half in 30 minutes on a stagnant index.
  • VEGA: Check the India Volatility Index. Below 13 means an Implied Volatility expansion tailwind. Above 20 means an Implied Volatility contraction risk. Never buy options just before scheduled events.
  • GAMMA: Weekly expiries have the highest Gamma. Small index moves equal outsized profits or losses. Great when you are right, devastating when you are wrong.
