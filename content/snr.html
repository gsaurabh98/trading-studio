SUPPORT & RESISTANCE FOUNDATION

What is Support and Resistance?

Think of it like a ball bouncing inside a room:
· The FLOOR is the SUPPORT — the price level where the ball stops falling and bounces UP. Buyers are waiting at this level.
· The CEILING is the RESISTANCE — the price level where the ball stops rising and falls DOWN. Sellers are waiting at this level.

Why does this happen? Because traders REMEMBER price levels. If Nifty bounced from 24,000 last week, the moment Nifty comes back to 24,000 again, those traders rush to BUY again — thinking "it bounced last time, it will bounce again". Their buying actually CAUSES the bounce. This is called a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Why this is the FOUNDATION of all trading: Every other technical analysis tool — candlestick patterns, chart patterns, indicators — works MUCH better when used at a Support / Resistance level. A bullish candle in the middle of nowhere is just noise. A bullish candle AT a support level is a high-quality trade.
WHAT IS SUPPORT & RESISTANCE (in pictures)
SUPPORT LEVEL

A price level where buyers keep stepping in and pushing the price back UP. The more times the price bounces from this level, the stronger the support becomes.

RESISTANCE LEVEL

A price level where sellers keep stepping in and pushing the price back DOWN. The more times the price gets rejected at this level, the stronger the resistance becomes.

When to take this trade — Bounce from Support
Setup
Price has bounced from the same level at least 2 times before. The level shows up on a higher timeframe (daily or weekly chart). Today's price is approaching that level from above.
Entry
Wait for a green reversal candle (such as a Hammer, Bullish Engulfing, or Morning Star) to form right at the support level. Buy a Call Option at or near the spot price (At the Money or one strike Out of the Money) the moment that reversal candle closes.
Stop Loss
Place the stop loss 10 to 15 points below the support level on Nifty. For the option premium itself, set a hard stop at 30 to 40 percent loss on the option price.
Target
Aim for the next resistance level above. The minimum target should be at least 2 times your stop loss distance (this is called a 1 to 2 risk-to-reward ratio).
Skip if
The broader trend is strongly downward (do not catch a falling knife), volume is dead at the support level, or the bounce candle has a long upper wick (showing sellers are still active).
HOW TO IDENTIFY KEY LEVELS
SWING HIGHS & LOWS
Swing Highs (peaks on the chart) become Resistance levels. Swing Lows (valleys on the chart) become Support levels. Connect the peaks and troughs on your chart to find them.
ROUND NUMBERS
Numbers like Nifty 26,000 or 26,500, BankNifty 57,000 or 58,000, and Sensex 85,000 act as natural support and resistance because traders mentally focus on these levels. Always mark them on your chart.
PREVIOUS DAY HIGH AND LOW
The Previous Day High (PDH) and Previous Day Low (PDL) are the most important intraday levels. Mark these on your chart before the market opens every single day.
OPENING RANGE
The Opening Range is the high and low of the first 15-minute candle of the day. If price breaks above this range, buy a Call Option. If price breaks below, buy a Put Option. This is a high-probability intraday setup.
MOVING AVERAGES AS SUPPORT & RESISTANCE
The 20 period Exponential Moving Average acts as moving support during an uptrend. The 50 period Exponential Moving Average is even stronger support. When a candle reversal pattern forms right at one of these moving averages, that is a high-probability trade.
VOLUME PROFILE / POINT OF CONTROL
The Point of Control is the price level where the most volume traded during a session. It acts as strong support and resistance. A candle reversal pattern at the Point of Control is one of the highest-probability trade setups available.
When to take this trade — Previous Day High Breakout (intraday)
Setup
Yesterday's high is clearly marked on your chart. Today's market opened below yesterday's high. Price is now approaching yesterday's high from below with rising volume. Nifty is in an uptrend on the daily chart.
Entry
Buy a Call Option (At the Money or one strike Out of the Money) when a 15-minute candle closes above yesterday's high with volume at least 1.5 times the average of the last 20 candles.
Stop Loss
Place the stop loss just below yesterday's high (10 to 15 points back inside on Nifty). For the option premium, exit if you lose 30 to 35 percent of the option price.
Target
Aim for the next resistance above (often a round number like 23,500 or 24,000) or 1.5 times yesterday's range. Book half at the first target and trail the stop on the rest.
Skip if
The breakout happens during the lunch hour (12:30 PM to 1:30 PM IST), volume is below average, or the broader market index is moving in the opposite direction.
SUPPORT BECOMES RESISTANCE (ROLE REVERSAL)

When price breaks below a support level, that level flips its role and becomes resistance instead. The price often comes back up to test the broken level from below before falling again. This retest is a high-probability Put Option entry point. The same principle works in reverse: when price breaks above a resistance level, that level flips to become support, and a pullback to retest it is a high-probability Call Option entry.

When to take this trade — Role-Reversal Retest (very high success rate)
Setup
Price has clearly broken through a major support or resistance level on strong volume in the last few candles. The break candle had a body close past the level, not just a wick poke. Now price is slowly coming back to test the broken level from the new side.
Entry
For a broken support (now resistance), buy a Put Option when price touches the level and forms a red candle. For a broken resistance (now support), buy a Call Option when price touches the level and forms a green candle.
Stop Loss
Place the stop loss just on the wrong side of the level. For example, if support flipped to resistance at 23,500, your stop loss for the Put Option goes at 23,520 (above the level by 20 points).
Target
Aim for the next major support or resistance level. The retest entry usually gives you the best risk-to-reward ratio of any trade setup, often 1 to 3 or better.
Skip if
The retest takes more than 5 to 10 candles to happen (momentum is gone), the retest candle has a long wick on the wrong side, or volume is dead during the pullback.
FINDING THE BEST TIMEFRAME FOR SUPPORT & RESISTANCE
A key rule: Higher timeframe support and resistance is stronger than lower timeframe support and resistance. A level visible on the daily chart will hold better than a level visible only on the 5-minute chart. Always mark your levels top-down — start from the highest timeframe (weekly) and work your way down to the lowest (15-minute).
WEEKLY / MONTHLY CHART
LONGEST-TERM LEVELS
What to mark: All-time highs, 52-week highs and lows, major quarterly swing points, and psychological round numbers (26,000, 27,000, 50,000 and so on).

Why it matters: These levels are watched by big institutions and Foreign Institutional Investors. They rarely fail on the first test (more than 80 percent reaction rate). You only need to mark these once a month.
DAILY CHART
MASTER TIMEFRAME
What to mark: The last 10 to 15 swing highs and lows, the last month's high and low, the open price of the current month, and the most obvious horizontal levels where price has bounced or reversed at least 2 times.

Why it matters: This is the most important timeframe for intraday option buyers. Daily levels act like magnets for intraday price action. Mark these every night before the next trading day.
60-MINUTE (HOURLY) CHART
INTRADAY TREND
What to mark: The last 5 to 7 swing highs and lows of the past 5 trading days. Also mark Previous Day High, Previous Day Low, and Previous Day Close.

Why it matters: Connects daily levels to intraday action. Great for spotting the trend direction and where the day's range is likely to end.
15-MINUTE CHART
SIGNAL TIMEFRAME
What to mark: Today's Opening Range (the high and low of the first 15-minute candle), the Volume Weighted Average Price line, the 20 period Exponential Moving Average, and the day's developing high and low.

Why it matters: This is where candle reversal patterns form for your actual trade signal. A level on the 15-minute chart must line up with a level on a higher timeframe to be tradeable.
5-MINUTE AND BELOW
AVOID FOR DRAWING LEVELS
Do NOT draw support and resistance levels from these timeframes. 5-minute swings are mostly noise, with too many fake levels. They will confuse you and make every single candle look like a "reversal at support".

Correct use: Only use these timeframes to fine-tune your entry AFTER the 15-minute, 60-minute, and daily levels have already given you a signal.
CONFLUENCE = TOP-QUALITY SETUPS
GOAL
The magic number is 3: A price level that is a key support or resistance on 3 different timeframes (for example, weekly support and daily support and 60-minute swing low all at the same price) is almost unbreakable on the first test.

This stacking of levels is called confluence. Only trade options at confluence levels. The rest are noise.

THE TOP-DOWN WORKFLOW (DO THIS EVERY NIGHT — 10 MINUTES)

  • Step 1 — Weekly chart (1 minute): Zoom out. Mark 2 to 3 horizontal lines at major multi-month highs and lows. Use one colour (such as gold) for these.
  • Step 2 — Daily chart (4 minutes): Mark the last 10 swing highs and lows. Mark Previous Day High, Previous Day Low, and Previous Day Close. Mark the monthly opening price. Use a different colour (such as orange) for these.
  • Step 3 — 60-minute chart (3 minutes): Mark the last 5 swing highs and lows of the past week. If any of these match your daily marks, make the line thicker (this is confluence).
  • Step 4 — 15-minute chart (2 minutes): Tomorrow morning, wait for the 9:15 AM to 9:30 AM Opening Range candle to close. Add its high and low as today-only intraday levels.
  • Only take a trade when: Price approaches a level that shows up on 2 or more timeframes, you see a clear candle reversal pattern, and volume confirms the move. One timeframe alone is not enough — skip those setups.
COMMON MISTAKES WHEN DRAWING LEVELS
TOO MANY LINES ON THE CHART
More lines does NOT mean more accuracy. If every single candle is touching a "support level", you have drawn too many lines. The rule: If you cannot explain a line in one sentence, delete it. Target 4 to 6 lines per timeframe at the most.
OBSESSING OVER THE EXACT PRICE
Support and resistance is a zone, not a single pixel-perfect line. Draw a faint band of 10 to 20 points around the level for Nifty. The price can overshoot by 0.2 to 0.3 percent and the zone can still be respected.
USING ONLY ONE TIMEFRAME
Drawing levels on 5-minute chart only means trading noise. Drawing only on the daily chart means missing intraday precision. You need at least 2 timeframes marked at the same time.
IGNORING OLD LEVELS
A level from 3 months ago is STILL relevant. The market has memory. Do not erase a level just because price went past it. Instead, flip the level to the opposite role (broken support becomes resistance, and vice versa).
