MORE CANDLESTICK PATTERNS 16 RARE PATTERNS

These patterns appear less often but are statistically very reliable (according to Thomas Bulkowski and Steve Nison’s research). When you spot them at a key support or resistance level with high volume, treat them as a high-conviction signal.
HIGH-RELIABILITY RARE PATTERNS
BULLISH THREE LINE STRIKE
VERY HIGH RELIABILITY
Three red candles in a row, followed by ONE massive green candle that covers (engulfs) all three. Bulkowski classifies this as one of the most reliable bullish reversals, with around an 84 percent success rate.
→ STRONG BUY Call Option
BEARISH THREE LINE STRIKE
VERY HIGH RELIABILITY
Three green candles in a row, followed by ONE massive red candle that covers all three. An extreme bearish reversal — a major top signal.
→ STRONG BUY Put Option
ISLAND REVERSAL (BOTTOM)
MAJOR REVERSAL
A gap down into a cluster of candles, then a gap up out of it. The "island" is isolated by two gaps moving in opposite directions. Rare but decisive bottom.
→ STRONG BUY Call Option
ISLAND REVERSAL (TOP)
MAJOR REVERSAL
A gap up into a cluster, then a gap down out of it. The island at the top = distribution → reversal. A violent move down follows.
→ STRONG BUY Put Option
TOWER BOTTOM
BOTTOM REVERSAL
A tall red candle → a cluster of small indecision candles → a tall green candle. The two "towers" flank a basing phase. A clear capitulation → accumulation → breakout sequence.
→ BUY Call Option
TOWER TOP
TOP REVERSAL
A tall green candle → a cluster of indecision candles → a tall red candle. Distribution is happening between the towers — smart money is selling to retail traders who are chasing the highs.
→ BUY Put Option
FRY-PAN BOTTOM
GRADUAL REVERSAL
A slow, rounded decline of small candles that gradually shift from red → doji → green. Unlike a sharp V-bottom, this is patient accumulation.
→ BUY Call Option on the breakout
DUMPLING TOP
GRADUAL REVERSAL
An inverted fry-pan. A slow rounded top with shrinking green candles → dojis → growing reds. A distribution pattern — slow but reliable top.
→ BUY Put Option on the breakdown
BULLISH MEETING LINES
REVERSAL (WEAK)
A big red, then a green that opens lower but closes at exactly the same level as the red’s close. Similar to a piercing pattern, but it only "meets", it does not pierce through.
→ Call Option with confirmation
BEARISH MEETING LINES
REVERSAL (WEAK)
A big green, then a red that opens higher but closes at exactly the same level as the green’s close. Weaker than a dark cloud cover.
→ Put Option with confirmation
IDENTICAL THREE CROWS
STRONG DOWNTREND
A stricter version of Three Black Crows — each red candle opens at exactly the prior candle’s close (no gap, no upper wick). Ruthless, mechanical selling.
→ BUY Put Option
Trap-variant patterns (Last Engulfing Top / Bottom, Nison’s traps, fake-outs) are taught in Patterns → Traps and Failures with full setups and stop-loss rules.
TWO-BAR REVERSALS — PIPE PATTERNS
Named by Thomas Bulkowski. Two nearly identical tall bars that stand out on the chart like a "pipe" sticking out of the surrounding price action. The highest historical reliability of any two-bar pattern: about a 77 percent reversal rate on weekly charts. Strongest on the weekly and daily timeframes, usable on the 60-minute timeframe.
PIPE BOTTOM
HIGH-RELIABILITY REVERSAL
Two adjacent tall candles (one red, one green) making nearly identical lows. The lows must stick out below the surrounding price — they look like the legs of a pipe.

Confirmation: The third candle closes above the higher of the two pipe highs. Ideally, the volume on the second bar is greater than the volume on the first bar.

Statistics (Bulkowski): 77 percent reversal follow-through on the weekly chart, about 65 percent on the daily. The average rally is 46 percent in bull markets.

Target: Project the pipe-height (the distance from the low to the highest pipe high) upwards from the breakout point.
→ BUY Call Option on the 3rd-candle close above the pipe high
PIPE TOP
HIGH-RELIABILITY REVERSAL
A mirror image — two adjacent tall candles with nearly identical highs that stick out above the surrounding candles. The twin spikes act as exhaustion markers.

Confirmation: The third candle closes below the lower of the two pipe lows.

Statistics: About a 75 percent reversal follow-through on the weekly chart. Used by swing traders as a major top-marker.

Target: The pipe-height projected downwards from the breakdown point.
→ BUY Put Option on the 3rd-candle close below the pipe low
VOLATILITY-CONTRACTION PATTERNS — SETUP FOR BREAKOUTS
These are not directional signals — they are compression markers. They tell you "a big move is coming", but not in which direction. Pair them with a directional trigger (a support / resistance break, or a trend) for a high-probability entry.
INSIDE BAR
CONSOLIDATION
What it is: A candle whose entire range (the high AND the low) sits inside the previous candle’s range. It signals a temporary pause — the market is digesting the previous move.

How to trade: Buy a break of the mother-bar high (Call Option) or buy a break of the mother-bar low (Put Option). The stop loss is the opposite side of the mother bar.

Best when: It appears at a support / resistance level, at a trendline, or after a strong impulse move. Multiple inside bars in a row (Inside Bar 2, Inside Bar 3) = extreme compression, with an explosive break ahead.
→ Trade the mother-bar break
NR4 (NARROW RANGE 4)
VOLATILITY CONTRACTION
What it is: A candle whose range (the high minus the low) is the narrowest of the last 4 bars. The term was coined by Toby Crabel and extensively studied by Linda Raschke.

Why it matters: Volatility is mean-reverting — periods of contraction are nearly always followed by periods of expansion. Narrow Range 4 = a "coiled spring" signal.

How to trade: Place a BUY-stop just above the Narrow Range 4 high, and a SELL-stop just below the Narrow Range 4 low (a one-cancels-the-other order pair). Whichever side breaks first decides the direction. Works on daily and 60-minute timeframes.
→ One-Cancels-the-Other stops on both sides of the Narrow Range 4 bar
NR7 (NARROW RANGE 7)
EXTREME CONTRACTION
What it is: Narrow Range 7 = the narrowest range of the LAST 7 bars. A stricter cousin of Narrow Range 4 — it is rarer, but when it triggers, the expansion is usually larger.

Statistics (Raschke): After a Narrow Range 7 bar, the next-day range expands beyond the prior average in about 70 percent of cases. The follow-through in the break direction is about 60 percent.

Inside Bar combined with Narrow Range 4 / Narrow Range 7 (Inside Day / Narrow Range 4, Inside Day / Narrow Range 7): A single candle that is BOTH an inside bar AND a Narrow Range 4 or 7 bar. The most powerful compression setup — trade the mother-bar break.
→ Wait and trade the break

USING COMPRESSION PATTERNS (INSIDE BAR / NARROW RANGE 4 / NARROW RANGE 7) IN INDIAN OPTIONS

  • Best time of day: 11:00 AM to 1:30 PM IST (the "dead zone") is where compression patterns form most often. Mark them during the lunch break, then trade the 2:00 to 3:00 PM expansion.
  • Best contracts: Nifty and Bank Nifty on 15-minute and 60-minute charts. Compression on the 5-minute chart is too noisy. Daily compression on Futures and Options stocks often precedes multi-day moves.
  • Risk management: Because the stop loss is well defined (the mother-bar low or high), you can size precisely using Calculator 1. Compression trades typically have a TIGHT stop loss — so you can afford more lots than usual within your risk percentage.
  • Avoid: A compression pattern that runs into a MAJOR event (the Union Budget, the Reserve Bank of India policy, or the US Federal Reserve). The break direction is then dictated by news, not by price structure — effectively a 50/50 coin flip. Stay flat.
When to take this trade — The Pipe Bottom Reversal at a major support level
Setup
Nifty has fallen 3 to 5 percent over the last 1 to 2 weeks. On the daily chart, the current candle and the previous candle have nearly identical lows (within 0.2 percent of each other), and these lows touch a major support zone (the previous swing low, or the 200-day Exponential Moving Average, or the Volume Profile Point of Control). The volume on the second candle is higher than on the first candle (a sign of buying absorption). The Relative Strength Index is below 35 (oversold).
Entry
Wait for the THIRD daily candle to close above the higher of the two pipe highs (the confirmation candle). Buy a slightly In The Money or At The Money Call Option of the current-month expiry on the next-day open. Use the spot price level as your reference (not the option premium).
Stop Loss
10 to 15 points below the lower of the two pipe lows on the spot index. On the option premium itself, set a hard stop at 35 percent loss. Use a Stop-Loss-Market order on the broker.
Target
Project the pipe-height (the distance from the pipe low to the pipe high) UPWARDS from the breakout point. Book 50 percent at the first target, then trail the rest with a stop at the previous swing low on the daily chart. Hold for 5 to 15 trading days.
Skip if
The two lows are NOT at a major support zone (then the pipe is just random consolidation), OR the volume on the second bar is lower than on the first (no absorption is happening), OR the India Volatility Index is above 25 (the Implied Volatility crush will hurt your option premium even if you are right on direction), OR a major event (the Reserve Bank of India policy, Union Budget) is scheduled within the next 5 trading days.
