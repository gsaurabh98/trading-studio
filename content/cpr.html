CPR & PIVOT POINTS INDIA'S FAVOURITE INTRADAY TOOL

CPR (Central Pivot Range) is the single most popular intraday tool among Indian option buyers. Used by 60%+ of active intraday traders, plotted automatically by every major broker (Zerodha, Upstox, Dhan, Angel). Set up once, never draw a line manually again.
WHAT IS CPR?
WHAT IS CPR?
CPR is a three-line price zone derived purely from yesterday's High, Low and Close. It projects tomorrow's most probable area of balance — the price band where buyers and sellers are likely to contest — before the market even opens.

The three lines:
• TC — Top Central (upper edge)
• PP — Pivot Point (centre of gravity)
• BC — Bottom Central (lower edge)

Together, TC and BC form a shaded band, and PP runs through the middle. That band is the CPR.
WHY IT EXISTS — THE CORE IDEA
Every trading session has a fair-value zone where most volume traded. Yesterday's H/L/C is the market's best summary of that zone. CPR projects this zone forward to today — giving you a reference grid before the noise of the opening bell starts.

Think of it as: a price gravity map for today — with magnet zones (the band), rejection zones (R1/S1), and extremes (R2/S2/R3/S3). Price doesn't have to follow it, but it tends to. And when it doesn't, that itself is a signal.
HOW CPR SHAPES YOUR INTRADAY CHART
Once CPR is drawn on your 5-min / 15-min chart, it stops being a random collection of lines. It changes what you look at and what you trust throughout the day. Below is exactly how it affects a live intraday chart.
1 • DAY-TYPE FORECAST (PRE-MARKET EDGE)
The width of the CPR band (|TC − BC|) tells you whether today is likely to trend or range — before the first candle prints.

• Narrow band → compressed energy → trending-day bias
• Wide band → energy already spent → range-day bias

Effect on your chart: you switch playbooks. Narrow → ORB & breakout setups. Wide → fade the extremes, stay inside R1–S1. (Full workflow in the CPR WIDTH — CALCULATION IN DETAIL block below.)
2 • DIRECTIONAL BIAS FILTER (CE vs PE)
CPR is a bias line, not an entry line. Where price sits relative to the band decides which side of the option chain to touch.

• Price above CPR → CE bias only
• Price below CPR → PE bias only
• Price inside CPR → no trade, wait for a break

Effect on your chart: removes 50% of random setups. If the pattern is bullish but price is below CPR, you skip it.
3 • AUTOMATIC S/R LADDER
Above CPR: R1 → R2 → R3 are automatic resistance targets. Below CPR: S1 → S2 → S3 are automatic support targets. No manually drawn trendlines — the levels plot themselves at market open.

Effect on your chart: your profit targets and stop-loss anchors are pre-defined. On a trending day breaking above TC, you know R1 is the first target; on a sideways day rejecting at R1, you know the fade target is PP.
4 • MAGNET & REJECTION BEHAVIOUR
Price repeatedly pulls toward CPR (magnet) or bounces off its edges (rejection). When price leaves the band sharply, it usually returns to test it at least once — this is the classic CPR pullback trade.

Effect on your chart: you get clear "expected retracement" zones after a breakout. A breakout candle above TC with volume → wait for pullback to TC (now-support) → that pullback + candle trigger is often the safest entry.
5 • NOISE FILTER (FEWER BAD TRADES)
Without CPR, a hammer on every 5-min chop looks tradable. With CPR, you only act on hammers that print at a meaningful level — TC, BC, R1, S1, PP.

Effect on your chart: the number of "tradable" setups drops by ~60%, but their hit rate climbs meaningfully. Fewer trades, bigger edge per trade — which is exactly what beats cost drag in Indian option buying.
6 • ZERO SUBJECTIVITY
CPR is pure arithmetic on three numbers: yesterday's H, L, C. No opinion, no drawing, no hindsight bias. Every trader looking at the same stock sees the same CPR — which is also why these levels self-fulfill.

Effect on your chart: reproducible lines, same every morning, same for every broker. Backtesting and journalling become honest, because the levels can't be retro-fitted.
HOW CPR IS CALCULATED & READ
HOW CPR IS CALCULATED (DAILY)
Based on PREVIOUS DAY'S High, Low, Close.

Pivot (PP) = (H + L + C) ÷ 3
BC (Bottom Central) = (H + L) ÷ 2
TC (Top Central) = (PP × 2) − BC

CPR = area between TC and BC

Plus support/resistance pivots:
R1 = PP × 2 − L · R2 = PP + (H − L) · R3 = H + 2(PP − L)
S1 = PP × 2 − H · S2 = PP − (H − L) · S3 = L − 2(H − PP)

Your broker plots these automatically — no manual maths needed.
CPR WIDTH — THE MOST IMPORTANT SIGNAL
DAY-TYPE FORECAST
Width = |TC − BC| — the distance between the two central lines. It predicts the type of day before market opens.

• Narrow (<0.2% of spot) → trending day likely. Trade breakouts aggressively.
• Normal (0.2–0.5%) → mixed. Wait for direction.
• Wide (>0.5%) → sideways / range day. Avoid breakout trades, fade extremes.

Full worked example, thresholds per index, and the "why" are in the CPR WIDTH — CALCULATION IN DETAIL block below.
PRICE VS CPR BIAS
DIRECTIONAL FILTER
• Price consistently above CPR → Bullish bias. Only take CE setups.
• Price consistently below CPR → Bearish bias. Only take PE setups.
• Price oscillating inside CPR → Don't trade. Wait for a sustained break above or below.
VIRGIN CPR — ULTRA-HIGH PROBABILITY
A CPR is called Virgin if price did NOT touch it during the entire previous trading day.

Why it matters: Virgin CPRs act as ultra-strong magnets. Price reaches them ~80% of the time within the next 1–2 sessions.

How to trade: Identify virgin CPR from prior day → wait for price to approach it → trade bounce (with candle + volume confirmation) back toward prior direction.
STANDARD PIVOT S/R LEVELS
Beyond CPR, the pivot set gives you automatic S/R levels for the day:

Resistances: R1 (most tested) · R2 (target for trending days) · R3 (exhaustion)
Supports: S1 (first bounce) · S2 (trend-day target) · S3 (panic low)

Use as: Profit targets when trading breakouts (R1 from CPR break, then R2), and stop-loss anchors.
OTHER PIVOT TYPES
Most platforms offer multiple pivot formulas — default Classical is fine.

• Classical / Standard → default, what most use
• Fibonacci Pivot → uses 38.2/61.8% of range for R1/S1. Better for volatile stocks.
• Camarilla → 8 intraday levels (H1–H4, L1–L4). Popular for scalping.
• Woodie's → weights prior close more. Less used.

Recommendation: Stick with Classical + CPR for 95% of use cases.
CPR WIDTH — CALCULATION IN DETAIL
CPR width is the single most predictive signal on this tool. Before the market opens, it tells you whether to expect a trending day (narrow) or a range-bound day (wide). Below is the exact maths with a worked example — once you do this twice by hand, you'll read CPR width at a glance.
STEP-BY-STEP FORMULA
Inputs — previous day's:
• High (H) · Low (L) · Close (C)

Step 1 — Pivot Point (PP):
PP = (H + L + C) ÷ 3

Step 2 — Bottom Central (BC):
BC = (H + L) ÷ 2

Step 3 — Top Central (TC):
TC = (PP × 2) − BC

Step 4 — CPR Width (absolute):
Width = |TC − BC|

Step 5 — CPR Width (normalised):
Width % = (Width ÷ Close) × 100

The % is what you compare across days and indices — 20 pts on Nifty is not the same as 20 pts on a ₹500 stock.
WORKED EXAMPLE — NIFTY
ONE FULL CALCULATION
Previous day's Nifty:
H = 26,250 · L = 26,050 · C = 26,180

PP = (26,250 + 26,050 + 26,180) ÷ 3
    = 78,480 ÷ 3 = 26,160

BC = (26,250 + 26,050) ÷ 2
    = 52,300 ÷ 2 = 26,150

TC = (26,160 × 2) − 26,150
    = 52,320 − 26,150 = 26,170

Width = |26,170 − 26,150| = 20 pts
Width % = (20 ÷ 26,180) × 100 = 0.076%

< 0.2% → NARROW CPR → expect a trending day. Look for a clean break above TC or below BC with volume; trade in that direction toward R1/S1.

WHY IT WORKS — THE INTUITION
NARROW CPR → TRENDING DAY
A narrow CPR means yesterday's close was near the midpoint of the day's range — buyers and sellers finished balanced. That balance is a compressed spring: when today's open tips the scale, positioning follows in one direction and the market runs.

Rule of thumb: if today's open is noticeably above TC with volume, the day usually trends up; noticeably below BC, it trends down. Breakout playbooks (ORB, supply/demand break) have their highest edge on these days.
WIDE CPR → RANGE DAY
A wide CPR means yesterday's close was far from the midpoint — one side got exhausted. Today, the most likely behaviour is mean reversion: price oscillates inside TC–BC or between R1 and S1.

Rule of thumb: fade extremes rather than chase them. Breakout trades fail the most on wide-CPR days — stick to reversion setups at R1/S1 with candle confirmation, or simply sit out.
Two things the width does NOT tell you: (1) direction — you still need price vs CPR bias plus a candle trigger; (2) certainty — big-event days (RBI, Fed, results) can flip a narrow CPR into a gap-and-reverse trap. CPR width is a conditional prior, not a forecast.
NORMAL vs INVERTED CPR — THE SHAPE SIGNAL
Width tells you the type of day. Shape tells you the bias of the day. When TC sits below BC on the chart, the CPR is inverted / flipped — a genuine bearish structural signal that most beginners miss because their broker's script labels lines by formula, not by visual position.
NORMAL CPR — TC ABOVE BC
PREV-DAY CLOSED IN UPPER HALF
What it is: TC plots above BC (conventional layout).

Why it forms: yesterday's Close ≥ (H + L) / 2 — the session closed in the upper half of its range. Buyers held control at the bell.

Bias it broadcasts: neutral with a mild bullish tilt for today.

How to read: treat TC as first resistance, BC as first support, PP as the pivot. Use the normal price-vs-CPR rules — long above, short below, no-trade inside.
INVERTED / FLIPPED CPR — TC BELOW BC
PREV-DAY CLOSED IN LOWER HALF
What it is: TC plots below BC — the lines appear visually flipped.

Why it forms: yesterday's Close < (H + L) / 2 — the session closed in the lower half of its range. Sellers had the last word. The formula TC = 2·PP − BC mechanically pushes TC under BC.

Bias it broadcasts: structurally bearish for today — a continuation lean, not a forecast.

How to read: treat the upper line (BC) as the harder resistance, lower line (TC) as the softer resistance. Above the entire band → bias flips and you stop shorting.
HOW TO SPOT INVERSION ON YOUR BROKER CHART
Watch the values, not the labels. Upstox / Dhan / Zerodha scripts label the lines by formula role (Top Pivot = TC, Bottom Pivot = BC) even when the visual order flips. If the line named "Top Pivot" has a smaller number than the one named "Bottom Pivot", the CPR is inverted — regardless of which line sits higher on the chart.
WORKED EXAMPLE — INVERTED CPR (NIFTY, LIVE READING)
Broker shows:
• Daily Top Pivot = 24,184.21 → this is TC
• Daily Central Pivot = 24,190.77 → this is PP
• Daily Bottom Pivot = 24,197.33 → this is BC

Check #1 — is it inverted? TC (24,184) < BC (24,197) → YES, inverted. Bearish tone.
Check #2 — width? |24,184.21 − 24,197.33| = 13.12 pts. On Nifty at ~24,043, that is 0.055% → ultra-narrow. Strong trending day.
Check #3 — where is price? Spot 24,043 is ~150 pts below the band → trend already down; CPR band is now stacked resistance.

Combined read: strong bearish trend day. Primary trade = fade rallies into TC (24,184) with a bearish candle trigger; buy PE; SL above BC (24,197); targets S1 → S2. Flip condition = clean 5-min close above 24,197 with volume.
HOW TO TRADE A NORMAL CPR
Above the band: CE bias. Buy pullbacks into TC with a bullish candle trigger. SL = below BC. Targets R1 → R2.

Below the band: PE bias. Sell rallies into BC with a bearish candle trigger. SL = above TC. Targets S1 → S2.

Inside the band: no trade. Let price pick a side.

Best when paired with: ORB + volume confirmation + narrow width.
HOW TO TRADE AN INVERTED CPR
Default bias: PE. Sell rallies into the lower edge of the band (TC) with a bearish candle trigger. SL = above the upper edge (BC). Targets S1 → S2.

Invalidation → switch to CE: a clean 5-min close above BC with expanding volume flips intraday structure. Wait for a retest of BC as support; only then take CE.

Avoid: aggressive long trades just because price is bouncing — the CPR shape is telling you sellers had the last word yesterday. Respect that until structure proves otherwise.
Inverted CPR is not "wrong" or rare — it's just a bearish-tone day. Most books skip this distinction and retail beginners end up long-biased even when the structure screams weakness. Checking inversion is a 2-second habit that pays for itself on every trending-down day.
CPR + CANDLE CONFIRMATION — THE ENTRY TRIGGER
CPR alone is only a bias filter, not an entry signal. You still need a candle pattern + volume at the level. CPR tells you where to trade; candles tell you when.
BULL SETUP EXAMPLE
PULLBACK TO TC
Condition: price pulls back to TC from above.
Trigger: bullish engulfing / hammer + above-average volume.
Action: buy ATM CE. SL: below BC. Target: R1 (first), R2 (trend day).
BEAR SETUP EXAMPLE
RALLY TO BC
Condition: price rallies to BC from below.
Trigger: bearish engulfing / shooting star + volume spike.
Action: buy ATM PE. SL: above TC. Target: S1 (first), S2 (trend day).
BREAKOUT SETUP
NARROW CPR BREAK
Condition: narrow CPR, price consolidates near TC for 30–60 min.
Trigger: breakout candle closes above TC with 1.5× average volume.
Action: buy CE. SL: back below TC. Target: R1 → R2.
USING PIVOT S/R LEVELS (R1, R2, R3 / S1, S2, S3)

HOW TO USE PIVOT LEVELS AS TARGETS AND STOPS

  • R1 / S1 → first profit target after a CPR break. Usually reached on normal-volatility days.
  • R2 / S2 → extended target for trend days. Typically hit only when momentum is strong and volume expands.
  • R3 / S3 → exhaustion zone — reached on fewer than 10% of days. If price tags R3/S3, book profits; don't expect more.
  • Stop-loss anchors: place SL 5–10 points beyond the next pivot level, never exactly on it — operators hunt round levels.
CPR DAILY ROUTINE — WHAT TO DO WHEN
Time (IST) Action with CPR Why it matters
8:45 AM Note today's CPR width (narrow / normal / wide) and location vs expected open. Decide day-type before the open — breakout strategy vs fade strategy.
9:15 AM Observe opening location: above TC, below BC, or inside CPR. Sets directional bias for the whole session.
9:15–9:30 Do NOT trade the opening 15 min. Let CPR "digest" the gap. First 15 min has maximum noise — operators sweep liquidity around CPR.
9:30 AM onward Trade only in direction of CPR bias + candle confirmation + volume. Highest probability window is 9:30–11:30.
12:00–1:30 CPR levels often re-tested. Good for adding to running trades on pullback. Lunch session — slow momentum, respect of levels is cleanest.
2:00 PM onward Tighten SL. Avoid fresh CPR breakouts. Late-day breakouts often fail — operators trap late entries and hunt stops into close.
COMMON CPR MISTAKES
TRADING INSIDE CPR
Price inside CPR is no-man's land. Theta bleeds your option and moves are small. Wait for a clean break above TC or below BC.
IGNORING CPR WIDTH
Trading breakouts on a wide CPR = losing money. Trading fades on a narrow CPR = missed trends. Always match strategy to width.
USING CPR ALONE
CPR is a filter, not a trigger. Without candle + volume, you're guessing. Combine CPR with price action for a real edge.
EXACT SL AT A PIVOT
Operators run stops parked at R1/S1. Place SL 5–10 points beyond the pivot, never on it.
WEEKLY/MONTHLY PIVOTS INTRADAY
Intraday trading uses daily pivots. Weekly pivots are for swing traders. Always confirm your reference timeframe.
IGNORING VIRGIN CPR
If yesterday's CPR was untouched, today's target is very likely to be it. Missing this setup is leaving free probability on the table.
CPR GREEN-LIGHT CHECKLIST

BEFORE EVERY CPR TRADE

  • Did I note today's CPR width (narrow / normal / wide)?
  • Is price CLEANLY above TC or below BC (not inside CPR)?
  • Is there a candle pattern at the pivot level (not just price touching)?
  • Is volume ≥ 1.5× the 20-candle average?
  • Is my SL placed beyond the next pivot, not exactly at it?
  • Is my target at least R1/S1 (minimum 1:1.5 risk-reward)?
  • Is this trade aligned with the day-type (breakout on narrow, fade on wide)?
  • Is it between 9:30 AM and 2:00 PM (the high-probability window)?
Rule of thumb: if CPR is narrow and volume is expanding, the break will run — stay with it. If CPR is wide and price chops inside, do not trade — fight the urge. Discipline at the CPR level is the 80/20 of intraday edge.
