RETRACEMENT & PULLBACKS HIGHEST-EV ENTRY IN THE BOOK

What is a retracement? A temporary counter-move within an established trend. Price "takes a breath" by retracing a portion of the prior leg, then resumes in the trend direction. A retracement is NOT a reversal — the trend structure remains intact.

Why it is the single best entry for option buyers: breakouts enter at extreme prices (expensive premium, late). Pullbacks enter during the pause (cheap premium, early). A well-timed pullback entry in a trending instrument gives you the best risk-reward of any setup — often 1 : 3 or better. This tab teaches you how to time them.
RETRACEMENT vs REVERSAL — THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION
Mistaking a reversal for a retracement is the #1 way pullback traders blow up. Use these 5 tests to distinguish — if 3+ fail, it's no longer a pullback; it's a new trend forming.
RETRACEMENT (BUY THE DIP)
· Trend structure intact (higher lows still holding)
· Counter-move is shallower & faster than the impulse
· Volume lower on the pullback
· Momentum (RSI) stays above 40 (or below 60 in downtrend)
· Price respects a prior support / Fib level
REVERSAL (TREND IS OVER)
· Structure broken (lower low formed / higher high lost)
· Counter-move equals or exceeds the impulse in size & speed
· Volume higher on the counter-move
· Momentum crosses the mid-line (RSI sub-40 from an uptrend)
· Price breaks through the previous swing level
PULLBACK vs THROWBACK — THE POST-BREAKOUT RETURN MOVE
After a valid breakout, price very often returns to retest the broken level before continuing. This return is NOT a failed breakout — it is a second-chance entry. Kirkpatrick's research: ~50–60% of all breakouts produce one of these two return moves within 5–10 bars. Knowing which one you're looking at determines the option (CE vs PE) and the direction of the trade.
THROWBACK (AFTER UP-BREAKOUT)
BULL CONTINUATION
What it is: After price breaks above a resistance, it dips back down to test that same level (now support). The candle bounces off and trend resumes up.

Statistics (Kirkpatrick & Bulkowski):
· Occurs in ~55% of upside breakouts
· Happens within 30 days (usually 3–10 bars)
· Successful throwback → higher probability of reaching target
· But patterns WITH throwback average slightly smaller moves than those that run without — because some energy is consumed

When to buy CE: Wait for reversal candle (hammer / bullish engulfing / pin bar) at the broken resistance. Enter on the NEXT candle open.
→ BUY CE at the retest bounce
PULLBACK (AFTER DOWN-BREAKOUT)
BEAR CONTINUATION
What it is: After price breaks below a support, it rallies back up to test that same level (now resistance). The candle rejects and trend resumes down.

Statistics (Kirkpatrick & Bulkowski):
· Occurs in ~60% of downside breakouts
· Happens within 30 days (usually 3–10 bars)
· Even more common than throwbacks — fear rallies fade faster
· Successful pullback → high probability of full measured decline
· Same energy-consumption effect applies

When to buy PE: Wait for rejection candle (shooting star / bearish engulfing / pin bar) at the broken support. Enter on the NEXT candle open.
→ BUY PE at the retest rejection

REMEMBER THIS MNEMONIC

  • THROW-back → thrown UP — market threw price up past resistance, then it drops back to test. Direction of trend: UP.
  • PULL-back → pulled DOWN — market pulled price down past support, then it rallies up to test. Direction of trend: DOWN.
  • Both are RETESTS after a successful break — NOT reversals. The broken level MUST hold for the retest to qualify. If it doesn't hold, you're looking at a failed breakout (see TRAPS tab), not a pullback/throwback.
  • Key distinction from "retracement": A retracement is a pause WITHIN a trend. A pullback/throwback is the first dip or rally AFTER a valid pattern breakout. The concepts overlap but the trigger is different.
ENTRY RULES — PULLBACK / THROWBACK TRADE
RULE 1 · CONFIRM THE BREAK FIRST
The original break must be valid: closing candle beyond the level + volume > 1.3× 20-bar average. No break-confirmation = no pullback/throwback trade.
RULE 2 · WAIT FOR THE RETEST
Price must return to (within 0.3× ATR of) the broken level — not exceed it. A retest that closes back through = failed breakout, skip the trade.
RULE 3 · REQUIRE A REVERSAL CANDLE
Hammer, engulfing, or pin bar AT the broken level, in the direction of the original break. No candle = coin-flip. Wait.
RULE 4 · VOLUME PROFILE
Ideal: volume declines on the retest (weak counter-move) then expands on the reversal candle. Opposite volume pattern = suspect.
RULE 5 · TIME WINDOW
Retests that happen within 3–10 bars of the break are the most reliable. Beyond 15–20 bars, the original breakout thesis weakens — re-evaluate before trading.
RULE 6 · STOP & TARGET
SL: just beyond the retest extreme (opposite side of the broken level). Target: measured-move target from the ORIGINAL pattern, because energy lost in retest is offset by higher probability.
Why traders LOVE pullbacks & throwbacks: They solve the two biggest problems with breakout trading — late entry at expensive premium, and uncertainty about whether the break is real. By the time the retest prints, you already know the break worked (price held). And you enter deeper, at cheaper option premium, with a tight, structural stop at the retest low/high. It's the single most under-used entry by retail option buyers in India.
THREE PULLBACK ZONES — WHAT TO DO IN EACH
SHALLOW · 23–38%
AGGRESSIVE ENTRY
Signals very strong trend. Quick, shallow dips. Enter aggressively with smaller size; expect fast continuation. Ideal for momentum / flag-breakout setups.
MODERATE · 38–61.8%
OPTIMAL ENTRY
The sweet spot. Full-size entries, best risk-reward. Reversal candle at 50%–61.8% with volume = near-textbook high-EV pullback trade.
DEEP · > 78.6%
CAUTION
Trend in danger. Skip unless you have overwhelming confluence (key S/R + reversal candle + divergence). Reduce size. Quick exit plan essential.
CONFLUENCE — STACK MULTIPLE SIGNALS AT THE PULLBACK
A single Fibonacci level alone is a 50-50 bet. Stack 3+ signals at the same price zone and your hit-rate jumps above 70%.
FIB + PRIOR S/R
Fib 50% / 61.8% coincides with a previous resistance that became support. Highest-EV confluence — textbook continuation entry.
FIB + MOVING AVERAGE
Fib aligns with 20 / 50 EMA. Dynamic support confirms the Fib zone. Very common in trending equities.
FIB + TRENDLINE
Rising trend-line meets Fib retracement. Two independent supports converging — operators love these setups.
FIB + CPR / PIVOT
Fib coincides with CPR mid / pivot S1 / TC-BC level. See CPR tab for pivot-level nuances.
FIB + VOLUME POC
Fib lines up with a high-volume node (volume profile POC / value-area edge). Heavy prior trading = strong support.
FIB + ROUND NUMBER
Fib near a psychological level (NIFTY 22,000, RELIANCE ₹2,500). Round numbers anchor price; confluence amplifies.
PULLBACK RULE OF THIRDS
1st PULLBACK
HIGHEST EV
After the first strong trend leg. Most reliable entry of any pullback. Smallest SL, biggest remaining move.
2nd PULLBACK
GOOD
Still high probability. Slightly tighter stop; slightly smaller move left. Use confluence to confirm.
3rd+ PULLBACK
CAUTION
Trend maturity warning. Late-stage — likely exhaustion ahead. Reduce size or skip; next pullback may become a reversal.

6-STEP PULLBACK PLAYBOOK

  • 1 · IDENTIFY THE TREND — daily + 1-hour must show higher highs / higher lows (or opposite for shorts).
  • 2 · MARK THE IMPULSE LEG — draw Fibonacci from the swing low to the latest swing high.
  • 3 · WATCH THE 38–61.8% ZONE — this is the primary entry band.
  • 4 · WAIT FOR REVERSAL CANDLE — hammer, bullish engulfing, or morning-star at the Fib level. Entry on next candle open.
  • 5 · CONFIRM VOLUME & STRUCTURE — volume on reversal, higher low holding, RSI still >40. 3 of 3 required.
  • 6 · MANAGE — SL below the pullback low. Trail to breakeven at 1:1. Partial at 1:2, ride the rest to prior swing high / Fib extension.

BEGINNER PRIORITY — MASTER THESE FIRST

  • First pullback after a fresh breakout — simplest, highest probability, textbook setup.
  • 20 EMA pullback in a clean trend — beginners can spot it with one indicator; works on most liquid stocks.
  • 50%–61.8% Fib in trend direction — the golden-zone pullback; scale into it with a reversal candle.
  • Avoid the 78.6% zone for the first 3 months — too close to reversal territory; requires experience to trade safely.
The single biggest pullback-trader mistake: entering without a reversal candle at the level. Buying just because price "reached Fib 50%" is a coin-flip — wait for the market to confirm the level is holding (a hammer, engulfing, or morning star printing AT the zone). Patience here separates profitable pullback traders from everyone else.
FIBONACCI RETRACEMENT & EXTENSION — FULL PLAYBOOK
Fibonacci levels are where retail traders, algos, and institutions all watch for bounces. They're self-fulfilling — so many eyes on 61.8% that it often reacts just because everyone expects it to.
FIBONACCI RETRACEMENT — KEY LEVELS
PULLBACK ENTRY LEVELS
The 6 key levels:
• 23.6% → shallow pullback (strong trends)
• 38.2% → normal pullback
• 50% KEY → psychological midpoint
• 61.8% GOLDEN RATIO → THE most-watched level
• 78.6% → deep pullback, last chance
• >100% → retracement failed, trend over
HOW TO DRAW FIB CORRECTLY
In UPTREND (looking for CE entry):
Draw from swing LOW to swing HIGH of the impulse move. Retracements fall BETWEEN the extremes — buy on bounce from 50–61.8%.

In DOWNTREND (looking for PE entry):
Draw from swing HIGH to swing LOW. Sell on rejection at 50–61.8% bounce.

Timeframe to draw on: Match your trading TF — 15-min Fib for intraday, daily Fib for swing.
THE GOLDEN ZONE (50%–61.8%)
A+ ENTRY ZONE
The 50–61.8% zone is where 70%+ of healthy retracements reverse. If a candle pattern (hammer, engulfing, morning star) forms inside this zone AT a prior S/R level = highest-probability trade in the book.
FIBONACCI EXTENSION (TARGETS)
PROFIT TARGET LEVELS
Used to project how far price will move after a successful retracement bounce.
• 1.272 / 1.414 → conservative T1
• 1.618 KEY → primary target (most common reaction point)
• 2.618 → extended move, for runner positions

Rule: Book 50% at 1.272–1.414, trail rest to 1.618.
FIB + S/R CONFLUENCE = GOLD
The magic combo: When a Fibonacci level (especially 61.8%) lines up with a pre-existing horizontal S/R level OR a moving average → treat this as confluence. Statistically, confluence zones react ~80% of the time.

Example: 15-min Fib 61.8% retracement = 26,080. Daily support = 26,075. 50 EMA = 26,082. → Strong CE entry zone.
FIB MISTAKES TO AVOID
• Drawing too many Fibs — use only the last major swing, not every wiggle
• Trading Fib level alone — always require candle confirmation
• Ignoring trend direction — only trade Fib WITH higher-TF trend
• Obsessing over exact price — treat levels as ±0.1% zones
• Using sub-5-min Fib — noise-level, meaningless
HOW TO DRAW A FIBONACCI RETRACEMENT — STEP BY STEP
The retracement tool answers: "after a strong move, where is price likely to pause and reverse?" Drawing it correctly is 80% of the work — wrong anchors = wrong levels = wrong trades.
THE 5 STEPS
STEP 1 — identify the trend direction
Look at the higher timeframe (1-hour or daily). Is price in an uptrend, downtrend, or sideways? Never draw a Fib in a sideways market.

STEP 2 — find the IMPULSE leg
Pick the latest clean, strong, one-directional move — multiple green candles with volume (uptrend) or multiple red candles (downtrend). Avoid choppy swings.

STEP 3 — select the Fibonacci Retracement tool
On your broker chart, select the Fib Retracement tool (keyboard shortcut on TradingView / Upstox: Alt + F).

STEP 4 — click your two anchor points
• UPTREND: click at the swing LOW first, then drag to the swing HIGH.
• DOWNTREND: click at the swing HIGH first, then drag to the swing LOW.

STEP 5 — read the retracement levels
The tool auto-plots horizontal lines at 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%. Price will pull back into these. Focus on the golden zone: 50–61.8%.
WORKED EXAMPLE — NIFTY UPTREND
REAL NUMBERS, HAND-CALCULATED
Impulse leg (uptrend):
Swing LOW = 23,800 · Swing HIGH = 24,300
⇒ Impulse range = 500 pts

Retracement formula (uptrend):
Level = HIGH − (range × X%)

Compute each level:
• 23.6% → 24,300 − (500 × 0.236) = 24,182
• 38.2% → 24,300 − (500 × 0.382) = 24,109
• 50.0% → 24,300 − (500 × 0.500) = 24,050
• 61.8% → 24,300 − (500 × 0.618) = 23,991
• 78.6% → 24,300 − (500 × 0.786) = 23,907

GOLDEN ZONE = 23,991 – 24,050
Trade logic: if price pulls back into 23,991–24,050 AND prints a bullish candle (hammer / engulfing) AND volume spikes → high-probability CE entry. For a downtrend, flip all signs: swing HIGH is 0%, swing LOW is 100%, and you are looking to short a rally into the golden zone with a bearish candle.
HOW TO DRAW A FIBONACCI EXTENSION — PROJECTING YOUR TARGET
The extension tool answers: "if the trend continues after the pullback, where is the next profit target?" Extensions project levels beyond the impulse high (uptrend) or low (downtrend).
THE 4 STEPS (2-POINT METHOD — EASIEST)
STEP 1 — draw the Fib retracement first
Use the same anchors (swing LOW → swing HIGH for uptrend). Most Fib tools show extension levels automatically once retracement is drawn.

STEP 2 — enable extension levels
In the Fib tool settings, tick the boxes for 1.272, 1.414, 1.618, and 2.618. These auto-plot above the swing high (uptrend).

STEP 3 — read the target ladder
• 1.272 / 1.414 → first profit target (book 50% here)
• 1.618 (golden target) → primary profit target
• 2.618 → stretch target for runners

STEP 4 — confirm target with context
Always check if the extension level aligns with a prior horizontal S/R, pivot, or round number. Alignment = confluence → highest-probability reaction point.
WORKED EXAMPLE — NIFTY TARGET LADDER
FROM THE SAME 500-PT IMPULSE
Same impulse as before:
Swing LOW = 23,800 · Swing HIGH = 24,300 · Range = 500 pts

Extension formula (uptrend):
Target = LOW + (range × X)

Compute each target:
• 1.272 → 23,800 + (500 × 1.272) = 24,436
• 1.414 → 23,800 + (500 × 1.414) = 24,507
• 1.618 (golden target) → 23,800 + (500 × 1.618) = 24,609
• 2.618 → 23,800 + (500 × 2.618) = 25,109

YOUR PROFIT LADDER: T1 = 24,436 · T2 = 24,609 · T3 = 25,109

Execution plan (after a valid golden-zone entry near 24,000):
• Book 50% at 1.272 = 24,436
• Book 30% at 1.618 = 24,609
• Trail remaining 20% with a moving average; exit on trend break or at 2.618 = 25,109.
The 3-point method is more accurate because it uses the actual retracement low instead of assuming one. Use it once a clean pullback has confirmed in the golden zone (50–61.8%).
3-POINT METHOD (TREND-BASED FIB EXTENSION)
When to use: after a clear retracement has already happened. This is the more accurate method because it uses the actual completed pullback as an anchor, instead of assuming where one will end.

The 3 anchors (uptrend):
• Point A = swing LOW (start of impulse)
• Point B = swing HIGH (end of impulse)
• Point C = pullback LOW (after retracement, ideally in the 50–61.8% zone)

What it measures: the projected continuation move from C, scaled to the original A→B impulse. Ratios (1.272, 1.618, 2.618) are calculated from C, not from A.

In TradingView / Upstox: select the "Trend-Based Fib Extension" tool. Click 3 times in sequence (A → B → C). Levels auto-plot above C.

For downtrend: A = swing HIGH, B = swing LOW, C = pullback HIGH. Levels project below C.
WORKED EXAMPLE — 3-POINT EXTENSION (NIFTY)
CONTINUATION TARGETS FROM PULLBACK
Setup (uptrend):
• A = LOW 23,800
• B = HIGH 24,300
• C = pullback LOW 24,000 (clean 60% retracement — golden zone)

Impulse range (A→B): 500 pts

Extension formula (3-point, uptrend):
Target = C + (range × X)

Compute targets from C:
• 1.000 → 24,000 + (500 × 1.000) = 24,500
• 1.272 → 24,000 + (500 × 1.272) = 24,636
• 1.618 (golden) → 24,000 + (500 × 1.618) = 24,809
• 2.618 → 24,000 + (500 × 2.618) = 25,309

2-point vs 3-point compared: 2-point gave 24,436 / 24,609 / 25,109. 3-point gives 24,636 / 24,809 / 25,309 (~200 pts higher because the pullback is "given back"). Use 3-point only AFTER a confirmed reversal candle in the retracement zone.
FIBONACCI EXTENSION — KEY CONCEPTS YOU MUST KNOW
Drawing the tool is mechanical — understanding which level matters in which context is the actual edge. These cards cover everything else you need to know about extensions.
EACH EXTENSION LEVEL EXPLAINED
1.000: the original swing high/low. Re-test target after a deep retracement.

1.272: √1.618. First profit target — book 30–50% here. Often coincides with prior resistance / round numbers.

1.414: √2. Conservative T1 used by some traders. Same role as 1.272.

1.618 (golden target): THE most-watched extension. Phi (φ) — the golden ratio. ~60%+ of strong continuation moves stop at or just past this level. Primary book-profit zone.

2.000: exactly 2× the impulse. Round-number magnet for psychological reasons.

2.618: φ-squared. Stretch target for strong trend days — trail the last 20% of position here.

3.618 / 4.236: "blow-off" levels. Only relevant on rare parabolic moves. Most option buyers will never touch these.
WHY 1.618 IS THE GOLDEN TARGET
THE GOLDEN RATIO TARGET
Phi (φ = 1.618) is hard-wired into nature, art, architecture — and into market psychology. Algos, retail, and institutions all watch this level. Result: massive resting orders cluster around 1.618 ⇒ price reacts there with statistical significance.

Practical rule: set your primary book-profit zone at 1.618. If price stalls there with a reversal candle → close the trade. Don't get greedy waiting for 2.618.
EXTENSION + CONFLUENCE = HIGH-PROBABILITY TARGET
A single Fib level is suggestive. Fib + 1 other system = strong. Fib + 2 other systems = jackpot.

Confluence sources to overlay on extensions:
• Prior swing high / swing low (horizontal S/R)
• Round numbers (24,000 / 24,500 / 25,000)
• CPR pivot R1, R2, S1, S2
• Daily / weekly classical pivots
• 50 / 200 EMA on higher timeframe
• VWAP (especially weekly VWAP for swing targets)

Example: 1.618 extension = 24,605. Daily R1 = 24,610. Round number = 24,600. Three-way confluence → very high probability of reaction. Book aggressively.
RETRACEMENT vs EXTENSION vs PROJECTION
Beginners confuse these three. Quick reference:

RETRACEMENT (entry tool): drawn between two points; levels fall between them. Answers "where will price pause and reverse?"

EXTENSION (target tool): drawn between two or three points; levels project beyond them. Answers "how far will price move after the pullback?"

PROJECTION: a 3-point variant projecting a similar-sized leg forward. In TradingView this is the "Trend-Based Fib Extension" tool. Effectively the same thing for option-buyer use.

Rule of thumb: use retracement to ENTER, use extension to EXIT. Together they form a complete trade plan from entry to target.
WHEN NOT TO USE EXTENSIONS
• Sideways / range market — no clean impulse to anchor to
• After a fake / failed breakout — the impulse was a trap, not a real move
• Sub-5-min charts — price noise dominates the projection
• Against higher-TF trend — counter-trend extensions rarely complete
• Low-volatility days (ATR contracted) — price won't have fuel to reach 1.618
EXTENSION MISTAKES TO AVOID
• Targeting only 2.618 — greedy; miss the safer 1.618 exit
• No partial booking — book 50% at 1.272, trail rest
• Holding past time-stop — if extension stalls, theta eats premium fast
• Drawing extension during the retracement — premature; wait for confirmed reversal candle in golden zone first
• Ignoring confluence — a Fib level alone is suggestive; with confluence it's actionable
FIBONACCI — COMPLETE TRADE PLAYBOOK
THE 8-POINT FIBONACCI PLAYBOOK
WHY IT MATTERS: Fibonacci levels are self-fulfilling because retail, algos, and institutions all watch them. The 61.8% golden ratio is the single most-respected retracement level in all of technical analysis. Drawing correctly removes guesswork about where to enter pullbacks and where to book profits.

HOW TO READ:
• Shallow pullback (23.6 / 38.2%) → trend is very strong; expect continuation
• Golden zone (50 / 61.8%) → healthy pullback; best entry zone
• Deep pullback (78.6%) → trend weakening; last-chance entry
• Break beyond 100% → retracement failed, trend is over — stand aside

BUY CE WHEN: Higher-TF uptrend + price pulls back into 50–61.8% golden zone + bullish reversal candle (hammer / bullish engulfing / morning star) at the zone + volume spike. Best if zone overlaps a horizontal S/R or moving average (confluence).
BUY PE WHEN: Higher-TF downtrend + price rallies up into 50–61.8% zone + bearish reversal candle (shooting star / bearish engulfing) + volume spike. Confluence with resistance increases edge.

SL: Just beyond the 78.6% level (uptrend: below 78.6%; downtrend: above 78.6%). If price takes out 78.6%, the Fib setup is invalidated — the trend is breaking.
TARGET: Use the extension ladder: T1 = 1.272 / 1.414 (book 50%) · T2 = 1.618 (book 30%) · T3 = 2.618 (trail last 20%).

TIMEFRAME: 15-min / 1-hour Fib for intraday option buyers · daily Fib for swing / positional. Never draw Fibs on sub-5-min charts — the swings are noise.
AVOID: (1) Sideways markets — no real impulse to anchor to. (2) Fib drawn on a choppy leg — garbage in, garbage out. (3) Entering at a Fib level without a candle trigger. (4) Drawing multiple Fibs across every wiggle — stick to the one latest clean impulse.
