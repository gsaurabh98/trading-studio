OPTIONS BUYING GUIDE STRIKE · EXPIRY · ORDER

You can be correct on direction and still lose. Most retail option buyers pick strikes too far OTM, expiries too close to zero, and trade without a pre-defined exit. These three mistakes cost more money than wrong direction calls ever do.
Uncomfortable fact from SEBI Sep-2024 study (FY22–FY24): option buyers were hit the hardest — the loss-ratio is significantly higher for buyers than writers, because time decay and IV crush work against the long side every single day. This guide is for buyers who accept that asymmetry and still choose to engage, with tight risk rules.
WHAT IS AN OPTION? — THE 5 CORE COMPONENTS
An option is a contract. It gives the buyer the right (but not the obligation) to buy or sell an underlying asset at a pre-decided price, on or before a set date. The seller (writer) takes the opposite side and collects the premium. Understanding the five components below is non-negotiable before you place a single trade.
1 • CALL OPTION (CE)
Gives the buyer the right to BUY the underlying at the strike price. Used when you expect price to go UP.

Example: Nifty spot at 24,300. Buy NIFTY 24400 CE @ ₹80. If Nifty rallies to 24,600, the premium may rise to ₹210 — a 162% profit. If Nifty falls, the option loses value fast.
2 • PUT OPTION (PE)
Gives the buyer the right to SELL the underlying at the strike price. Used when you expect price to go DOWN.

Example: Nifty spot at 24,300. Buy NIFTY 24200 PE @ ₹70. If Nifty falls to 24,000, the premium may rise to ₹190. If Nifty rallies, the option loses value.
3 • STRIKE PRICE
The pre-decided price at which the option can be exercised. Chosen by the trader from a list on the option chain (e.g., 24,000 / 24,100 / 24,200 …). Strike relative to spot defines moneyness:

ATM — strike = spot
ITM — strike is already profitable
OTM — strike needs spot to move to be profitable
4 • PREMIUM
The price you pay the seller (writer) for the contract. Quoted per share; total cost = Premium × Lot Size.

Example: NIFTY 24400 CE at premium ₹80, lot size 65 → cost = 80 × 65 = ₹5,200.

Premium = Intrinsic Value + Time Value. Time Value evaporates daily — this is theta decay.
5 • EXPIRATION DATE
The date the contract expires and settles. After expiry the option is worth only its intrinsic value (often zero).

Weekly: Nifty (Tue), Sensex (Thu) • Monthly: BankNifty, FinNifty — last Tue of month.

Wasting asset: every option loses time value daily; holding longer ≠ more profit.
BONUS • BUYER vs WRITER
Every option has two sides.

Buyer (retail): pays premium. Max loss = premium paid. Max profit = unlimited (CE) / very large but capped at strike × lot (PE).

Writer (institution, HNI): receives premium. Max profit = premium. Max loss = unlimited (CE) / very large but capped at strike × lot (PE) — which is why writing requires margin & capital that retail rarely has.

Reality check (SEBI Sep-2024): 93% of individual F&O traders — almost all of whom are buyers — lost money in FY24, with an average per-trader loss of ~₹2 lakh.

Every option contract has a unique 5-part symbol. Once you can decode one, you understand them all.

HOW OPTIONS MAKE / LOSE MONEY — MENTAL MODEL
FOR CALL OPTION BUYERS (CE)
You profit when: underlying rises above strike + premium paid before expiry.
You lose when: underlying stays below strike, OR stays flat (theta decay), OR IV crashes.

Break-even: Strike + Premium. For 24400 CE @ ₹80, Nifty must reach 24,480 just to break even at expiry.

Max loss: 100% of premium (₹5,200 in the example). Max profit: theoretically unlimited.
FOR PUT OPTION BUYERS (PE)
You profit when: underlying falls below strike - premium paid before expiry.
You lose when: underlying stays above strike, OR stays flat (theta decay), OR IV crashes.

Break-even: Strike - Premium. For 24200 PE @ ₹70, Nifty must fall to 24,130 just to break even at expiry.

Max loss: 100% of premium. Max profit: large (capped when underlying = 0).
The "wasting asset" truth. An option that is ₹80 today can be ₹65 tomorrow and ₹40 the day after — even if the underlying doesn't move. Time is not on your side as a buyer. This is why scalping (see Scalping tab) and buying only current-week ATM strikes are the cleanest entry paths for retail. Never "hold overnight hoping it recovers".

ORDER TYPES — EXECUTION MATTERS
LIMIT ORDER
USE THIS FOR OPTIONS
Fills at your chosen price or better. Avoids the wide bid-ask spread on options, especially at open. Always use a limit buy at the mid-price — you save ₹3–10 per trade vs market.
MARKET ORDER
AVOID FOR OPTIONS
Fills at the best available price — meaning the ask when buying. On slow-moving strikes you can lose ₹10+ per lot to slippage. Only acceptable for breakouts needing instant fill.
BRACKET ORDER (BO)
AUTO SL + TARGET
One entry + SL + target placed together. Automatically closes on either hit. Good for disciplined execution. Note: most brokers disabled BO for options in 2024 — check if your broker offers it.
COVER ORDER (CO)
ENTRY + SL
Entry + mandatory SL in one order. Provides higher intraday margin on equity, but generally not enabled for options post-SEBI margin framework changes.

COSTS & TAXATION — KNOW YOUR BREAK-EVEN
COST BREAKDOWN PER TRADE
Option buying, round-trip (1 lot Nifty):
• Brokerage: ₹20 × 2 = ₹40
• STT (sell side): 0.10% of premium value
• Exchange txn charges: ~₹3.5
• GST (18% on brokerage): ~₹7
• SEBI + Stamp: ₹1–2

Total ≈ ₹55–70 + 0.10% STT. For ₹150 ATM × 65 = ₹9,750 premium, STT alone ≈ ₹10.
BREAK-EVEN CALCULATION
Entry premium: ₹150
Costs: ~₹70 + STT
Lot: 65 units
Break-even premium sell: ~₹151.2

Doesn't sound like much — but on 10 trades/month, ₹700 leaks out to costs alone. A 60% win rate at 1:1 RR = 0 after costs. You NEED 1:2 minimum just to be profitable after fees.
EXERCISE-STT TRAP — THE ITM-EXPIRY LANDMINE
If you let an ITM option expire instead of squaring off, STT jumps to 0.125% of the FULL intrinsic settlement value (not just premium). This is the single biggest silent retail wipe-out cost.

Example: Nifty 26,000 CE, spot settles 26,050, lot 65.
• Sold before 3:30 PM → STT on premium ≈ ₹5–10
• Let expire ITM → STT = 0.125% × 26,050 × 65 ≈ ₹2,117

Rule: Always square off ITM options before expiry close. The "do nothing" button is the most expensive button on your broker app.
TAXATION (F&O INTRADAY & POSITIONAL)
F&O income = Non-speculative business income under Section 43(5)(d) of the Income-Tax Act (not capital gains, not speculative).
• Taxed at your applicable slab rate, added to total income
• Losses can offset any non-speculative business income, carry-forward 8 years
• Tax audit (Sec 44AB) if turnover > ₹10 cr, or if opting out of presumptive taxation with profit <6% of turnover
• Turnover = sum of absolute profit/loss on each trade (ICAI Guidance Note)
• STT, brokerage, exchange & SEBI charges = allowable deductions

Always consult a qualified CA — treatment of presumptive 44AD for F&O is contested. This note is educational, not tax advice.
COMMON COST MISTAKES
Averaging losing options → 2× the STT drain
Scalping for ₹5 → costs eat the profit
Chasing deep OTM → low Delta + high costs = mathematically losing
Over-trading → 10 trades/day × ₹70 = ₹700 daily toll
2–3 trades/day at 1:2 RR = costs become noise
