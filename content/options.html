OPTIONS BUYING GUIDE STRIKE · EXPIRY · ORDER

Read this first: You can guess the direction RIGHT and still lose money on an option. How? Most beginners pick the wrong strike (too far away), the wrong expiry (too close to expiry day), and they have no exit plan. These three mistakes cost MORE money than wrong direction calls ever do. This page fixes all three.
An eye-opener fact: The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) studied Indian retail traders from April 2021 to March 2024. The result? 93 out of every 100 retail option buyers LOST money. The average loss was about ₹2 lakh per trader.

Why? Because two invisible forces work against an option BUYER every single day:
· Time decay (called Theta): The option loses value every day, like ice melting. Even if the market does not move, you LOSE money.
· Volatility crush (called the Implied Volatility drop): When fear in the market drops, option premiums shrink fast. Even if you get the direction RIGHT, the premium can drop.

This page is for buyers who accept this reality and still choose to trade — with tight rules.
WHAT IS AN OPTION? — THINK OF IT LIKE A MOVIE TICKET BOOKING
The simplest way to understand an option: Think of booking a movie ticket online for a show 3 days from now.

· You pay a small booking fee (the premium).
· The fee gives you the right to watch the movie at the locked-in price (the strike price).
· If you decide NOT to go on movie day, you simply forfeit the booking fee. Maximum loss = the small booking fee, nothing more.
· If the movie tickets become very expensive on movie day (a sold-out show), your locked-in booking is now valuable — you can transfer it to someone else for a profit.

That is exactly how an option works. You pay a small premium today for the RIGHT to buy or sell at a fixed price later. If price moves in your favour, the option becomes valuable. If not, you lose only the small premium.

Two parties exist in every option trade:
· The BUYER (you) — pays the premium. Has the RIGHT, not the obligation.
· The SELLER (also called the WRITER) — collects the premium. Has the obligation if the buyer chooses to use the right. Sellers are usually big institutions and high-net-worth individuals (because writing requires lots of margin money).
1 • CALL OPTION (buy this when you think price will GO UP)
A Call Option gives you the right to BUY the index at a fixed price. You buy a Call Option when you EXPECT the index to go UP.

Real example: Nifty is at 24,300 right now. You buy the "NIFTY 24400 Call Option" for a premium of ₹80 per share.
· If Nifty rallies to 24,600 → the premium can jump to ₹210. That is a 162 percent profit.
· If Nifty drops instead → the option loses value FAST and you can lose most of the ₹80.

Memory trick: Call = Calling price UP.
2 • PUT OPTION (buy this when you think price will GO DOWN)
A Put Option gives you the right to SELL the index at a fixed price. You buy a Put Option when you EXPECT the index to go DOWN.

Real example: Nifty is at 24,300 right now. You buy the "NIFTY 24200 Put Option" for a premium of ₹70 per share.
· If Nifty drops to 24,000 → the premium can jump to ₹190. Big profit.
· If Nifty rallies instead → the option loses value.

Memory trick: Put = Putting price DOWN.
3 • STRIKE PRICE (the locked-in price)
The strike price is the fixed price you lock in when you buy the option. You pick a strike from a long list shown in the option chain (24,000, 24,100, 24,200, and so on, in steps of 50 or 100).

Three types of strike (called "moneyness"):
· At The Money (ATM) — the strike is right around today’s spot price. (Most beginner-friendly.)
· In The Money (ITM) — the strike is already profitable. The premium is high but the option moves like the index.
· Out Of The Money (OTM) — the strike is far away. The premium is cheap but most of the time it expires worthless.

Beginner rule: always start with At The Money. Never buy far Out Of The Money because it "looks cheap".
4 • PREMIUM (the price you pay)
The premium is the price you PAY the seller for the option. It is quoted per share — so to know your total cost, multiply it by the lot size.

Real example: The NIFTY 24400 Call Option has a premium of ₹80. The Nifty lot size is 65. So total cost = 80 × 65 = ₹5,200. That is the maximum you can lose.

The premium has two parts:
· Intrinsic Value — how much profit the option already has if you were to use it RIGHT NOW.
· Time Value — the extra amount you pay because there is still time left for price to move in your favour. This time value SHRINKS every single day (this shrinking is called Theta decay).
5 • EXPIRY DATE (the deadline)
The expiry date is the deadline for the option. After this date, the option is finished — it is either worth its intrinsic value or it is worth zero.

Indian index expiry days (current rules):
· Nifty — weekly expiry every Tuesday at 3:30 PM IST.
· Sensex — weekly expiry every Thursday at 3:30 PM IST.
· Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty — only monthly expiry now (last Tuesday / Wednesday of the month). Weekly was discontinued in November 2024.

Important: An option is a "wasting asset". Every option loses time value daily. Holding LONGER does not mean MORE profit — it usually means LESS profit.
BONUS • BUYER vs SELLER (who is on the other side?)
Every option has two sides — like every insurance policy has a buyer and a company.

BUYER (mostly retail traders, like you):
· PAYS the premium upfront.
· Maximum loss = ONLY the premium paid.
· Maximum profit = very large (unlimited for a Call Option).

SELLER (also called WRITER) (mostly big institutions and rich individuals):
· RECEIVES the premium upfront.
· Maximum profit = ONLY the premium received.
· Maximum loss = very large (unlimited for a Call Option).
· Needs huge margin money — that is why retail rarely sells.

Reality check: The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) study from 2024 found that 93 out of every 100 retail traders LOST money in Futures and Options. Average loss was around ₹2 lakh per trader. Most retail traders are BUYERS — the side that loses the most.

Every option contract has a unique 5-part symbol. Once you can decode one, you can understand them all.

Part Meaning Explanation
NIFTY Underlying The index (or stock) that the option is based on — the Nifty 50 index in this case.
17-NOV-2026 Expiration Date The day this contract expires and settles. For Nifty weekly options this is always Tuesday. After this date, the option ceases to exist.

HOW OPTIONS MAKE OR LOSE MONEY — THE SIMPLE PICTURE
FOR CALL OPTION BUYERS (you bet UP)
You MAKE money when: the index goes UP enough — specifically, above (strike price + premium you paid) by the expiry day.

You LOSE money when:
· the index drops, OR
· the index stays flat (time decay eats the premium), OR
· the fear in the market suddenly drops (the volatility crush).

Break-even point: Strike + Premium. So for the 24400 Call Option bought at ₹80 → Nifty must reach 24,480 just to BREAK EVEN by expiry day.

Maximum loss: 100 percent of the premium (so ₹5,200 in this example).
Maximum profit: in theory, unlimited.
FOR PUT OPTION BUYERS (you bet DOWN)
You MAKE money when: the index goes DOWN enough — specifically, below (strike price - premium paid) by expiry.

You LOSE money when:
· the index rises, OR
· the index stays flat (time decay), OR
· the fear in the market suddenly drops.

Break-even point: Strike - Premium. So for the 24200 Put Option bought at ₹70 → Nifty must fall to 24,130 just to BREAK EVEN by expiry day.

Maximum loss: 100 percent of the premium.
Maximum profit: very large (capped only when the index reaches zero).
The melting ice cube reality: An option that costs ₹80 today can be ₹65 tomorrow and ₹40 the day after — even if the index does not move at all. Time is NOT on your side when you are a buyer. The premium melts every day, like an ice cube in the sun.

That is why these two rules matter:
· Trade only the current-week At The Money strikes.
· Never "hold overnight hoping it recovers" — the next morning you will lose more from time decay alone.

When to take this trade — The right strike for your first intraday option trade
Setup
You have a clean entry signal (a candle pattern at support / resistance with volume confirmation), India Volatility Index is between 12 and 18, and the time is between 9:30 AM and 2:00 PM IST.
Entry
Buy the At The Money strike of the current-week expiry with a limit order at the mid-price (the average of the bid and ask). Position size: NEVER risk more than 1 percent of total capital on this single trade.
Stop Loss
30 percent loss on the option premium — this is the hard stop, no exceptions. For a premium of ₹100, the stop is ₹70.
Target
Book 50 percent of your position when the premium gains 30 percent. Trail the rest with a 15-point Volume Weighted Average Price stop on Nifty. Aim for at least a 1 to 2 risk-to-reward ratio after costs.
Skip if
India Volatility Index is above 22 (premiums are too inflated), OR it is the day before expiry (Theta decay accelerates and kills the buyer side), OR the time is after 2:30 PM and you do not have a confirmed trend already in place.

ORDER TYPES — EXECUTION MATTERS
LIMIT ORDER
USE THIS FOR OPTIONS
Fills only at your chosen price or better. Avoids the wide bid-ask spread on options, especially at the open. Always use a limit buy at the mid-price — you save ₹3 to ₹10 per trade compared to a market order.
MARKET ORDER
AVOID FOR OPTIONS
Fills at the best available price — meaning the ask price when buying. On slow-moving strikes you can lose ₹10 or more per lot to slippage. Only acceptable for breakout trades that need an instant fill.
BRACKET ORDER
AUTO STOP LOSS + TARGET
One entry order, one stop loss order, and one target order, all placed together. Closes automatically when either is hit. Good for disciplined execution. Note: this has been mostly disabled for option buying since 2020 (the Securities and Exchange Board of India peak margin framework) — check whether your broker still offers it.
COVER ORDER
ENTRY + STOP LOSS
An entry order plus a mandatory stop loss in one order. Provides higher intraday margin on equity, but it is generally not enabled for options after the Securities and Exchange Board of India margin framework changes.

COSTS & TAXATION — KNOW YOUR BREAK-EVEN
COST BREAKDOWN PER TRADE
Option buying, full round-trip (1 lot Nifty):
• Brokerage: ₹20 × 2 = ₹40
• Securities Transaction Tax (sell side only): 0.10 percent of premium value
• Exchange transaction charges: about ₹3.5
• Goods and Services Tax (18 percent on brokerage): about ₹7
• Securities and Exchange Board of India fee + Stamp duty: ₹1 to ₹2

Total comes to about ₹55 to ₹70, plus 0.10 percent Securities Transaction Tax. For a ₹150 At The Money premium × 65 lot size = ₹9,750 premium value, the Securities Transaction Tax alone comes to about ₹10.
BREAK-EVEN CALCULATION
Entry premium: ₹150
Costs: about ₹70 + Securities Transaction Tax
Lot size: 65 units
Break-even sell price for premium: about ₹151.2

That doesn't sound like much — but if you do 10 trades a month, ₹700 leaks out to costs alone. A 60 percent win rate at 1:1 risk-to-reward equals zero profit after costs. You NEED at least a 1:2 risk-to-reward just to be profitable after fees.
SECURITIES TRANSACTION TAX TRAP — THE IN-THE-MONEY EXPIRY LANDMINE
If you let an In The Money option expire instead of squaring it off, the Securities Transaction Tax jumps to 0.125 percent of the FULL intrinsic settlement value (not just the premium). This is the single biggest silent retail wipe-out cost.

Example: A Nifty 26,000 Call Option, with spot settling at 26,050, lot size 65:
• Sold before 3:30 PM → Securities Transaction Tax on premium is about ₹5 to ₹10.
• Let it expire In The Money → Securities Transaction Tax = 0.125 percent × 26,050 × 65 = about ₹2,117.

Rule: Always square off In The Money options before expiry close. The "do nothing" button is the most expensive button on your broker app.
TAXATION (FUTURES & OPTIONS, INTRADAY & POSITIONAL)
Income from Futures & Options is treated as Non-speculative Business Income under Section 43(5)(d) of the Income Tax Act (it is NOT capital gains, and NOT speculative income).
• Taxed at your applicable slab rate, added to your total income.
• Losses can offset any non-speculative business income, and can be carried forward for 8 years.
• A tax audit (under Section 44AB) is required if turnover exceeds ₹10 crore, or if you opt out of presumptive taxation with profit less than 6 percent of turnover.
• Turnover = the sum of absolute profit or loss on each trade (per the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India Guidance Note).
• Securities Transaction Tax, brokerage, exchange, and Securities and Exchange Board of India charges = all allowable as deductions.

Always consult a qualified Chartered Accountant. The treatment of Section 44AD presumptive taxation for Futures & Options is contested. This note is educational, not tax advice.
COMMON COST MISTAKES
Averaging losing options → doubles your Securities Transaction Tax drain.
Scalping for ₹5 → the costs eat the entire profit.
Chasing deep Out Of The Money options → low Delta combined with high costs equals a mathematically losing strategy.
Over-trading → 10 trades a day × ₹70 = a ₹700 daily toll on your account.
Take 2 to 3 trades a day at 1:2 risk-to-reward → costs become noise.
