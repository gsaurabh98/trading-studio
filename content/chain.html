OPTION CHAIN READING THE MARKET'S X-RAY

If you take ONE thing from this page: the option chain is the only free, real-time intelligence that retail traders have, and it tells you what the big players are betting on. Master it and you trade with them. Ignore it and you fight them blind.

Key terms used in this page: Open Interest (OI) = the live count of option contracts not yet squared off. Change in Open Interest (Chg OI) = how the Open Interest moved versus the previous close. Implied Volatility (IV) = the option market’s estimate of how volatile the index will be. Last Traded Price (LTP) = the most recent traded option premium. Put-Call Ratio (PCR) = total Put Open Interest divided by total Call Open Interest. Call Option (CE) = a contract that profits when price goes UP. Put Option (PE) = a contract that profits when price goes DOWN.

How to read each column (in plain English):
OI (Open Interest) = how many contracts are open at this strike right now. Bigger number = more money parked here = more important level.
Chg OI (Change in Open Interest) = how the number changed today. Positive = new traders are JOINING in. Negative = traders are EXITING.
IV% (Implied Volatility) = how scared the market feels. Higher = options are more expensive (people are paying more for protection).
LTP (Last Traded Price) = the most recent option premium.
Strike = the locked-in price (the middle column). At The Money (ATM) means the strike closest to today’s Nifty spot price.

THE 6 SIGNALS YOU MUST READ FROM THE OPTION CHAIN
1 MAX OPEN INTEREST STRIKE (Support / Resistance)
The simple idea: The strike with the most parked money becomes a magnet (or a wall) for price.

· Highest Put Option Open Interest = the strongest support level today. Why? Put writers have sold puts at this strike and will defend it (because if Nifty falls below, THEY lose money).
· Highest Call Option Open Interest = the strongest resistance level. Call writers will defend it from above.

Real example: Nifty is at 24,400 right now. The biggest Put Option Open Interest is at 24,000. The biggest Call Option Open Interest is at 24,800. So today’s expected trading range = 24,000 to 24,800. Trade inside this range until one side BREAKS with high volume.
2 MAX PAIN (The Expiry Day Magnet)
The simple idea: Max Pain is the strike at which option WRITERS lose the LEAST money on expiry day.

Writers are the big institutions with deep pockets. They have a strong incentive to push price towards this level so that retail buyers lose the most. It is NOT a conspiracy — it is the natural result of how writers hedge their positions.

Real use: On expiry day (Tuesday for Nifty, Thursday for Sensex), Nifty tends to gravitate towards the Max Pain level — UNLESS a major news event happens.

Free Max Pain data: sensibull.com, opstra.definedge.com, nseindia.com.
3 PUT-CALL RATIO (Overall Mood)
The simple idea: A single number that tells you whether the market mood today is bullish or bearish.

Formula: Put-Call Ratio = Total Put Option Open Interest divided by Total Call Option Open Interest.

How to read it:
· Above 1.3 = bullish (more put writers means writers expect support to hold).
· Below 0.7 = bearish (more call writers means writers expect resistance to hold).
· Between 0.8 and 1.2 = neutral / range-bound day.

Reverse rule (advanced): Extreme readings (above 1.8 or below 0.5) often signal a REVERSAL is coming. The crowd is too one-sided.
4 OPEN INTEREST CHANGE (Who is JOINING the trade?)
The simple idea: Total Open Interest tells you what is parked there. Open Interest CHANGE today tells you who is JOINING the party right now — and that matters more.

Read this with the option PREMIUM together. Two clues = one decision.

For a Call Option strike (resistance side):
· Premium UP + Open Interest UP → call BUYERS are aggressive. Bullish for Nifty.
· Premium DOWN + Open Interest UP → call WRITERS are aggressive, building a ceiling. Bearish at this strike.
· Premium UP + Open Interest DOWN → writers are running away (short covering). The rally is usually weak and fades.
· Premium DOWN + Open Interest DOWN → call buyers are giving up. Trend losing energy.

For a Put Option strike (support side):
· Premium UP + Open Interest UP → put BUYERS are aggressive. Bearish for Nifty.
· Premium DOWN + Open Interest UP → put WRITERS are aggressive, building a floor. Bullish at this strike.
· Premium UP + Open Interest DOWN → put writers running away (short covering). Decline usually fades.
· Premium DOWN + Open Interest DOWN → put buyers giving up.

Easy rule of thumb: Rising Call Option Open Interest ABOVE Nifty spot = a ceiling. Rising Put Option Open Interest BELOW Nifty spot = a floor.
5 IMPLIED VOLATILITY SKEW (Where is the FEAR?)
The simple idea: Compare the Implied Volatility of the At The Money Put Option with the Implied Volatility of the At The Money Call Option. Normally they are close. When they DIFFER — that is a clue.

Three readings:
· Put IV is much higher than Call IV → the market is buying CRASH protection. Often the sign of a coming BOTTOM.
· Call IV is higher than Put IV → traders are chasing upside in euphoria. Often the sign of a coming TOP.
· Both Implied Volatilities are very high → a big event is around the corner. Expect "Implied Volatility crush" (a sudden premium drop) AFTER the event.
6 LIQUIDITY (Can you EXIT easily?)
The simple idea: The bid-ask spread is the difference between the BUY price and SELL price of the option. A wide spread = no buyers when you want to exit = you LOSE money on every entry and exit.

Rule: Only trade strikes where the spread is LESS than 0.5 percent of the premium. For Nifty and Bank Nifty, only the At The Money strikes ± 3 strikes on either side are safe for intraday trading.

Warning: Far Out Of The Money strikes look CHEAP (only ₹5-10 premium) — but you can lose ₹5 to ₹15 per contract JUST in spread when you exit. They look cheap and feel expensive.

READING THE OI-CHANGE BAR CHART → CE / PE DECISION
The OI Change view (visible on Sensibull, Opstra, NSE’s OI dashboard) plots today’s OI change at every strike as side-by-side bars — green = PE OI change, red = CE OI change, with the dashed line at current spot. Read it visually and the CE / PE call comes out in 30 seconds.
THE 30-SECOND VERSION — THINK OF IT AS A TUG OF WAR
THE WHOLE CHART IN ONE SENTENCE
Two teams are pulling on Nifty's price — put writers (green team) pull it UP, call writers (red team) pull it DOWN. Each bar shows how much money they bet at that strike today. The tallest green bar is where the green team has dug in — the floor. The tallest red bar is where the red team has dug in — the ceiling. Price will mostly stay between the two walls.
3 RULES, NOTHING ELSE
1. Spot near the BIGGEST GREEN bar → bounce expected → BUY CE
2. Spot near the BIGGEST RED bar → rejection expected → BUY PE
3. Spot in the middle → no edge → WAIT

That's it. The walls move during the day, so re-check every 30 minutes. If the wall starts shrinking, that team is giving up — expect a strong move in the opposite direction.
DISTANCE FROM SPOT IS THE WHOLE GAME
The size of the green/red bar matters, but where spot sits between the two walls matters more. A spot that is 28 points from the ceiling and 372 points from the floor is a completely different trade from a spot that's halfway between. Always ask "how far is spot from each wall?" — the closer wall is the one that's about to act.
SETUP 1 · SPOT NEAR PE WALL
Spot is close to the biggest GREEN bar. Floor is right under your feet, ceiling is far away.

What it means: Put writers will fight to defend this strike. A bounce is the most likely next move.

Trade: BUY CE — small bounce scalp toward the next CE-OI strike above. SL just below the PE wall.
SETUP 2 · SPOT NEAR CE WALL
Spot is close to the biggest RED bar. Ceiling is right above your head, floor is far away.

What it means: Call writers will fight to defend this strike. A rejection down is the most likely next move.

Trade: BUY PE — small rejection scalp toward the next PE-OI strike below. SL just above the CE wall.
SETUP 3 · SPOT IN THE MIDDLE
Spot is roughly equidistant from both walls. Neither side has the edge.

What it means: Both teams are waiting. Whoever moves first wins. Range-day chop is most likely.

Trade: NO TRADE — theta will eat option buyers in a sideways tape. Wait for spot to drift to one side.
WALL MIGRATION = THE BREAKOUT SIGNAL
WHAT TO WATCH FOR
Refresh the OI Change view every 30 minutes. You're looking for one specific thing: a wall that was big at, say, 24,400 is now shrinking and a NEW big bar is appearing at 24,500.

That migration tells you the writers at 24,400 have given up and bought back their calls. The wall has fallen. The next wall (24,500) is the new ceiling.

When this happens, momentum is real and the move is fast — that's a 100–200 point squeeze in 30–60 minutes.
HOW TO TRADE A MIGRATING WALL
CE wall migrating UP (writers covering above) → aggressive BUY CE, target = the new wall

PE wall migrating DOWN (writers covering below) → aggressive BUY PE, target = the new wall

Both walls staying still all day → range day, fade extremes only, no breakout trade.

This is the highest-edge setup the option chain gives you. Most beginners miss it because they only check OI once at 9:30 AM and never refresh.

WHY WALLS BREAK — THE SHORT-SQUEEZE CHAIN REACTION
The illusion of the wall: 70L Call OI at a strike does NOT mean Nifty cannot go above it. It only means a lot of money is betting against it. When the bet starts losing, writers don't sit there and bleed — they cover, and their forced buying becomes the fuel for the move. The wall doesn't slow the move down — it ACCELERATES it once it breaks.
Step What happens Effect
1 News, FII flow, or institutional buy comes in Spot pushes from 24,372 toward 24,400
2 24,400 CE buyers keep aggressively buying Premium rises (e.g. ₹50 → ₹70)
3 Writers who sold at ₹50 are now down ₹20 × lot. Margin requirement also rises. Pain mounting on the writer side
4 A few panicky writers buy back their short calls to cap the loss Their buy order = upward pressure on the option AND on Nifty
5 Premium spikes (₹70 → ₹110), spot pushes through 24,400 Cascade begins
6 All remaining writers rush to cover at once Squeeze — Nifty rips through 24,400 with no resistance, often 100–200 pts in 30 min
Key insight: It's not the BUYERS that break the wall. It's the WRITERS giving up that breaks the wall. Their forced covering becomes the fuel for the rally. That's why the move accelerates after the level breaks — not stalls.
READING SOLID / HATCHED / HOLLOW BARS — THE EARLIEST WARNING
Sensibull's "Show OI change" toggle (Opstra calls it the same) breaks every bar into three visual states. Once you can read these, you'll catch the squeeze 10–20 minutes before the price actually breaks the level.
SOLID BAR  
What it shows: Existing OI from before today (yesterday's carry-over).

What it means: The historical wall — old positions still sitting open. Stable, not a fresh signal.
HATCHED BAR  
What it shows: Today's INCREASE in OI at this strike.

What it means: Writers are ADDING to the wall — defence is being reinforced. Wall is GROWING → rejection more likely.
HOLLOW BAR  
What it shows: Today's DECREASE in OI at this strike.

What it means: Writers are COVERING — closing their shorts. Wall is SHRINKING → squeeze in progress, BUY in the breakout direction.
The earliest signal you'll ever get: a HOLLOW red bar at 24,400 + a HATCHED red bar appearing at 24,500 or 24,600 = wall has migrated UP = squeeze underway. This appears 10–20 minutes before price actually breaks 24,400. The retail crowd is still buying PE while you're already long CE.
THE QUICK WALL STRENGTH CHECK
OI tells you who has bet what. It does not tell you who will fold first. Run these 5 checks in 30 seconds before any wall trade. Each gives +1 / 0 / −1. The total tells you the probability the wall holds.
1 PREMIUM DIRECTION (the inverse OI check)
Look at the LIVE LTP of the wall strike's option (e.g. 24,400 CE LTP) for the last 5–10 minutes.

+1 — Premium falling while OI rising → writers piling on, wall holding
 0 — Both flat → neutral
−1 — Premium rising fast while OI falling → writers covering, wall failing

This is the single most important check — it gives you 60% of the answer in 5 seconds.
2 CANDLE REACTION AT THE WALL
Look at the 5-min candle that touched the wall.

+1 — Pin bar / shooting star / bearish engulfing at a CE wall (rejection)
 0 — Doji / small body (indecision)
−1 — Strong-bodied candle closing through the wall (real break)

Charts show commitment, OI shows count. A wall that holds always prints a visible rejection candle.
3 VOLUME ON THE TEST BAR
Compare the test-candle's volume to the average volume of the last 20 candles.

+1 — Above-average volume + rejection candle → real defence
 0 — Average volume
−1 — Above-average volume + break candle → institutional flow

Walls only break on real money. A break on weak volume is almost always a fakeout that gets reversed.
4 VWAP RELATIONSHIP
Where is spot relative to today's VWAP when it tests the wall?

Testing a CE wall (ceiling):
+1 — Spot below VWAP → bias-aligned, wall holds
 0 — Spot at VWAP
−1 — Spot above VWAP → momentum favours buyers, wall vulnerable

Inverse for a PE wall (floor): spot above VWAP = floor holds, below = floor vulnerable.
5 TIME OF DAY
Is the test happening in a clean window or a noisy window?

+1 — 9:45–12:00 or 1:30–2:30 → clean signal window
 0 — 9:15–9:45 or 12:00–1:30 → noisy
−1 — 2:30–3:30 on expiry day → OI numbers are unreliable

First 30 min and last 30 min have garbage OI. Lunch is choppy. The "real" market is mid-morning and mid-afternoon.
SCORE → PROBABILITY

+1 / 0 50–55% Coin flip. NO TRADE.

APPLIED — THE 24,400 WALL THAT BROKE
Earlier we said "BUY PE because spot is at the 24,400 CE wall." Then Nifty broke 24,400. Why? Because we only checked OI — not the other four. Here's what running the full check would have looked like.
Check What you would have seen at the test Score
1. Premium direction 24,400 CE LTP climbing rapidly while OI starting to fall → writers covering −1
2. Candle reaction Strong green candle closing through 24,400, no rejection wick −1
3. Volume Above-average volume on the break candle −1
4. VWAP Spot was above VWAP, momentum aligned with the breakout −1
5. Time of day ~10:30–11:30 AM — clean window +1
TOTAL SCORE −3 (wall fails 70–85%)
Correct trade: A score of −3 means "flip — trade the BREAK". The right play was BUY 24,400 CE (or 24,500 CE) on the breakout candle, target 24,500/24,600. SL = a 5-min close back below 24,400.

The lesson: never fade a wall on OI alone. The OI tells you the wall exists. The other 4 checks tell you whether to trust it.
The whole framework in one line: OI shows you the wall. Premium tells you if it's real. Candle, volume, and VWAP confirm the breakout. Time decides if any of it is reliable. Use all 5 — never trade off OI alone.

A PE WALL BELOW · NO CE WALL ABOVE → BUY CE
Chart: 1–2 huge green bars below spot, only small/scattered red bars above spot.
Meaning: Put writers are confident; call writers are not committing — upside is open.
Trade: BUY ATM/ITM CE on any dip toward the green-wall strike. Stop = below the PE-wall strike. Target = next CE-OI cluster above.

Chart: One huge green bar below spot AND one huge red bar above spot, spot mid-way.
Meaning: Both sides agree on a range — this is the most common pattern, especially mid-week.
Trade for buyers: only fade extremes — CE bounce off PE wall, PE rejection at CE wall. Skip if spot is mid-range.
Trade for sellers: short straddle/strangle around the walls if you have the capital + skill.

Chart: Yesterday’s big PE/CE wall is now a shrinking bar (writers covering) and OI is shifting to a new strike beyond it.
Meaning: The defended level has failed — expect a strong move toward the new wall.
Trade: If CE wall breaks (red bar shrinking, OI moving up) → BUY CE aggressively. If PE wall breaks → BUY PE aggressively.
Highest-edge setup — especially on expiry day morning.

DOs & DON'Ts WHEN READING THE OI-CHANGE CHART
DO
• Refresh OI Change every 30 minutes — walls migrate intraday
• Use weekly expiry only (most active OI flow)
• Trade only when one wall is 2× bigger than its opposite-side counterpart
• Combine with price action (engulfing/pin bar at the wall) before entry
• Read the chart after 9:30 AM — first 15 min OI is noisy
DON'T
• Don't read OI Change without checking VIX & PCR — high VIX or extreme PCR overrides the walls
• Don't blindly buy when spot is between two walls (range = no trade)
• Don't trust the chart on event days (RBI / Budget / results) — OI is positioning, not direction
• Don't hold trades through 3:00 PM on expiry — OI walls collapse in last hour
• Don't confuse OI Change (today) with Total OI (cumulative) — only the change matters for fresh signals
RED FLAGS IN THE OPTION CHAIN
SUDDEN MASSIVE OI SHIFT PRE-NEWS
Aggressive OI build at a single far-OTM strike 1–2 days before an announcement = someone knows something. Either ride with them (small size) or stay out entirely. Never fade.
PCR EXTREMES
PCR >2.0 or <0.4 signals euphoria / panic. Reversal risk is high. Don't initiate fresh positions in the prevailing direction until PCR normalizes.
IV SURGE WITHOUT PRICE MOVE
If IV jumps >20% in a day but price hasn't moved, a large directional bet is being built. Options are now overpriced — do not buy. Wait for IV to settle.
ZERO-OI STRIKES
Strikes with <5,000 OI are liquidity traps. Any order will move the premium 10–15%. Only trade strikes with >50,000 OI for Nifty and >20,000 for BankNifty.

DAILY OPTION CHAIN ROUTINE (5 MINUTES)

  • 9:00 AM IST: Note the maximum Call Option Open Interest strike (today’s resistance) and the maximum Put Option Open Interest strike (today’s support) → this is today’s expected trading range.
  • 9:30 AM IST: Check the Put-Call Ratio direction compared to yesterday’s close. This confirms a bullish, bearish, or neutral bias.
  • 11:00 AM IST: Re-check the Change in Open Interest. Are fresh long positions being built on the Call Option side? Are fresh short positions being built on the Put Option side? → This is your direction confirmation.
  • 2:00 PM IST: A final chain check before the close. Where is the Max Pain moving? Are there any sudden Open Interest shifts?
  • End of day: Log today’s Maximum Open Interest strikes, the Put-Call Ratio, and whether price respected those levels. This builds your pattern library over time.
When to take this trade — The Option-Chain Wall Bounce (the most reliable option-chain setup)
Setup
It is between 10:00 AM and 1:00 PM IST. You have just refreshed the option chain. The Put Option side shows a very large concentration of Open Interest at one specific strike (for example, 24,800), and that Open Interest is GROWING (more Put writers are piling in to defend this level). This is the "Put Wall" — it acts as support. The current spot price is 50 to 100 points ABOVE this strike, and it is pulling back DOWN towards it. The India Volatility Index is between 12 and 18. The Put-Call Ratio is above 1.0 (a bullish lean overall).
Entry
Wait for price to reach the Put Wall strike on the spot index, AND for a bullish reversal candle (a hammer, bullish engulfing, or a long lower-wick rejection) to print on the 5-minute or 15-minute chart, AND for the volume to be greater than the recent average. BUY one lot of an At The Money Call Option of the current-week expiry on the next candle’s open.
Stop Loss
10 to 15 points below the Put Wall strike on the spot index (the wall is broken if price closes there). On the option premium itself, set a hard stop at 30 percent loss. Place a Stop-Loss-Market order on the broker.
Target
Target 1 = the next major Open Interest level on the upside (the maximum Call Option Open Interest strike) — book 50 percent here. Target 2 = the Max Pain level if it is above (book 30 percent). Trail the rest with a 5-minute Volume Weighted Average Price stop. Exit by 2:30 PM IST.
Skip if
The Put Wall Open Interest is SHRINKING (Put writers are running away — the wall is about to break, do NOT buy a Call Option here), OR the current spot price is already BELOW the Put Wall (the wall has been broken — it now becomes resistance, not support), OR the Put-Call Ratio is below 0.8 (overall bearish lean — the Call Option has poor odds), OR the India Volatility Index is above 22 (an inflated premium will be hurt by an Implied Volatility crush after the bounce).
