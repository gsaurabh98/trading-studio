CHART PATTERNS 30 PATTERNS + FORMULA TABLE

What is a chart pattern? (And how is it different from a candlestick pattern?)

Both are pictures on the chart that hint at the next move — but they are very different in size and time:

· A candlestick pattern = 1 to 5 candles. It forms in MINUTES or hours. It tells you about IMMEDIATE buyer / seller behaviour.
· A chart pattern = 20 to 100+ candles. It forms over HOURS, DAYS, or even WEEKS. It tells you about a BIGGER shift in who is buying and selling.

Why chart patterns are useful for option buyers: A chart pattern gives you a "measured move target" — meaning, you can MEASURE the size of the pattern and project EXACTLY where price is likely to go. This is a HUGE advantage — you know your profit target BEFORE you even enter the trade.
How to read each pattern card below:
· The DRAWING shows the shape of the pattern.
· The DASHED line is usually the "neckline" (the level that price must break for the pattern to be confirmed).
· After the break is confirmed, you measure the height of the pattern and project it from the break point — that gives you the TARGET.
· Each card has an ACTION (BUY a Call Option / BUY a Put Option) and the exact trigger.

Quick term reminders: "CE" = Call Option. "PE" = Put Option. These are the Indian symbols you see on every option chain.

HOW TO TRADE ANY CHART PATTERN — 5 SIMPLE STEPS

  • STEP 1 · SPOT THE PATTERN — The pattern must look CLEAN and obvious. If you have to squint or force-fit the shape, it is NOT really there. Use the DAILY or 60-MINUTE charts — chart patterns do NOT work well on the 5-minute chart (too much random noise).
  • STEP 2 · DRAW THE BREAKOUT LINE — This is the line that price must CROSS for the pattern to confirm. It is called the "neckline" in Head and Shoulders / double tops, or the upper trend line in triangles. Draw this line CLEARLY before the break happens, not after.
  • STEP 3 · WAIT FOR A FULL CANDLE CLOSE BEYOND THE LINE — An intra-candle wick that pokes through is NOT a real breakout. Wait for a full candle to CLOSE on the other side of the line, on HIGHER than usual volume. No volume = it is probably a fake-out.
  • STEP 4 · MEASURE THE TARGET — Take the height of the pattern (from the highest top to the lowest bottom). Then project that same distance from the breakout point in the direction of the break. That is your Target 1.
  • STEP 5 · PLACE STOP LOSS JUST INSIDE THE PATTERN — Place your stop loss just INSIDE the pattern boundary (below the neckline for upside breakouts, above the neckline for downside breakouts). If price comes BACK inside the pattern, the breakout has failed — EXIT immediately, do not wait.

Target: pattern height projected DOWN from neckline.
→ BUY PE on neckline break
DOUBLE BOTTOM (W)
BULLISH REVERSAL
Two equal lows with a bounce in between. Second low fails to break = bears exhausted. Breakout above bounce high confirms.
Target: pattern height projected UP from neckline.
→ BUY CE on neckline break

STRONG REVERSAL
Three failed attempts at same resistance. Very strong rejection signal. Break of support = high-conviction short.
Target: height × 1 projected down.
→ BUY PE
TRIPLE BOTTOM
STRONG REVERSAL
Three successful defenses of same support. Very strong buyer interest. Break of resistance = high-conviction long.
Target: height × 1 projected up.

ROUNDING BOTTOM (SAUCER)
SLOW REVERSAL
Gradual U-shaped transition from down to up over many sessions. Volume forms matching U shape. Patient but reliable.
Target: bowl depth projected up from breakout.

ROUNDING TOP
SLOW REVERSAL
Inverted U — gradual topping. Volume declines near peak, rises on breakdown. Often precedes extended decline.
Target: dome height projected down from breakdown.

CUP & HANDLE
CONTINUATION REVERSAL
Saucer-shape + short consolidation (handle). Classic O'Neil setup. Handle should be <1/3 of cup depth.
Target: cup depth projected up from handle breakout.

BEARISH CONTINUATION
Inverted cup + small upward handle. Sellers re-enter at top of handle.
Target: cup height projected down from handle breakdown.
→ BUY PE on handle break
RISING WEDGE
BEARISH REVERSAL
Converging up-sloping lines. Despite higher highs, momentum fades. Breakdown of lower trendline = reversal.
Target: wedge widest-part height projected down.
→ BUY PE
FALLING WEDGE
BULLISH REVERSAL
Converging down-sloping lines. Despite lower lows, downside momentum fades. Breakout of upper trendline = reversal.
Target: wedge widest-part height projected up.
→ BUY CE
DIAMOND TOP
RARE REVERSAL
Broadening then contracting range at tops. Rare but reliable. Combines megaphone + triangle.
Target: widest part projected down.
→ BUY PE on break
BROADENING TOP (MEGAPHONE)
CHAOS REVERSAL
Diverging highs and lows. Each swing larger than the last. Signals climactic top. Very volatile — wide SL required.
Target: last low × swing height.
→ BUY PE
DIAMOND BOTTOM
RARE REVERSAL
Mirror of Diamond Top at a downtrend bottom. Broadening then contracting range ends with a sharp upside break. Rare but highly reliable (~69% hit rate).
Target: widest part projected UP from breakout.
→ BUY CE on upside break
BROADENING BOTTOM
CHAOS REVERSAL
Diverging highs and lows at a downtrend bottom. Violent two-sided swings mark capitulation + aggressive accumulation. Break above last swing high = reversal confirmed.
Target: swing height projected UP from break.
→ BUY CE on upper-line break

POPULAR BONUS PATTERNS
Two extended patterns widely used by Indian equity swing traders: High-Tight Flag (O'Neil's most explosive bullish continuation — the pattern behind many multibaggers) and Dead Cat Bounce (the classic trap that fools buyers after a large decline).
HIGH-TIGHT FLAG (O'NEIL)
MOST EXPLOSIVE BULLISH CONTINUATION
What: Stock rallies ≥ 90–100% in 4–8 weeks (the "pole"), then consolidates tightly — correction < 25% — for 3–5 weeks (the "flag"). Break above flag high = resumption.

Why it's rare: Very few stocks rally 90%+ in 2 months. When they do, institutional accumulation is happening — operators are defending every dip.

Historical stats (O'Neil, Minervini): Pattern precedes many multibagger runs; in India — spotted on small/mid-cap breakout stocks. ~75% follow-through.

Target: Pole length + projected from flag breakout. Many runs exceed 2–3× pole length.
→ BUY CE on flag-high break with volume
DEAD CAT BOUNCE
BEAR TRAP — FADE THE RALLY
What: After a sudden sharp decline (> 15–20% in days), price bounces 30–50% of the drop. Bargain hunters pile in thinking "bottom is in" — only for price to roll over and make a new lower low.

Why it works: Shorts cover + retail buys the dip = short-term rally. But institutions are still distributing. Bounce dies at the 38–50% retrace of the original drop.

Warning signs:
· Bounce on declining volume
· Rally stops at prior support (now resistance)
· RSI stalls below 50

Target: New low below the initial drop's bottom. Typical: prior-drop low − 0.5× drop height.
→ FADE the bounce, BUY PE
PRICE-STRUCTURE NUANCES (ADVANCED)
These are pattern behaviours that most books skip — but Bulkowski, Kirkpatrick, and Fidelity research show they materially change the trade plan. Each one answers: "what if the pattern looks right but price is behaving oddly?"
RECTANGLE SHORTFALL — HIDDEN TREND INSIDE THE RANGE
BREAKDOWN BIAS
What it is: Inside a rectangle, price fails to reach the opposite boundary on each swing — each high is LOWER than the last (or each low is HIGHER). The range LOOKS intact but a trend is forming inside it.

Reading:
· Lower highs inside the box → sellers gaining, breakdown likely
· Higher lows inside the box → buyers gaining, breakout likely
· Shortfall pattern precedes the eventual break ~70% of the time (Bulkowski)

Action: Bias your entry in the direction of the shortfall. On an apparent bullish-sweep to the upper edge with lower highs, fade it — target lower boundary & the breakdown.
→ Fade the weaker side, trade the eventual break
EXPLOSION GAP PIVOT — RUNAWAY GAP AS SUPPORT
CONTINUATION
What it is: A breakaway or runaway gap that occurs mid-trend. The gap-low becomes a pivot level: as long as price stays above it, the trend is confirmed and accelerating.

Why it matters:
· Runaway gaps rarely get filled — holding them = institutional defence
· A held gap gives you the best trend-stop in any playbook: beneath the gap low
· Kirkpatrick: runaway gaps mark the 50% point of the total move on average — doubles the remaining distance from the gap

Action: BUY CE on any pullback that tests (but doesn't close below) the gap-low. SL just under the gap. Target = 2× distance from breakout-point to gap.
→ BUY CE on retest of gap-low

HOW TO USE THIS TABLE

  • 1 · Identify the pattern — only proceed when structure is clean (daily or 60-min charts preferred).
  • 2 · Mark the breakout/break-down level precisely — this is the anchor for all measurements.
  • 3 · Measure the height as described in column 3 — use the widest vertical distance of the pattern.
  • 4 · Apply the formula — project the height from the break point in the break direction.
  • 5 · Set T1 = measured target, T2 = 1.5× measured target. Book 50% at T1 and trail the rest to T2.
  • 6 · Follow-through stats are from Bulkowski's Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns (3rd ed.) — averages across US & global equities. Indian indices broadly match these numbers.
Critical caveat: A measured target is the MINIMUM follow-through that statistically happens. It is NOT the maximum — don't exit the full position at T1. Equally, a target being "on the chart" does NOT guarantee it will be hit — expected hit rates above range 50–85%. Always use a stop. Target = planning tool, not prediction.

BEGINNER PRIORITY — MASTER THESE CHART PATTERNS FIRST

  • Double Top (M) & Double Bottom (W) — the most frequent and most reliable reversal pattern on Indian index charts. Easy to spot, clean entry (neckline break), clear target (pattern height).
  • Rectangle / Range — most of the trading day is sideways. Knowing the range boundaries = knowing where to fade, where to buy, and where the breakout will come. The single most useful day-trading pattern.
  • Bull Flag / Bear Flag — the highest-R:R continuation pattern. Sharp trend → tight pullback → trend resumes. Perfect for adding to winners.
  • Ascending / Descending Triangle — directional triangles are more reliable than symmetrical ones. Ascending = bullish bias, Descending = bearish bias.
  • Head & Shoulders — the textbook major reversal. Takes longer to form but when confirmed, the follow-through is large. Best on daily charts for swing traders.
Common beginner mistake with chart patterns: jumping in before the breakout happens. "It looks like it's about to break!" is not a signal. Wait for the candle to close beyond the level with volume — trading pre-breakout hopes is the single biggest loser in chart-pattern trading. Patience pays — you may catch 10–15% less of the move, but your win rate doubles.
Combining chart patterns with candlesticks = A+ trades: A Double Bottom (chart pattern) that completes with a Bullish Engulfing (candlestick) on the neckline break, with volume expansion — this is the gold-standard setup. You will only see 2–4 of these per month on any given index, but they almost always pay out. Wait for them.
5 TYPES OF GAPS — NOT ALL GAPS ARE EQUAL
Gaps look identical on the chart but mean completely different things depending on WHERE they occur. Knowing the type tells you whether to fade it, follow it, or ignore it.
1 COMMON GAP
LOW SIGNAL
Where: Inside a sideways range, no trend context.
Fills: ~80%+ within 1–2 days.
Action: Usually a non-event. Fade with caution or wait for the fill and re-plan.
2 BREAKAWAY GAP
TRADE IT
Where: OUT of consolidation, breaking a well-defined S/R.
Fills: Rarely — the old S/R flips and holds.
Action: Strong directional signal. Enter on retest of the broken level + volume confirmation. Gap itself becomes new support.
3 RUNAWAY / MEASURING GAP
MIDDLE OF TREND
Where: Mid-trend, usually halfway to the final top/bottom.
Fills: Rarely during the trend.
Measuring rule: Project the distance from trend start → gap onto gap → target. Often gives a precise 2× move target.
4 EXHAUSTION GAP
REVERSAL WARNING
Where: After a long trend — last thrust, often on euphoria/panic news.
Fills: Usually YES, within days.
Action: Do NOT chase. Look for reversal candle the next 1–3 sessions. High-volume exhaustion gap + bearish engulfing = top signal.
5 ISLAND REVERSAL
RARE · POWERFUL
Where: Exhaustion gap UP + cluster of 1–5 candles + exhaustion gap DOWN (or mirror for bottoms).
Fills: Often yes, eventually — but the reversal is almost always sharp.
Action: One of the highest-conviction top/bottom signals in TA. Position aggressively with tight SL just above/below the island high/low.

GAP TRADING RULES (INDIA-SPECIFIC)

  • First 15-min after gap usually retests the gap edge — wait for it before entering.
  • Gap < 0.3% of index → treat as noise, often fills the same session.
  • Gap > 1% on news → breakaway candidate, don't fade blindly.
  • Monday gaps (after weekend) often fade by lunch as traders digest weekend news.
  • Check global cues (SGX Nifty, Dow futures) before classifying any gap.
  • VIX > 20 with gap → volatile fill likely, widen your SL.
GAP UP / GAP DOWN OPENING STRATEGY
GAP UP OPENING
STRATEGY
Small gap (less than 0.5 percent): Wait for the Opening Range Breakout, then trade in the breakout direction.

Large gap (greater than 0.5 percent): Wait for the gap-fill attempt. If price holds the Previous Day Close as support → BUY a Call Option. If price fills the gap and breaks the Previous Day Close → BUY a Put Option.
GAP DOWN OPENING
STRATEGY
Small gap (less than 0.5 percent): Likely to fill. Wait for Opening Range Breakout confirmation.

Large gap (greater than 0.5 percent): If the Previous Day Close acts as resistance on the bounce → BUY a Put Option. If the gap fills and price sustains → a potential Call Option setup.
When to take this trade — The Head and Shoulders Breakdown (the most reliable bearish chart pattern)
Setup
On the daily or 60-minute chart, Nifty has been in an uptrend, and now it has formed a clear Head and Shoulders pattern: a left shoulder (a peak), then a higher peak (the head), then a right shoulder (a peak that is roughly the same height as the left shoulder). The neckline (the horizontal line connecting the two valleys between the three peaks) is clearly drawn. Volume is shrinking from the left shoulder to the head to the right shoulder (a sign of weakening buying). The Relative Strength Index shows a bearish divergence (a lower high while price made the head’s higher high).
Entry
Wait for a daily / 60-minute candle to CLOSE below the neckline, with volume greater than 1.5 times the recent average. BUY one lot of an At The Money Put Option of the current-month expiry on the next-day open. If you missed the breakdown candle, wait for a pullback to the broken neckline (now resistance) and BUY at the rejection.
Stop Loss
Just above the right shoulder’s peak (5 to 10 points buffer above the wick). On the option premium itself, set a hard stop at 30 to 35 percent loss. Place a Stop-Loss-Market order on the broker.
Target
Measure the pattern height (from the top of the head down to the neckline). Project this distance DOWN from the neckline-break point — that is your Target 1 (book 50 percent here). Target 2 = 1.5 times the pattern height (book 30 percent). Trail the rest with the previous swing high. Hold for 5 to 20 trading days.
Skip if
The right shoulder is significantly higher than the left shoulder (the pattern is distorted, low edge), OR there is no volume confirmation on the neckline break (likely a fake-out), OR the pattern formed in less than 4 weeks (it is too small to be a structural top), OR the higher-timeframe weekly chart is still bullish (the daily pattern may be just a deep correction, not a true top).
