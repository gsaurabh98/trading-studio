FAQ COMMON QUESTIONS

Q1 · Why did my Call Option lose money even though Nifty went UP?
Three possible reasons:
• Theta decay — the spot price move wasn't enough to offset the time decay, especially after 1 PM on expiry day.
• Implied Volatility crush — the India Volatility Index fell after an anticipated event. Your option lost Vega value faster than it gained Delta value.
• Out Of The Money entry — if your Call Option was far Out Of The Money, Delta was just 0.1 to 0.2. A 30-point Nifty move only moved your premium ₹3 to ₹6, while Theta ate ₹10.

Fix: Buy At The Money or slightly In The Money strikes. Exit by 1:30 PM on expiry. Avoid buying when the India Volatility Index is near a multi-day high.
Q2 · Why does price gravitate towards the Max Pain level on expiry?
Market makers (the institutional option sellers) have short Gamma near the most-written strikes. As expiry approaches, they dynamically hedge by buying or selling Nifty futures — and the net effect of all that hedging pushes the spot price towards the strike where their loss is minimised (this is called the Max Pain level).

It is NOT a conspiracy — it is the mechanical result of Delta hedging. The strongest effect is seen after 1 PM on expiry day.
Q3 · Why does Theta kill me on Tuesday afternoon?
The Nifty weekly expiry is Tuesday at 3:30 PM. The Theta decay on At The Money options is non-linear — roughly:
• Monday: ₹15 to ₹20 per hour.
• Tuesday morning: ₹25 to ₹35 per hour.
• Tuesday 12 PM to 3 PM: ₹40 to ₹60 per hour.
• Tuesday 3 PM to 3:30 PM: a near-vertical collapse.

Rule: Do NOT buy At The Money Nifty options after 1 PM on Tuesday. Either exit, or switch to short strangles or iron condors if you are experienced.
Q4 · Should I paper trade before real money?
Yes — at least 50 trades. Paper trading doesn't teach emotional control (that only comes with real skin in the game), but it teaches:
• Execution mechanics (order types, exits)
• Pattern recognition
• Setup filtering
• Journal discipline

After 50 paper trades, start with 1 lot Nifty ATM only. Don't skip this phase to "save time" — you'll pay for that time in losses instead.
Q5 · What capital do I need to start trading options?
After the Securities and Exchange Board of India 2024 to 2026 lot revision (Nifty lot is 65, Bank Nifty lot is 30, Sensex lot is 20):
• Minimum realistic: ₹1,00,000 — 1 lot of Nifty At The Money, 1 to 2 trades a day.
• Comfortable: ₹3,00,000 — 1 to 2 lots, with scaled position sizing.
• Ideal: ₹5,00,000 and above — full diversification across Nifty and Sensex.

Below ₹1 lakh, even one losing trade can breach the 2 percent risk rule. Paper trade until you have ₹1 lakh of dedicated risk capital.
Q6 · Is trading on expiry day (Zero Days To Expiry) worth it?
Trading at Zero Days To Expiry is gambling disguised as trading for most retail traders. Premiums are tiny, Theta is brutal, and one small wrong move equals a 100 percent loss.

Only consider it if:
• You trade in the first 30 minutes (9:15 AM to 9:45 AM IST), when Gamma still works for you.
• You set a strict stop loss of 30 percent of the premium or less.
• Maximum 2 attempts, then stop trading.

For beginners: skip Zero Days To Expiry trading entirely.
Q7 · At The Money, In The Money, or Out Of The Money — which should I buy?
At The Money: The best balance of Delta + Theta. Default choice for 90 percent of intraday option buying.
In The Money: Higher Delta (around 0.6 to 0.8), less Theta, but 2 to 3 times more capital required per lot. Use for high-conviction trades with a wider stop loss.
Out Of The Money: Cheapest, but you need a BIG move to profit. A low-probability trade. Avoid these except in specific breakout setups.
Q8 · When should I book profit / exit a winning trade?
Use a scale-out approach:
• Target 1 (1 to 1 risk-to-reward): Book 50 percent. Move stop loss to your entry (this makes the rest of the trade risk-free).
• Target 2 (1 to 2 risk-to-reward): Book another 30 percent. Trail the stop loss below the most recent swing low.
• Target 3 (the runner): Let the remaining 20 percent ride with a trailing stop loss.

This gives you a lower average drawdown plus the psychological relief of banking some profit, while still capturing the big winners.
Q9 · Why did Bank Nifty weekly options get discontinued?
In November 2024, the Securities and Exchange Board of India reduced each exchange to ONE weekly option, to curb the retail option-buyer wipeout (regulators estimated that 91 percent of Futures and Options traders were net losers). The consequence:
• The National Stock Exchange kept the Nifty weekly (moved to Tuesday in September 2025).
• The National Stock Exchange dropped Bank Nifty, Fin Nifty, and MidCap Nifty weeklies — monthly contracts only.
• The Bombay Stock Exchange kept the Sensex weekly (moved to Thursday in September 2025).

For retail traders, this means Bank Nifty is now far less useful for weekly Theta plays.
Q10 · Scalping vs positional vs swing — which suits beginners?
Swing / positional (2 to 10 day holds): BEST for beginners. Lower emotional load, tolerates imperfect entries, gives you longer decision windows.
Intraday (hold for 1 to 4 hours): The standard option-buying timeframe. Second-best for beginners, once their basics are solid.
Scalping (hold less than 10 minutes): Requires microsecond discipline, low transaction costs, and full-time attention. Retail option buyers almost always lose at scalping because of the bid-ask spread and brokerage costs.
Q11 · Technical analysis or fundamentals — what matters for intraday options?
For intraday index options: Technical analysis is 95 percent of your edge. Fundamentals move on quarterly or yearly cycles — they are irrelevant for a 2-hour option trade.

What matters for intraday trading:
• Chart patterns + Support and Resistance + volume + Volume Weighted Average Price + Open Interest.
• The India Volatility Index.
• Gift Nifty + global market cues (for the market open).
• Upcoming events (Reserve Bank of India, Federal Reserve, Union Budget, quarterly results).

Fundamentals only matter for delivery equity holdings.
Q12 · How do I know I am ready to increase my lot size?
Before scaling up, require ALL of the following:
6 months of consistent profitable trading.
50 or more trades with logged data, average risk-to-reward of 1 to 1.5 or better.
Win rate on your main setup of 45 percent or more.
Maximum drawdown over the 6 months less than 15 percent of capital.
You did NOT break the risk rules even once in the last 30 days.

Scale by 1.5 times, never by 2 times. Scaling too fast means the first losing streak at the new size hurts disproportionately.
