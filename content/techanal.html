TECHNICAL ANALYSIS OPTIONS-TRADER EDITION

What is technical analysis (in simple words)? Technical analysis is just the study of CHARTS. You look at the price and the volume of past trades, and you use them to GUESS where price will go next. That is it.

It rests on 3 basic beliefs:
· Price knows everything — whatever the news, whatever the rumour, it is already reflected in today’s price.
· Prices move in trends — once Nifty starts going up, it tends to keep going up for some time.
· History repeats — because humans always feel the same emotions (greed, fear, hope), the chart patterns also repeat.

Important reminder: You are NOT predicting the future. You are simply estimating the CHANCE of the next move — and then deciding how much money to put on the trade.

Why technical analysis matters MORE for option buyers than for stock investors: If you buy a stock and the price moves UP a week later, you still make money. But if you buy an option and the price moves UP a week later, you might LOSE money — because time decay (Theta) ate the premium while you were waiting. So getting the TIMING right matters more than getting the direction right. Technical analysis helps you with that timing.
THE 4 PILLARS OF TECHNICAL ANALYSIS (KEEP THESE 4 IN MIND)
1 · PRICE ACTION
PRIMARY
What it is: The actual movement of price on the chart — candles, trend lines, swing highs and swing lows, and chart patterns.

What it answers: "Where is price going? And how FAST?" This is the single most important question for option buyers.
2 · VOLUME
CONFIRMS THE MOVE
What it is: The number of shares (or contracts) traded in a given period.

What it tells you: Is the move REAL or fake?
· Price UP + Volume UP = real trend.
· Price UP + Volume DOWN = suspect, often a fake-out.
Volume confirms or rejects every price signal.
3 · MOMENTUM (Speed of move)
SPEEDOMETER
What it is: Indicators that measure how FAST price is moving — like the Relative Strength Index (RSI), Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD), and Stochastics.

What it tells you: Is the trend still alive, getting tired, or about to flip? When momentum slows down BEFORE price does, that is the EARLIEST warning of a reversal. (This is called "divergence".)
4 · MARKET STRUCTURE
THE BIG PICTURE
What it is: The skeleton of the chart — support and resistance levels, the Central Pivot Range, and trend lines.

What it tells you: Is the market in an UPTREND (Higher Highs and Higher Lows) or DOWNTREND? A trade setup is only valid INSIDE the right structure. A bullish pattern in a clear downtrend usually fails.
TOP-DOWN TECHNICAL ANALYSIS WORKFLOW FOR OPTION BUYERS
Never trade a stock in isolation. The index sets the weather, the sector sets the wind, and the stock is the ship. If the index is trending up, fighting it with a Put Option is a low-probability trade no matter how good the stock chart looks.
STEP 1 · INDEX BIAS
Check Nifty and Bank Nifty on the daily and 1-hour charts. Are they above or below the 20-period Exponential Moving Average? Above the Central Pivot Range or below it? This is your directional filter. Trade WITH it, not against it.
STEP 2 · SECTOR ROTATION
Open the sectoral indices (Nifty IT, Nifty Bank, Nifty Auto, Nifty Fast-Moving Consumer Goods, Nifty Pharma). Which sectors are leading? Which are lagging? Trade the leaders long, the laggards short — never the opposite.
STEP 3 · STOCK SELECTION
Pick a liquid Futures & Options stock from inside the leading sector. Check its daily trend, the last 5-day high and low, and today's opening gap. Only trade setups aligned with the stock's own daily trend.
STEP 4 · SETUP CONFIRMATION
Look for at least 3 confluences: candle pattern + a key level + volume confirmation, or Open Interest / Relative Strength Index confirmation. Less than 3 confluences means it is not a trade. This filter alone removes 80 percent of bad trades.
STEP 5 · STRIKE SELECTION
Choose your strike based on the technical analysis context: In The Money for high-conviction swing trades, At The Money for intraday momentum trades, slightly Out Of The Money only for fast breakouts. Never deep Out Of The Money, unless you fully accept the lottery-ticket math.
STEP 6 · EXPIRY CHOICE
Match the expiry to your view's time horizon. Same-day move expected → use weekly. 3 to 5 day swing → use the next-week expiry. Multi-week trend → use monthly. Never buy an expiry that is shorter than your trade plan.
When to take this trade — Top-Down Confluence Setup (the highest hit-rate technical entry)
Setup
Nifty (the index) is trending up on the daily chart (price above 20 Exponential Moving Average AND above the Central Pivot Range top). The Nifty Bank sector is the day's leader (up more than 0.5 percent while Nifty is flat). Your chosen stock is trending up on the daily chart, has retraced to a previous resistance that became support, and now shows a bullish candle pattern (Hammer / Bullish Engulfing) with volume above 1.5 times the 20-period average.
Entry
Buy the At The Money Call Option of that stock, with the next-week expiry. Use a limit order at the mid-price.
Stop Loss
Just below the bullish candle's low (NOT a percentage of the option premium). On the premium itself, set a hard stop at 35 percent loss as a safety net.
Target
The first target is the next prior swing high (book 50 percent there). Trail the rest with a daily 20-period Exponential Moving Average stop. Aim for at least a 1 to 2 risk-to-reward.
Skip if
Nifty is trending DOWN on the daily chart (you are fighting the index), OR the stock's sector is the day's worst performer (sector is acting as a drag), OR you cannot find at least 3 confluences (candle + level + volume).
WHY TECHNICAL ANALYSIS IS CRITICAL FOR OPTION BUYERS (NOT OPTIONAL)
DIRECTIONAL EDGE
Options give you 5 to 10 times leverage on direction. Getting direction wrong half the time still leaves you profitable, IF your winners are 2 to 3 times the size of your losers. Technical analysis gives you that directional hit-rate.
TIMING EDGE
A stock can take 10 days to move where you expected. By then Theta decay has eaten your premium. Technical analysis narrows the entry window so you catch the first 60 percent of the move.
LEVEL-BASED RISK
Technical analysis defines where you are wrong — the price level that invalidates your thesis. Your stop loss is set from that level, not from a random 30 percent drop in the option premium.
STRIKE SELECTION
The distance to the nearest support / resistance tells you how far price is likely to travel. That, combined with Delta, tells you which strike gives you the best rupee payoff per rupee risked.
EXIT DISCIPLINE
Targets based on technical analysis (next support / resistance, prior day high / low, Central Pivot Range edges) are objective. Without them, profit-taking becomes an emotional coin-flip between "hold more" and "book now".
CONTEXT FOR IMPLIED VOLATILITY
Technical analysis tells you when a move is "about to happen" — which is exactly when Implied Volatility is low. Buying options when both price AND Implied Volatility are low is the highest expected-value setup in the book.
6 CORE TECHNICAL ANALYSIS TOOLS YOU MUST MASTER
1 · MULTI-TIMEFRAME TREND
Use the daily, 15-minute, and 5-minute charts together. The daily chart is for bias, the 15-minute for the setup, and the 5-minute for the entry trigger. Never trade if the 15-minute and the daily charts disagree.
2 · SUPPORT & RESISTANCE
Previous Day High / Low, swing points, the Central Pivot Range. All technical analysis entries must happen NEAR a level — mid-range setups are coin-flips.
3 · VOLUME CONFIRMATION
Breakouts need 1.5 times average volume or more. Breakdowns on rising volume are real. No volume = no trade, no matter what the chart looks like.
4 · MOMENTUM (RSI & MACD)
A Relative Strength Index reading between 40 and 60 is neutral. Above 70 is overbought, below 30 is oversold. Divergence is the gold signal — price higher but the Relative Strength Index lower means the trend is dying.
5 · CANDLESTICK AT A LEVEL
A Hammer or Bullish Engulfing at a key level is far more meaningful than the same candle in the middle of a range. Pattern + level + volume = the trifecta of a valid technical analysis trade.
6 · OPTION CHAIN / OPEN INTEREST
Maximum Open Interest strikes, Put-Call Ratio, Open Interest change, and the Max Pain level are taught in their own dedicated section → Futures & Options Playbook → Option Chain.

PRE-TRADE TECHNICAL ANALYSIS CHECKLIST (7 ITEMS)

  • Index direction — Is Nifty / Bank Nifty aligned with my trade direction?
  • Sector momentum — Is the stock's sector leading or lagging today?
  • Daily trend — Am I trading with the stock's daily higher-timeframe trend?
  • Key level nearby — Is my entry within 0.3 to 0.5 percent of a prior support / resistance, the Central Pivot Range, or a pivot point?
  • Candle confirmation — Has a closed candle signalled my direction at that level?
  • Volume — Is the breakout or rejection backed by above-average volume?
  • Option chain agreement — Is the Open Interest change supporting the same directional view?

Fewer than 5 of these 7 = skip the trade. Options are expensive; half-formed setups are how accounts bleed.

BEGINNER PRIORITY — LEARN IN THIS ORDER

  • Price action first — candles, swing structure, support and resistance. 70 percent of your edge lives here.
  • Volume second — add volume as a confirmer; it filters out 80 percent of failed breakouts.
  • One indicator third — pick ONE momentum tool (Relative Strength Index OR Moving Average Convergence Divergence). Resist the urge to stack 5 indicators — that is noise, not signal.
  • Option chain last — once your technical analysis is solid, overlay Open Interest, Put-Call Ratio, and Max Pain to add conviction — never as the primary signal.
Three technical analysis mistakes that wreck option buyers: (1) trading a setup that ignores the higher-timeframe trend, (2) stacking 5 indicators and rationalising any direction from them, (3) entering mid-range without a clear invalidation level. Fix these three and your hit-rate jumps significantly.
HOW TO COMBINE INDICATORS WITH CANDLES

INDICATOR STACKING FOR A+ ENTRIES

  • Minimum confirmations: Never enter on just ONE indicator. Require at least a candle pattern + 1 indicator + volume.
  • Do not stack duplicates: The Relative Strength Index, Stochastics, and Moving Average Convergence Divergence are ALL momentum tools — they will always agree with each other. Pick ONE momentum tool + ONE trend tool + the Volume Weighted Average Price line.
  • The A+ checklist: Candle pattern + at support/resistance + volume spike + Volume Weighted Average Price aligned + Exponential Moving Average aligned + Relative Strength Index not at an extreme. Hit 5 of 6 → high conviction.
  • Lagging versus leading: Price action and volume are leading indicators. Moving averages and Moving Average Convergence Divergence are lagging indicators. Use leading to enter, and lagging to confirm direction.
  • Beware curve-fitting: Adding a 7th indicator because your backtest failed is noise. Keep your setup to a maximum of 3 tools.
