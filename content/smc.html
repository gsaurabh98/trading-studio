SMART MONEY CONCEPTS FOLLOW THE INSTITUTIONS

Who is "Smart Money"? Smart Money is just a nickname for the BIG players in the market — banks, mutual funds, hedge funds, foreign investors, and proprietary trading desks. They are the ones moving SIZE, the kind of money that actually pushes Nifty up or down.

Why does this matter? Big players cannot just click BUY for ₹500 crore. Nobody is on the other side of such a huge order. So they have to create the other side — they trick small retail traders into placing the opposite order, then they scoop up the liquidity. The clues they leave behind on the chart are called "footprints".

What is "Smart Money Concepts" (SMC)? SMC is just a way of READING those footprints so YOU know where the big money is going — instead of becoming the small fish they trap.

How is it different from regular chart reading?
· Regular technical analysis asks: "What shape is the chart making?" (triangles, flags, Head and Shoulders).
· Smart Money Concepts asks: "Where are retail stop losses parked, and which big player needs to fill a big order?"
Same chart, totally different question. Once you see it, you cannot un-see it.

Key short forms used on this page (read this once and the page will be easy):
· Higher High (HH) = the chart made a NEW high above the last one.
· Higher Low (HL) = the chart made a low ABOVE the last low (uptrend).
· Lower Low (LL) = the chart made a NEW low below the last one (downtrend).
· Lower High (LH) = the chart made a high BELOW the last high (downtrend).
· Break of Structure (BoS) = the trend has CONTINUED in the same direction.
· Change of Character (CHoCH) = the trend has FLIPPED. Up became Down (or vice versa).
· Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL) = a pool of stop loss orders sitting ABOVE a swing high (these are the stop losses of short sellers).
· Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL) = a pool of stop loss orders sitting BELOW a swing low (these are the stop losses of long buyers).
· Order Block (OB) = the last candle BEFORE a big strong move — this is where the institution’s order was placed.
· Fair Value Gap (FVG) = a 3-candle gap where price moved so fast that it skipped a price area. Price often comes BACK to fill this gap.
· Point Of Interest (POI) = a zone where you plan your entry.
· Higher Timeframe (HTF) = bigger charts (1 hour, 4 hour, daily).
· Lower Timeframe (LTF) = smaller charts (5 minute, 15 minute).
· Equilibrium (EQ) = the 50 percent middle line of any range.
· Previous Day High / Low (PDH / PDL) = yesterday’s top and bottom prices.
· Foreign Institutional Investors (FII) = foreign big-money players.
THE 4 PILLARS OF SMART MONEY CONCEPTS
Think of SMC like reading a crime scene:
· Pillar 1 (Structure) = which direction was the criminal walking?
· Pillar 2 (Liquidity) = where did they park their car (the loot)?
· Pillar 3 (Order Blocks) = where did they take a quick break?
· Pillar 4 (Fair Value Gap) = where did they leave a clear footprint by running too fast?

Read all 4 pillars in order to know exactly where the big money will go next.
1 · MARKET STRUCTURE
HH / HL / BoS / CHoCH
The roadmap. Tells you the prevailing direction and the moment that direction flips. Without structure, every other SMC tool is noise.
2 · LIQUIDITY
BSL / SSL / SWEEP
Pools of resting orders above swing highs (BSL) and below swing lows (SSL). Smart money pushes price into these pools to fill its own orders, then reverses.
3 · ORDER BLOCKS
OB / BREAKER
The last candle before a strong move — the institutional footprint zone. When price returns to mitigate (test) that block, expect a reaction.
4 · FAIR VALUE GAP
FVG / IMBALANCE
A 3-candle gap where price moved so fast that one direction had no opposing liquidity. Markets are mean-reverting — price tends to come back and "fill" it.
Read in this order. SMC is a hierarchy, not a checklist of equal-weight items. Structure first (which way are we going?), then liquidity (what's the target?), then OB/FVG (where is my entry?). Skip a layer and you'll trade a perfect OB into a trend that's quietly reversing against you.
1 · MARKET STRUCTURE — THE FOUNDATION
Every SMC trade starts with one question: are we in an uptrend, downtrend, or transitioning? Answered by reading the sequence of swing highs and swing lows. A swing high is a candle whose high is greater than the N candles on either side (N=2 for low TF, 5 for HTF). Same logic, inverted, for swing lows.
UPTREND — HH / HL SEQUENCE
Each impulse leg makes a Higher High, each pullback bottoms at a Higher Low. As long as both conditions hold, the structure is bullish. Trade with the trend — buy CE at pullbacks into demand.
DOWNTREND — LL / LH SEQUENCE
Each impulse leg makes a Lower Low, each bounce tops at a Lower High. Structure is bearish. Trade with the trend — buy PE at pullbacks into supply.
BoS vs CHoCH — THE TWO STRUCTURE EVENTS THAT MATTER
Only two events change the meaning of structure. Mis-reading them is the single biggest reason newer SMC traders take wrong-direction trades.
BREAK OF STRUCTURE (BoS) — CONTINUATION
Price breaks the previous swing high in the same direction as trend. In an uptrend, BoS = a new HH. Meaning: the trend is alive and likely to extend. Trade with it — look for a pullback into the OB/FVG that produced this BoS.
CHANGE OF CHARACTER (CHoCH) — REVERSAL
Price breaks the most recent counter-trend swing. In a downtrend, CHoCH = breaking the last LH to the upside. Meaning: the trend MAY be over — the character has shifted. Do not enter on CHoCH; wait for confirmation (next BoS in the new direction).
Common mistake: calling every break a BoS or every pullback a CHoCH. Rule of thumb — BoS = with the trend, CHoCH = against the trend. If a wave breaks structure in the same direction the existing wave was going, it's a BoS. If it breaks against, it's a CHoCH.
When to take this trade — The Break of Structure Pullback (the safest trend-continuation entry in Smart Money Concepts)
Setup
The 1-hour Nifty chart is in a clear UPTREND (Higher Highs and Higher Lows visible on the last 4-5 swings). Price has just broken ABOVE the most recent swing high (called a Break of Structure or BoS). After the breakout, price has pulled BACK and is now sitting at the Order Block (the last red candle BEFORE the strong-up move). For a downtrend setup, mirror this: HHLL trend → BoS to the downside → pullback to the last green candle.
Entry
Wait for a bullish reversal candle (Hammer, Bullish Engulfing, or Pin Bar) to CLOSE inside the Order Block on the 15-minute chart. BUY a Call Option that is At The Money or one strike In The Money the moment that candle closes. For a Put Option entry on a downtrend setup, mirror the logic.
Stop Loss
Place the chart stop loss 5-10 Nifty points BELOW the bottom of the Order Block. On the option premium, set a hard stop loss at 30-40 percent of the premium paid. If price closes back below the prior swing high (the BoS level), exit immediately — the breakout has failed.
Target
Target 1 = the most recent liquidity pool above (the previous high or the equilibrium of the prior range) — book 50 percent of the position. Target 2 = the next Higher Timeframe resistance (1-hour or 4-hour swing high). Trail the rest with a 15-minute swing-low stop.
Skip if
The Higher Timeframe trend (daily) is in the OPPOSITE direction (you would be fighting the daily flow), OR the Break of Structure happened on LOW volume (likely a fake-out), OR there is no clean Order Block to enter from (a vertical move with no clear pullback zone).
2 · LIQUIDITY — WHERE STOPS LIVE
Retail traders place stop-losses in predictable spots: above the most recent high (if short) or below the most recent low (if long). Those stops, together with breakout buyers' entry orders, sit there as pending order pools. Smart money pushes price into those pools to fill their own large positions, then reverses. This is a liquidity sweep (a.k.a. stop hunt). Spotting one in real time is the single most profitable skill in SMC.
BUY-SIDE LIQUIDITY (BSL)
The pool of buy stop orders resting above a swing high (short-sellers' SLs + breakout-buyers' entries). Price spikes through, fills the orders, then often reverses. If you're long — this is your profit-take zone.
SELL-SIDE LIQUIDITY (SSL)
The pool of sell stop orders resting below a swing low (long-buyers' SLs + breakout-sellers' entries). Price wicks down, sweeps the lows, then often reverses up. If you're short — this is your profit-take zone.
LIQUIDITY POOLS WORTH MARKING ON YOUR CHART
EQUAL HIGHS / EQUAL LOWS
EQH / EQL
Two or more swing highs at the same level form a magnet. Retail calls it "double top" — in SMC, it's a flashing red BSL target. Same with EQL below.
PREV DAY HIGH / LOW
PDH / PDL
The most respected intraday levels in Indian indices. Every desk in Mumbai has them mapped. PDH/PDL sweep on the open is a daily setup. See Support & Resistance.
SESSION HIGH / LOW
ASIAN / LON / NY
In FX SMC, the Asian range top & bottom are key. For Indian indices, use opening 15-min range + previous session close.
ROUND NUMBERS
23500 / 24000
Psychological levels. Massive option-OI buildups cluster here in Nifty/Bank Nifty. Frequently swept before genuine moves.
Liquidity sweep recipe (most common SMC setup):
1. HTF (1h/4h) structure is bullish.
2. LTF (5m/15m) makes a counter-trend pullback that sweeps the prior swing low (SSL).
3. Right after the sweep, LTF prints a CHoCH up.
4. Enter long on the OB or FVG that produced the CHoCH. SL just below the sweep wick.
5. Target the opposite-side BSL (prior swing high or HTF resistance).
REAL NIFTY EXAMPLE — TUESDAY MORNING PDH SWEEP
SETUP
Day: Tuesday (Nifty weekly expiry) — the single biggest sweep / expansion day of the week.
HTF context: Daily structure bullish (HH-HL-HH); 1h pulled back into 50% of the recent leg up.
Marked liquidity: Previous Day High (PDH) = 24,180, with three prior 5m closes parked right under it — classic BSL pool (every retail short-stop and breakout-buy order resting just above).
Premarket: Gift Nifty +50, expected open 24,210 (~30 pts above PDH).
Plan: let the open spike take out PDH, then look for an LTF rejection & CHoCH back down for an intraday PE buy to the next SSL pool (PDL).

WHY THIS WORKED — FIVE INPUTS ALIGNED

  • Time of day: Tuesday 09:15-09:30 is the highest-probability sweep window of the week (expiry-day expansion).
  • Pre-marked liquidity: PDH was a textbook BSL pool — visible to every desk in Mumbai.
  • Sweep depth: 45 pts above PDH = enough to fill stops, too little to count as a real breakout. The 24,225 wick was the “tell”.
  • CHoCH confirmation: 5m closed back below the swing low — not a fake-rejection candle, an actual structure flip.
  • Entry quality: FVG mid is the institutional 50% — gives R:R 1:3 with SL just past the sweep wick. Tight enough that you’re wrong fast and right cheap.
FALSE-SWEEP TRAPS — HOW TO AVOID THEM
A wick above PDH is not automatically a sweep. About 40% of the time the wick is just the start of a real breakout — if you fade every one you’ll get steamrolled. Use these four filters before pulling the trigger.
1 · NO CHoCH AFTER WICK
SKIP
If the 5m doesn’t close back inside the prior range AND break the most recent LTF swing low (CHoCH), it’s a real breakout, not a sweep. No CHoCH = no trade.
2 · WEAK DISPLACEMENT
SKIP
A real reversal leaves an FVG / 3+ strong-body candles. If the move down after the sweep is just choppy small candles, institutions aren’t selling — you’re fading retail noise.
3 · HTF AGAINST YOU
SKIP
If 1h structure is bullish, even a clean PDH sweep usually gets bought back. Counter-HTF sweeps win <30% of the time in Indian indices — only trade sweeps that are with the 1h trend.
4 · SL TOO WIDE
SKIP
If the sweep wick is >80 pts and your SL would be 100+ pts away, the trade no longer has the R:R that makes the strategy work. Skip — better setups print every day.
When to take this trade — The Liquidity Sweep Reversal (the highest reward-to-risk Smart Money Concepts setup)
Setup
It is between 9:30 AM and 11:30 AM IST (highest sweep success window). On the 1-hour Nifty chart, the trend is in your planned direction (for a Put Option trade, uptrend OR equilibrium with overhead resistance; for a Call Option trade, downtrend OR equilibrium with support below). Price has just SWEPT a clear liquidity pool — it spiked ABOVE the Previous Day High (BSL pool) for a Put Option trade, OR spiked BELOW the Previous Day Low (SSL pool) for a Call Option trade. The wick was 30-80 Nifty points beyond the level, then closed BACK INSIDE the prior range. A 5-minute Change of Character (CHoCH) candle has formed in the OPPOSITE direction.
Entry
Wait for price to retrace UP into the Fair Value Gap or Order Block created by the displacement candle (usually the 50 percent mid-line of the move). BUY a Put Option (or Call Option) that is At The Money the moment price taps that zone AND a 5-minute reversal candle closes (Bearish Engulfing for a sweep above, Bullish Engulfing for a sweep below). Use the current-week expiry only.
Stop Loss
Place the chart stop loss 5-10 Nifty points beyond the SWEEP WICK (above for a short / Put Option, below for a long / Call Option). On the option premium, use a hard 30-40 percent stop loss. If price closes back beyond the swept level on the 5-minute chart, exit immediately — the sweep has FAILED and a real breakout is forming.
Target
Target 1 = the OPPOSITE liquidity pool of the day (so for a sweep above the Previous Day High, target is the Previous Day Low or today’s opening range low). Book 50 percent there. Trail the rest with a 5-minute swing-high stop. EXIT everything by 2:30 PM IST regardless — sweep trades work in the morning, not the afternoon.
Skip if
The Higher Timeframe (1-hour) trend is AGAINST your sweep direction (sweep success rate drops below 30 percent), OR there is no clear Change of Character on the 5-minute chart after the sweep, OR the sweep wick was below 30 points (too shallow, stops not actually filled), OR the displacement after the sweep is choppy with no Fair Value Gap (institutions are not really reversing — just retail noise).
3 · ORDER BLOCKS — THE INSTITUTIONAL ENTRY ZONE
An Order Block (OB) is the last candle in one direction before a strong move in the opposite direction. The theory: institutions could not fill their entire size at the extreme price; their unfilled orders rest inside that last candle. When price returns to test it ("mitigates" the OB), expect a reaction. This is the single most common SMC entry zone.
BULLISH ORDER BLOCK
The last RED candle before a strong up-move that breaks structure. When price returns down to that candle's range, expect buyers to step back in. Entry zone for CE buying.
BEARISH ORDER BLOCK
The last GREEN candle before a strong down-move that breaks structure. When price returns up to that candle's range, expect sellers to step in. Entry zone for PE buying.
VALIDATING AN ORDER BLOCK — THE 5 CHECKS
Not every last candle is an OB. A valid OB must produce a strong, displacement-style move. These five checks separate a real OB from a random candle you painted a box around.
1 · DISPLACEMENT
3+ STRONG CANDLES
The move away from the OB must be fast and aggressive — ideally 3 consecutive strong-body candles in one direction.
2 · BREAKS STRUCTURE
BoS REQUIRED
The displacement must take out the most recent swing point. An OB that doesn't break structure is just a candle, not an institutional zone.
3 · FAIR VALUE GAP
CONFLUENCE
A real OB usually leaves an FVG/imbalance in its wake. No FVG = weaker conviction = lower-grade OB.
4 · UNTESTED
FRESH ZONE
First touch is best. Once price has already revisited and bounced from the OB, its remaining order flow is mostly used up.
5 · HTF CONTEXT
ALIGNMENT
A bullish OB on 5m only matters if 1h structure also says bullish. Counter-HTF OBs fail far more often.
RELATED ZONES — BREAKER BLOCK & MITIGATION BLOCK
A clean OB is the gold-standard entry, but real charts also produce two close cousins. Knowing which is which prevents the most common SMC mis-classification — calling a Breaker an OB, getting in the wrong direction, and confidently losing money.
BREAKER BLOCK — THE FLIPPED OB
Definition: an OB that failed once — price broke cleanly through it. When price returns, its polarity is now flipped: a former bullish OB that broke down becomes a bearish resistance; a former bearish OB that broke up becomes a bullish support.

Why it works: the institutions that originally placed orders at the OB had to defend it. When price broke through, those orders got triggered AND new orders got built up against the new direction. The zone is now thick with the opposite-side liquidity.

Same logic as classical Support-Becomes-Resistance, with an institutional twist — only count it as a Breaker if the original OB met the 5 validation checks first.
MITIGATION BLOCK — THE FAILED RUN-UP
Definition: the OB whose move failed to take new highs/lows — it produced a swing point but didn’t make a fresh HH (or LL). When price comes back to it, the trapped buyers/sellers are still underwater and need an exit.

Why it works: trapped longs from the failed run-up will sell into any rally back to their entry to break even. Their pain is the algo’s alpha — same supply concept as Wyckoff distribution.

Distinguishing test: if the original move set a new HH/LL, it’s an OB. If it failed to and rolled over, it’s a Mitigation Block. They look identical on the chart but trade in opposite directions — misclassify and you’re fighting the trapped flow instead of joining it.

When to take this trade — The Order Block Mitigation Entry (the cleanest institutional-zone setup in Smart Money Concepts)
Setup
The 1-hour Nifty trend is in your direction (uptrend for a Call Option, downtrend for a Put Option). On the 15-minute chart, you can see a clean Order Block — the LAST opposite-coloured candle BEFORE a strong displacement move that broke structure (BoS). The Order Block must pass at least 4 of the 5 validation checks (clear displacement, broke structure, near a Higher Timeframe level, no inefficiency inside it, fresh / untouched). Price is now PULLING BACK to mitigate the Order Block.
Entry
Wait for price to ENTER the Order Block zone (between the high and low of that specific candle). Watch the 5-minute chart for a clear reversal candle (Bullish Pin Bar / Hammer for Call Option, Bearish Pin Bar / Shooting Star for Put Option). BUY the At The Money option the moment that 5-minute reversal candle CLOSES. Use the current-week expiry only.
Stop Loss
Place the chart stop loss 5-10 Nifty points BEYOND the far edge of the Order Block (below the low for Call Option, above the high for Put Option). On premium, use a 30-40 percent hard stop loss. If a 15-minute candle CLOSES through the Order Block, exit immediately — the institutional level has been violated.
Target
Target 1 = the most recent swing high (for Call Option) or swing low (for Put Option) — book 50 percent of the position there. Target 2 = the next Higher Timeframe liquidity pool (Previous Day High, Previous Week High, etc). Trail the remainder with a 15-minute swing stop.
Skip if
The Order Block has already been TESTED earlier (mitigated Order Blocks lose their power), OR there was no clean displacement after the Order Block formed (no Fair Value Gap or strong-body candle), OR the Higher Timeframe trend is opposite to your trade direction, OR the Order Block is too far away from price (more than 150 Nifty points to entry — option premium will leak too much waiting).
4 · FAIR VALUE GAP (FVG) — THE IMBALANCE
A Fair Value Gap (also called an imbalance or BPR) is a 3-candle pattern where the middle candle's price range was so violent that there's a visible gap between candle 1's wick and candle 3's wick. That gap represents untraded price — pure one-sided buying or selling. Markets are mean-reverting; price tends to return and fill (mitigate) the gap before continuing.
BULLISH FVG — STRONG UP MOVE
Definition: Candle 3's low is above Candle 1's high. The gap between them is the bullish FVG. How to use: when price returns down to the FVG, treat it as a buy zone. SL below the FVG bottom; entry usually at the FVG mid (50%).
BEARISH FVG — STRONG DOWN MOVE
Definition: Candle 3's high is below Candle 1's low. The gap between them is the bearish FVG. How to use: when price returns up to the FVG, treat it as a sell zone. SL above the FVG top; entry at the FVG mid (50%).
Inverse FVG (IFVG): when price breaks cleanly through an existing FVG without respecting it, the FVG's polarity flips. A bullish FVG that broke down becomes a bearish FVG on the way back up. Use the same way as a Breaker Block.
HOW AN FVG GETS FILLED — AND WHY IT VANISHES FROM YOUR CHART
This is the part that trips everyone up. A gap does not fill when a candle “closes” inside it, and it does not need to fill top-to-bottom. It fills the instant a later candle’s wick reaches the middle of the gap. Body and close are irrelevant — only the wick’s reach matters.
BULLISH FVG — FILLED FROM ABOVE
PRICE DROPS IN — usually a RED wick
A bullish FVG sits below price (it’s a support zone). To fill it, price has to come back DOWN into it — so it’s normally a red (down) candle’s lower wick that does the filling. It counts as fully filled the moment that wick reaches the gap’s 50% midpoint.
BEARISH FVG — FILLED FROM BELOW
PRICE RISES IN — usually a GREEN wick
A bearish FVG sits above price (it’s a resistance zone). To fill it, price has to climb back UP into it — so it’s normally a green (up) candle’s upper wick that does the filling. Same rule: fully filled once that wick reaches the 50% midpoint.

HOW THIS APP GRADES “FILL %” (and when the box disappears)

  • Wick, not body. The app checks each later candle’s high / low (the wick tip), never the close. One wick poking in is enough — the candle does not have to close inside the gap.
  • Halfway = done. The moment a wick reaches the gap’s 50% midpoint (the line SMC calls the CE — Consequent Encroachment), the gap is treated as 100% filled. You do not need a full edge-to-edge fill.
  • Edge touch = partial. A wick that only nicks the near edge is a small partial fill — the box stays on the chart.
  • Fill % scale: 0% = fresh / untouched (strongest). Up to ~50% = partly eaten (weaker). Reaching the midpoint snaps it to 100%; once it’s ≥ 90% the box is removed from the chart and no longer used as a trade reason.
  • Colour is a by-product, not a rule. Filling only requires price to travel in the right direction; the engine never looks at whether the candle is red or green.
Why a gap you expected can be missing. The chart only draws unfilled gaps. If price has already traded back through a gap’s midpoint — even weeks later, even with a single wick — that gap is “mitigated” and the box is dropped. So a perfectly valid 3-candle gap can be invisible today simply because a later candle already filled it. No box = either no gap formed, or the gap has already been filled.
WORKED EXAMPLES — READING REAL WEEKLY CANDLES
The trap that fools your eyes first. The gap is measured between candle 1 and candle 3 only. The big middle (displacement) candle is ignored in the math — but its long body paints straight through the empty band, so your eye sees a solid candle and thinks “there’s no gap here.” The void is the price range the neighbours (the calm weeks on either side) never traded — not the range of the big candle itself.
EXAMPLE 1 — A REAL BULLISH FVG (weekly)
Three consecutive weekly candles:
· Candle 1 (16 Feb): O=328.7, H=346.55, L=324.25, C=326.75
· Candle 2 (23 Feb): O=328, H=442.7, L=312.45, C=435.85 — the big green displacement week (a ~130-pt range)
· Candle 3 (02 Mar): O=421.4, H=528.95, L=405.95, C=465.05

Test: a bullish FVG needs candle3.LOW > candle1.HIGH → 405.95 > 346.55 ✓. So the band 346.55 → 405.95 (₹59.4) is a void: the week before never traded above 346.55, the week after never traded below 405.95. Only the giant 23 Feb candle passed through it. That is the blue box — and notice the box edges come from C1 and C3, never from the big candle in the middle.
EXAMPLE 2 — “3 RED CANDLES” THAT IS NOT SHOWN (and why)
A clean down-staircase of three red weeks:
· Candle 1 (05 Jan): O=454.4, H=464.45, L=410.40, C=417.15
· Candle 2 (12 Jan): O=399, H=403.45, L=363, C=364.85 — the displacement week
· Candle 3 (19 Jan): O=362.5, H=363.05, L=300, C=304.05

First surprise: a bearish FVG needs candle1.LOW > candle3.HIGH → 410.40 > 363.05 ✓. So a real 47-pt bearish gap (363.05 → 410.40) DID form here. Red colour alone never makes an FVG — but in this case the maths genuinely passed.

So why is there no box? Because it was filled later. The gap’s midpoint is (410.40 + 363.05) / 2 = 386.7. Weeks afterward, the 23 Feb candle rallied to H=442.70 — straight past 386.7 — so the gap hit 100% fill and the box was dropped. The same rally that built Example 1’s bullish gap is what erased this bearish one.
“Wouldn’t every candle then have an FVG?” No — and this is the key. On most 3-candle groups, candle 3 trades back into candle 1’s range (normal overlap), so candle3.LOW < candle1.HIGH → no void → nothing drawn. A gap only appears after a violent one-candle move big enough that the neighbours don’t share any price. That’s rare — which is why you see only a few FVG boxes on a whole chart, not one per candle.
EXAMPLE 3 — A WICK TOUCHED IT BUT IT’S STILL THERE
Say an orange (bearish) FVG runs from 560 (bottom) to 600 (top) — midpoint = 580. A later green candle’s upper wick pokes up to ~563, just grazing the bottom edge, then falls away.

Result: the box stays. 563 only reached the near edge — nowhere near the 580 midpoint — so it logs as a small partial fill only. To retire this gap, a wick has to reach 580. Touching the edge is not enough; it must reach the middle.

  • FVG was created by clean displacement — 60 pts on 3 green candles, not a slow drift. That’s the difference between an institutional FVG and a random gap between candles.
  • Entry at the FVG mid (50%), not the upper edge. The 50% rule gives the same R:R every time and avoids the “just barely missed it” psychological trap.
  • SL below the FVG bottom, not at it. If the FVG fully fills, the institutional thesis is broken — close the trade. The FVG’s lower edge is your “wrong” level.
  • Target = next opposite liquidity pool (here PDH 24,260). Don’t use percentage targets; use structural ones.
  • Time was right — 10:00-11:30 IST on a Wed/Thu is a high-probability FVG-fill window. Outside the 09:15-12:00 + 13:30-15:15 windows, FVGs fill less reliably (lunch & pre-close drift).
FVG QUALITY CHECKS — IS THIS ONE TRADABLE?
Every chart has dozens of FVGs — most are noise. Use these four filters before treating an FVG as a real entry zone.
1 · DISPLACEMENT SIZE
PASS >= 1 ATR
The candle that created the FVG must move ≥ 1 ATR(14) of the timeframe. A 5-pt FVG on Nifty 5m (~15 ATR) is too small — spread + slippage will eat any edge.
2 · IN-TREND DIRECTION
HTF AGREES
Bullish FVG only if 1h is bullish; bearish FVG only if 1h is bearish. Counter-HTF FVGs fill but rarely produce continuation — you get stopped on the next leg.
3 · UNFILLED
FRESH ZONE
First-touch FVG works best. If price has already wicked into the FVG once, the order flow inside is mostly used up — subsequent fills are 50/50.
4 · NOT TOO OLD
SAME SESSION
Intraday FVGs lose validity after 1-2 sessions. A Monday FVG traded on Friday is too stale — the regime has likely changed. For multi-day FVGs use 1h or higher.
FVG VARIANTS — ONE NAME, FOUR FLAVOURS
VariantAliasesHow to spot itHow to trade it
Regular FVG Imbalance, BPR (Balanced Price Range) Standard 3-candle gap (C1.high < C3.low for bullish). Entry at FVG mid (50%); SL beyond FVG far edge.
Inverse FVG (IFVG) Flipped FVG, Polarity FVG Existing FVG that price broke cleanly through — the gap survives but its polarity flips. Treat as opposite-direction zone (bullish FVG that broke down → sell on retest up).
Implied FVG (iFVG) Wick-based gap, Hidden FVG FVG hidden inside a single large candle — the wick + body create the imbalance internally. Advanced — skip until you can spot regular FVGs reliably.

When to take this trade — The Fair Value Gap Fill Entry (the highest reward-to-risk continuation setup in Smart Money Concepts)
Setup
The 1-hour Nifty trend is in your direction (uptrend for a Call Option, downtrend for a Put Option). On the 15-minute chart, a strong displacement candle has just broken structure (Break of Structure / BoS) AND created a clean Fair Value Gap (FVG) — the empty space where there is no overlap between the wick of candle 1 and the wick of candle 3. The Fair Value Gap is FRESH (untouched). Price is now pulling back into the gap.
Entry
BUY the option (Call Option for an uptrend, Put Option for a downtrend) the moment price taps the 50 percent midpoint of the Fair Value Gap AND a 5-minute reversal candle closes there (Bullish Engulfing for a Call Option, Bearish Engulfing for a Put Option). Use the At The Money strike with current-week expiry.
Stop Loss
Place the chart stop loss 5-10 Nifty points BEYOND the FAR EDGE of the Fair Value Gap (below the gap for a Call Option, above the gap for a Put Option). On the option premium, use a 30-40 percent hard stop loss. If a 5-minute candle CLOSES beyond the far edge of the Fair Value Gap, the gap is INVALIDATED — exit immediately.
Target
Target 1 = the swing high (for Call Option) or swing low (for Put Option) that originally created the displacement. Book 50 percent of the position there. Trail the rest with a 15-minute swing stop and aim for the next Higher Timeframe liquidity pool (Previous Day High / Previous Day Low).
Skip if
The Fair Value Gap has already been TESTED earlier (filled gaps lose their power), OR the Higher Timeframe trend is opposite to your trade, OR there is no clear structural break (Break of Structure) before the gap formed (without BoS, the gap is just noise), OR the Fair Value Gap is too small (less than 15 Nifty points — not enough room for a clean trade).
5 · PREMIUM / DISCOUNT — INSTITUTIONAL ENTRY ZONES
Draw a line from the most recent significant swing low to the swing high (or vice-versa). The 50% midpoint is the equilibrium. Above 50% is premium (overpriced); below 50% is discount (underpriced). The institutional rule: buy in discount, sell in premium. This is the SMC version of Fibonacci 50%.
DISCOUNT — LONGS ALLOWED
Price is in the lower half of its recent range. Below 50%. In a bullish trend, this is where you look for OBs and FVGs to go long. Above-50% bullish entries are riskier and give worse R:R.
PREMIUM — SHORTS ALLOWED
Price is in the upper half of its recent range. Above 50%. In a bearish trend, this is where you look for OBs and FVGs to go short / buy PE. Below-50% bearish entries are riskier.
EQUILIBRIUM (EQ) — the 50% midpoint itself. Not just a dividing line; it’s a magnet. Price returns to EQ on roughly 70% of intraday legs on Indian indices — that’s why institutions use it as their default entry. The phrase “trade from EQ” means: wait for price to retrace to the 50% of the most recent impulse leg, then enter in the trend direction. ICT’s “optimal trade entry” (OTE) is the 0.62-0.79 retracement zone of the impulse, with EQ as its anchor — a slightly tighter version of the same idea.
PD ARRAYS — THE FULL ENTRY-ZONE TOOLKIT
“PD Arrays” (Premium / Discount Arrays) is ICT’s name for the complete set of institutional zones price interacts with. Premium / Discount tells you which side of the range to be on; PD Arrays tell you exactly where in that side to enter. The five arrays below cover ~95% of clean intraday SMC entries on Nifty / BankNifty.
1 · ORDER BLOCK
PRIMARY
Last opposite-color candle before BoS + displacement. Strongest zone — trade if it’s in discount (for longs) or premium (for shorts).
2 · FVG / IMBALANCE
PRIMARY
3-candle gap that price tends to fill. Easier to spot than an OB — great secondary entry once you can identify displacement.
3 · BREAKER BLOCK
SECONDARY
A failed OB that price broke through; polarity flips on retest. Use only with HTF confluence.
4 · EQUILIBRIUM (50%)
CONFLUENCE
The 50% line of the impulse leg. Tradable on its own only on very clean trends; otherwise use it to upgrade an OB / FVG that happens to sit at EQ.
5 · LIQUIDITY (BSL/SSL)
TARGET-SIDE
Buy-side and sell-side pools mark where to take profit, not where to enter. Always project your target to the next opposite-side liquidity pool.
DECISION TREE — WHERE DO I ENTER?

THE 4-STEP FILTER YOU CAN MEMORISE

  • Step 1 — HTF bias: 1h / 4h structure bullish, bearish, or ranging? If ranging, skip the session. SMC needs trend.
  • Step 2 — Premium or Discount? Mark the most recent impulse leg, find its 50%. Are you in the right half for the HTF bias? If not, wait. Don’t buy in premium even if you see an OB.
  • Step 3 — PD Array on the right side? Find an OB / FVG / Breaker in the correct half. OB > FVG > Breaker in priority. If multiple stack at the same level, that’s an A+ setup.
  • Step 4 — Liquidity-swept first? Don’t enter the PD Array until LTF has swept its opposite-side liquidity (the inducement). Skip step 4 and you’ll get caught in inducements 60% of the time.
The premium/discount mistake every beginner makes: using the wrong leg to draw the 50%. Pick the most recent impulse leg in the HTF trend direction — not the choppy ranging move, not the leg before it, not the daily range. If you’re long-biased on Nifty 1h, the 50% you care about is the latest 1h leg up’s midpoint, projected onto your 5m chart.
When to take this trade — The Discount-Zone Long Entry (institutional bottom-buying setup in Smart Money Concepts)
Setup
The 1-hour Nifty trend is BULLISH (Higher Highs and Higher Lows on the 1-hour and 4-hour charts). Mark the most recent impulse leg up (from the last clean swing low to the most recent swing high). Calculate the 50 percent equilibrium of this leg. Price has now PULLED BACK below the 50 percent line into the DISCOUNT ZONE (between the 50 percent line and the swing low). Inside the discount zone, there is a clean Order Block OR Fair Value Gap from the original up-move. For a Put Option setup, mirror this: bearish HTF + price RALLIED into the PREMIUM ZONE above 50 percent.
Entry
Wait for price to TAP the Order Block or Fair Value Gap inside the discount zone. BUY the At The Money Call Option the moment a 5-minute bullish reversal candle CLOSES (Bullish Engulfing, Hammer, or Pin Bar). Use current-week expiry only. Use At The Money strike or one strike In The Money — do NOT use Out Of The Money in this setup, the move can take 1-3 hours to complete.
Stop Loss
Place the chart stop loss 5-10 Nifty points BELOW the swing low that defines the bottom of the discount zone (because if price breaks that low, the entire premise — that we are in a higher-low uptrend — is broken). On the option premium, use 30-40 percent hard stop loss.
Target
Target 1 = the 50 percent equilibrium line itself (book 50 percent of the position). Target 2 = the original swing high that defined the top of the impulse leg (this is where buy-side liquidity sits and where the next move will hunt). Trail the rest with a 15-minute swing-low stop loss.
Skip if
The Higher Timeframe trend has FLIPPED to bearish (a Lower Low on the 1-hour chart invalidates the long bias), OR price is in the PREMIUM zone (above 50 percent — you would be buying expensive), OR there is no clean Order Block or Fair Value Gap in the discount zone (no institutional level to lean on), OR the India Volatility Index is above 20 (too noisy — structural setups break down on event days).
THE COMPLETE SMC SETUP — 6-STEP WORKFLOW
This is the playbook that ties all four pillars together. Run through it on every trade. If any one step doesn't check out, skip the trade — SMC's edge comes from confluence, not from forcing setups.

THE WORKFLOW

  • 1 · HTF BIAS — on a higher timeframe (1h / 4h / daily), what is the structure? HH/HL = bullish bias only; LL/LH = bearish bias only. If structure is unclear/ranging, do not trade SMC; switch to range tactics.
  • 2 · HTF POI — on the same HTF, mark one Point of Interest in the direction of bias: a valid OB or FVG sitting in discount (for longs) or premium (for shorts).
  • 3 · WAIT FOR TAP — do nothing until price actually returns to your HTF POI. Most days, it won't. That's fine.
  • 4 · LTF SWEEP + CHoCH — once price is inside the HTF POI, switch to LTF (5m / 15m). Look for a liquidity sweep (it takes out a small LTF swing) followed by a CHoCH in the direction of your HTF bias.
  • 5 · ENTRY — enter on the LTF OB / FVG that produced the CHoCH. SL goes just past the sweep wick (15–25 pts for Nifty, 50–80 pts for Bank Nifty intraday).
  • 6 · TARGET — minimum 1:3 R:R. The first natural target is the opposite-side liquidity on LTF; the extended target is the HTF liquidity (previous HH / LL, PDH/PDL).

PRE-TRADE CHECKLIST

  • HTF structure clearly bullish (or bearish) — not in a range or transition.
  • HTF POI marked before price arrives, never drawn retroactively.
  • Price has reached the POI and swept LTF liquidity.
  • LTF has confirmed a CHoCH (not just a wick).
  • SL has a clean technical level (sweep wick + 5–10 pts buffer), not a fixed-rupee SL.
  • R:R ≥ 1:3 from entry to the nearest opposite liquidity.
  • You can name the trade in one sentence: "Long because HTF bull, swept SSL into discount OB, CHoCH up, target prior HH."
SMC ON INDIAN INDICES — HOW IT ACTUALLY PLAYS OUT
SMC was developed for FX and US futures. The framework transfers cleanly to Nifty / Bank Nifty / Fin Nifty if you use the right anchor levels. Here is how the daily playbook maps to Indian intraday.
PRE-OPEN (8:30–9:15 IST)
PREP
Mark on the chart: PDH, PDL, PD Close, prior day OB/FVG that wasn't mitigated, weekly OB/FVG. Note the HTF bias from the daily / 1h chart.
FIRST 15 MIN (9:15–9:30)
SWEEP SETUP
Most days, the open sweeps PDH or PDL in the first 15–30 min. Wait for the sweep + CHoCH on 5m. Do not enter on the first 1-minute candle — it's the institutional accumulation candle, not your entry.
10:30–14:30 (TREND DAY)
EXECUTE
If a clean 10am sweep + CHoCH happens, the rest of the day is usually a trend. Buy CE/PE on the first pullback into the 5m/15m OB; trail SL to breakeven after 1:1.
Bank Nifty specifics: Bank Nifty's tick-size (5 pts) and intraday range (300–800 pts) make sweeps cleaner and more frequent than Nifty. Stick to 5m / 15m. SL 50–80 pts; target 150–300 pts. Avoid Fin Nifty for SMC — lower liquidity = more random wicks that look like sweeps but aren't.
SMC + OPTION BUYING — STRIKE & EXPIRY GUIDANCE
SCALP (5–20 min)
ITM / ATM
5m sweep + CHoCH, target one liquidity pool away. Use ATM or 1 ITM — high delta means the option moves rupee-for-rupee with the index. Expiry day or current week. SL on premium = 25–40% of entry.
SWING INTRADAY (1–3 hr)
ATM
15m HTF POI + 5m entry, hold to opposite HTF liquidity. ATM same-week expiry. Trail SL on swing structure (each new HL bumps SL up). Target 1:3 to 1:5 R:R.
POSITIONAL (1–3 days)
ITM NEXT WEEK
Daily HTF POI + 4h entry. Use 1–2 strikes ITM, next-week expiry to reduce theta decay. Wider SL. Target multi-session liquidity. Hedge with the opposite OTM if holding over events.
SMC's worst weakness for option buyers: the framework can give you a perfect chart entry but say nothing about theta or IV crush. A correct SMC long that takes 3 hours to play out can still lose on the option if IV bleeds. Always check IV percentile and time-to-expiry before sizing. COMMON SMC MISTAKES — HOW TRADERS BLOW UP
1 · OB EVERYWHERE
Drawing an OB on every red/green candle turns the chart into a heat-map. A valid OB requires displacement + BoS + FVG. If you can't point to those, it's not an OB — it's a candle.
2 · TRADING WITHOUT HTF
Taking a 5m setup against the 1h structure. SMC works only with HTF alignment. Counter-HTF "setups" produce 30% hit rates and look like they were sabotaged.
3 · CHASING WITHOUT A SWEEP
Entering before liquidity is taken. If price hasn't swept anything, smart money hasn't filled its orders yet. Wait for the wick that takes the obvious stops, THEN enter.
4 · CONFUSING BoS & CHoCH
Calling a CHoCH a BoS pushes you to trade continuation when the market is actually reversing. Memorise: with-trend = BoS; against-trend = CHoCH.
5 · PREMIUM LONGS / DISCOUNT SHORTS
Buying near swing highs ("breakout!") and selling near swing lows ("breakdown!"). Both pay the worst R:R in SMC. Wait for the pullback into discount (for longs) or premium (for shorts).
6 · NO STRUCTURE = NO TRADE
SMC does not work in choppy ranges. If you can't draw HH/HL or LL/LH on the HTF without effort, the market is not trending. Switch to range tactics or sit out.

  • PDH / PDL sweep + CHoCH — the single most reliable Indian-index SMC setup. Available almost every day in Nifty / Bank Nifty.
  • Bullish & bearish OB with the 5 validation checks — the bread-and-butter entry zone.
  • Bullish & bearish FVG on 5m / 15m — the easiest secondary entry once you can spot an OB. Use the FVG quality checks (size / in-trend / unfilled / not too old) before treating one as tradable.

If you find yourself taking 5 or more Smart Money Concepts trades a day, you are NOT running Smart Money Concepts; you are running impulse trading with Smart Money Concepts vocabulary.
When to take this trade — The Previous Day Low Sweep and Change of Character (the most reliable Smart Money Concepts setup in Indian indices)
Setup
It is between 9:30 AM and 12:30 PM IST. On the daily chart, Nifty is in a clear uptrend (Higher Highs and Higher Lows over the last 4 to 6 weeks). Today, after the opening, price has dropped DOWN and swept just below the Previous Day Low (a Sell-Side Liquidity grab — stop losses below the Previous Day Low have been triggered). On the 5-minute or 15-minute chart, you can see a clear Change of Character: a strong green candle that closes above the most recent micro-swing high. There is a bullish Order Block (the last red candle before that strong-up Change of Character move). The India Volatility Index is between 12 and 18.
Entry
Wait for a pullback DOWN into the bullish Order Block (or to the Fair Value Gap left by the Change of Character move). When price taps the Order Block AND a bullish confirmation candle (a hammer or a bullish engulfing) prints, BUY one lot of an At The Money Call Option of the current-week expiry. Use a Limit order at the planned entry price.
Stop Loss
5 to 10 points below the swept Previous Day Low (the stop must be BELOW the liquidity sweep wick). On the option premium itself, set a hard stop at 30 percent loss. Place a Stop-Loss-Market order on the broker.
Target
Target 1 = the Previous Day High (book 50 percent here, AND move the stop loss to your entry / break-even). Target 2 = the next higher-timeframe swing high (book 30 percent). Trail the rest with a stop at the previous swing low on the 15-minute chart. Exit by 2:30 PM IST regardless.
Skip if
The higher-timeframe (daily) trend is DOWN (you would be fighting the daily flow), OR there is no clean Change of Character (a strong-up candle that closes above the recent micro-swing high), OR the bullish Order Block is on the wrong side of the Equilibrium (above the 50 percent midline of the dealing range — Smart Money buys at Discount, not at Premium), OR the sweep happened on very low volume (a true Smart Money sweep typically has a clear volume signature).
