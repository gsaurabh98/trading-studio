STOP LOSS & TARGETS THE ONLY THING THAT SURVIVES YOU

The uncomfortable truth: over 12 months, 80% of retail losses come from not exiting at SL. It is not wrong setups. It is not wrong strikes. It is hope — "maybe it will come back". This page exists to replace hope with mechanical rules.
STEP 1 · THE 4 TYPES OF STOP LOSS
Every option trade has 4 possible stop-loss mechanisms. Pick ONE per trade based on the setup, and stick to it. Mixing them mid-trade is the most common cause of blown stops.
1 PRICE SL (Chart-based)
What it is: A stop-loss placed on the underlying index level, not the option price. When Nifty touches X, you exit the option, whatever the premium.

How to set it:
CE (call): Just below the wick low of the breakout / signal candle
PE (put): Just above the wick high of the breakdown / signal candle
Add 2–3 points buffer so minor wicks don't trigger you

Best for: Breakout trades, S/R reversals, chart-pattern trades — anywhere the "structure" defines the invalidation level clearly.
Avoid when: Setup has no clear structural invalidation (e.g., scalps), or SL distance > 0.5% of index (too big for options).
2 PREMIUM SL (Percentage-based)
What it is: A stop-loss on the option premium itself. Set as a % of what you paid.

The 30–40% rule:
Bought at ₹100 → SL at ₹60–₹70
Bought at ₹80 → SL at ₹48–₹56
Never risk >50% of premium on any trade

Why 30–40%: Below 30% the stop is too tight — routine volatility triggers it. Above 40% the loss compounds too fast if you take multiple trades in a row.

Best for: Scalps, ATM intraday trades, momentum plays where the underlying level is moving too fast to set a chart-based stop.
Avoid when: IV is unstable (event days) — premium moves on IV can trigger it even when direction is right.
3 TIME SL (Clock-based)
What it is: A hard rule that if the trade hasn't worked in N minutes, you exit regardless of premium or structure.

Recommended time limits:
5-min setup → exit in 20–30 minutes if not moving
15-min setup → exit in 45–60 minutes
Expiry day → exit in 15–25 minutes (theta punishes waiting)

Why it works: Theta (time decay) is invisible but relentless. An ATM option can lose ₹30–₹50 per hour of non-movement even when your chart stop hasn't been hit.

Best for: ALL option-buying trades as a safety net on top of Price or Premium SL. Use it in combination, not alone.
Avoid when: Swing / overnight positions (different theta dynamics).
4 VOLATILITY SL (ATR-based)
What it is: A stop-loss sized relative to current volatility using the ATR (Average True Range) indicator. Expands on wild days, tightens on calm days.

How to set it:
Read ATR(14) on the 15-min chart (e.g., Nifty ATR = 35 pts)
CE SL: Entry index level − (1.5 × ATR) → index − 52
PE SL: Entry index level + (1.5 × ATR)
On calm days ATR shrinks → SL gets automatically tighter

Best for: Swing / positional options, high-VIX days, BankNifty trades (where fixed SL is either too tight or too loose depending on the day).
Avoid when: Scalping — too slow to compute in real time. Stick to Price or Premium SL for quick trades.

STEP 3 · THE 4 WAYS TO SET A TARGET
1 FIXED RISK:REWARD
Target = N × Risk. Simplest, most mechanical.

Minimum 1:2 RR for option buying — anything less doesn't cover your losers over a month. Preferred: 1:2.5 to 1:3.

Example: Bought CE at ₹100, Premium SL at ₹70 (risk ₹30). → Target at ₹160 (reward ₹60, 1:2). Exit there without hesitation.
2 STRUCTURAL TARGET (S/R)
Target = the next meaningful resistance (for CE) or support (for PE) on your chart.

Often the previous day's high/low, next pivot (R1, R2), or a prior swing level.

Example: Nifty CE bought near VWAP. Next pivot R1 is 80 pts away. That is your target level — translate the index move into expected premium using Delta.
3 MEASURED MOVE (Pattern)
For chart patterns, the target is derived from the pattern's own geometry:

Flag: pole length projected from breakout
H&S: head-to-neckline distance from neckline break
Triangle: widest part projected from apex break
BB Squeeze: 2× band-width at breakout
4 TRAILING STOP-LOSS
No fixed target. Instead, move your SL with price and let the market take you out.

Methods:
Trail by ATR (1.5 × ATR below recent swing)
Trail by 20-EMA on 5-min chart
Trail by fixed % of premium once in profit

Best for strong-trend days when you want to ride the move. Combine with partial booking.
STEP 3 (CONT.) · READ THE CHART → PICK YOUR CE / PE TARGET
Step 3 above told you the four methods. This section shows you the where — how to literally look at the chart, find the right levels in the right order, and convert them into a usable premium target. Targets must live on the chart, not in your head.
CE TARGET HIERARCHY (ranked priority — pick in this order)
Once your CE is filled, scan the chart upward from your entry. The first level you hit becomes T1, the next becomes T2, the next becomes T3.

1. Today's day-high (if not yet broken) — price respects intraday extremes. Often the first reaction zone.
2. CPR R1 → R2 → R3 — classical pivot resistance. R1 is the most-watched; R2/R3 only valid on trend days.
3. Round numbers (24,000 · 24,500 · 25,000) — psychological magnets; large resting sell orders cluster here.
4. Prior-day high / weekly resistance — visible on every chart, watched by everyone.
5. Fibonacci extension 1.272 / 1.618 (drawn from the impulse leg) — targets beyond the prior swing.
6. VWAP rejection from above — if you're long below VWAP, VWAP is your first target. If above, it becomes a trail-stop.

Confluence rule: when 2+ levels stack within 10–15 pts, treat them as one fat target zone. Book aggressively there.
PE TARGET HIERARCHY (ranked priority — pick in this order)
Once your PE is filled, scan the chart downward from your entry. Same logic, mirrored.

1. Today's day-low (if not yet broken) — first downside reaction zone.
2. CPR S1 → S2 → S3 — S1 is the most-watched; S2/S3 only on trend-down days.
3. Round numbers — same psychology, downside.
4. Prior-day low / weekly support — visible to everyone.
5. Fibonacci extension 1.272 / 1.618 downward — targets beyond the prior swing-low.
6. VWAP rejection from below — if you're short above VWAP, VWAP is T1. If short below, it becomes a trail-stop.

Asymmetry to remember: downside moves on Indian indices are typically faster than upside (panic > greed). PE targets reach faster but reverse harder — book early, trail tight.
CE WORKED EXAMPLE — READING TARGETS OFF THE CHART
Setup: Nifty breaks above CPR top (24,015) on volume. You buy 24,000 ATM CE @ ₹90, Delta 0.50, lot 65.

Scan the chart upward — what's there?
• Day-high so far 24,080 (50 pts away)
• CPR R1 = 24,115 (85 pts away)
• Round number 24,150 (120 pts away)
• Prior-day high 24,200 (170 pts away — reserve, only on trend day)

Targets in premium terms (using Delta 0.50):
• T1 = ₹115 (90 + 50×0.5) — book 50%
• T2 = ₹132 (90 + 85×0.5) — book 30%, trail rest
• T3 = ₹150 (90 + 120×0.5) — trail with 1.5×ATR or 20-EMA

Net result if all three hit: Avg exit ~₹126 on ₹90 entry = +40% on the lot while max risk was capped at −30% premium SL. That's a 1:1.3 to 1:2 RR played correctly.
PE WORKED EXAMPLE — READING TARGETS OFF THE CHART
Setup: Nifty rejects CPR top (24,015) on a bearish-engulfing 15-min candle. You buy 24,000 ATM PE @ ₹85, Delta -0.50, lot 65.

Scan the chart downward — what's there?
• Day-low so far 23,950 (50 pts away)
• CPR S1 = 23,916 (84 pts away)
• Round number 23,900 (100 pts away)
• Prior-day low 23,850 (150 pts away — reserve)

Targets in premium terms (using |Delta| 0.50):
• T1 = ₹110 (85 + 50×0.5) — book 50%
• T2 = ₹127 (85 + 84×0.5) — book 30%, trail rest
• T3 = ₹135 (85 + 100×0.5) — trail with 1.5×ATR or 20-EMA

Speed warning: downside moves arrive 30–50% faster than the equivalent upside. T1 and T2 may both hit in the same 15-min candle on a panic day — have your booking orders pre-placed, don't watch live.
CONVERTING INDEX POINTS → OPTION PREMIUM TARGET
THE 1-LINE FORMULA
Once you've picked your chart target in index points, convert it to an expected premium using Delta:

Expected Premium @ Target
= Entry Premium + (chart_pts × |Delta|) − (Theta × hours_held / 6.5)
Why each piece matters:
• Delta tells you how much the option moves per 1-pt index move
• Theta bleeds value while you hold — subtract it
• 6.5 = full trading hours per session (09:15–15:30)

Quick mental shortcut: ATM (Δ≈0.5) → option moves ≈ half the index. Slightly ITM (Δ≈0.65) → option moves ≈ 2/3 of the index. Deep ITM (Δ≈0.85) → option moves ≈ 85% of the index.
WORKED EXAMPLE — FULL ROUND TRIP
Entry: 24,000 CE bought at ₹90 at 10:30 AM. Δ = 0.50, Theta = -₹8/day, IV stable.
Chart target: day-high 24,080 → index move = +80 pts
Expected hold: ~2 hours (move it by 12:30 PM)

Apply the formula:
• Delta gain = 80 × 0.50 = +₹40
• Theta cost = 8 × (2/6.5) = −₹2.5
• Net move = +40 − 2.5 = +₹37.5

Premium target = ₹90 + ₹37.5 ≈ ₹127

This is why you place the limit-sell at ₹127 the moment your entry fills — not at ₹130 (greedy) and not at ₹120 (leaves money on table). The chart told you 80 pts; Delta told you that's worth ~₹37; Theta took its cut. Mechanical, not emotional.
DO THIS
• Mark T1, T2, T3 on the chart before entry — not after
• Place limit-sell orders for each tranche immediately after fill
• Use Delta to convert index targets to premium targets
• Adjust target to the nearest level — never further
• Treat round numbers as targets, not stops
• Move SL to entry once T1 fills (locks in breakeven)
DON'T DO THIS
• Pick a target by "feel" without a chart level supporting it
• Set the target at the absolute extreme (prior-day high) — price rarely tags the exact tick
• Skip T1 to "let it run" — you'll round-trip
• Move target further after price approaches it (greed move)
• Forget theta — a 4-hour hold can eat ₹20 of premium
• Hold past T3 hoping for a "moonshot" with 100% of the position
TIME-OF-DAY ADJUSTMENT
Targets behave differently across the session:

9:30–11:30 AM: targets reach fastest — full T1/T2 hierarchy works
12:00–13:30 PM: lunch chop. Book at T1 only, don't trail T2/T3
14:00–14:30 PM: often a strong directional leg — trail aggressively
14:30–15:15 PM: close-out volatility. Book everything by 15:00.
Expiry day: halve all targets. Theta crush makes T2/T3 rarely worth waiting for.
STEP 4 · THE PARTIAL-BOOKING FRAMEWORK
You rarely need to be 100% right or 100% wrong. Book profits in stages. This single habit converts "right but exited too early" and "right but exited too late" into consistent compounding.
Price action Action Leftover position New SL
Entry → +1R (1:1) Book 50% 50% running Move SL to entry (breakeven)
+1R → +2R (1:2) Book 25% 25% running Trail SL to +1R
+2R → +3R (1:3) Book 15% 10% running Trail SL to +2R
+3R and beyond Trail aggressively 10% moonshot 1.5× ATR trail
After booking 50% at 1:1 + moving SL to entry — your worst-case outcome is a small winner, not a loss. This is the single most underrated P&L move in retail option trading.
STEP 5 · MANDATORY TIME-BASED EXITS
Time (IST) Trade type Mandatory action Why
14:30 PM Any directional option buy (non-expiry day) Review — book or trail tight Low-volume window, reversals common
14:30 PM Expiry-day trade (any strike) EXIT. No exceptions. Theta + pin-risk + operator games
15:00 PM All long option positions EXIT Pre-close volatility, last 30-min tax
15:15 PM Everything still open MARKET-OUT ORDER Liquidity dries up in final 15 min
Pre-major event Intraday positions Flatten 30 min before release IV crush post-event is guaranteed
STEP 6 · HOW TO PLACE STOPS ON YOUR BROKER
A "mental stop-loss" is not a stop-loss. Always place the order in the system. Emotion overrides memory every single time.
SL-M (Stop Loss Market)
How it works: Trigger price → automatically fires a MARKET order at that moment.

Pros: Guaranteed to exit. No stuck orders.
Cons: Market execution can slip ₹2–₹10 on illiquid strikes.

Use for: All long option positions — you want OUT, not a better price.
SL-L (Stop Loss Limit)
How it works: Trigger price → fires a LIMIT order at a price you specify.

Pros: Control over exit price.
Cons: In a fast move, your limit may never fill — you get stuck holding a losing position.

Avoid for options. The small slippage saving is not worth the risk of getting skipped.
BRACKET ORDER (BO)
How it works: Entry + SL + Target as ONE linked order. When any leg fires, the others cancel automatically.

Pros: Full automation — you literally cannot forget to exit.
Cons: Not available for option buying on most Indian brokers post-2020 regulation.

Status (2026): Mostly gone. Use the equivalent "cover order" or place SL + Target GTT separately.
GTT / AMO / SPLIT ORDERS
Place the SL-M order immediately after entry — don't wait 30 seconds to "see how it moves". Every second unprotected is emotional exposure.

Target can be a second limit order at the profit level. When SL hits, manually cancel the target (or use your broker's OCO feature).
STEP 7 · THE 6 COMMON SL/TARGET MISTAKES
MENTAL STOP LOSS
"I'll exit if it hits X." You won't. At X your brain invents reasons to wait. Always place the order in the system immediately after entry.
MOVING SL FURTHER
"It's close to SL — let me move it down 10 pts." This is the trade equivalent of running a red light. SL only moves one way: in your favour. Never against.
TOO-TIGHT SL
Using a ₹10 SL on a ₹100 premium gets you stopped by spread alone. Minimum SL: 25–30% of premium or 1.5× the average 5-min candle range.
NO TARGET
Riding winners indefinitely leads to round-trips (profit → loss). Always define the first booking level before entry, even if you plan to trail the rest.
BREAKEVEN TOO EARLY
Moving SL to entry at +₹5 profit gets you stopped on normal noise. Wait for +1R before moving SL to breakeven — earn the right to be stopped at zero.
AVERAGING A LOSER
"The premium is cheaper now, let me add." This is the single biggest account killer in retail options. You never average a losing option position. Ever.
STEP 8 · WORKED EXAMPLE — ONE FULL TRADE
SETUP: Nifty 15-min ORB breakout. OR = 24,400–24,440. Price breaks 24,440 at 9:32 AM with 1.8× volume.

1 ENTRY: Buy Nifty 24,450 CE at ₹82. Lot size 65. Cost: ₹82 × 65 = ₹5,330 per lot.

2 DEFINE RISK BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE:
Price SL chosen: Below 24,400 (OR low − 3 pts = 24,397)
Expected premium at SL (using Delta 0.45): ₹82 − (43 × 0.45) = approx ₹63
Premium-SL sanity check: ₹63 / ₹82 = 23% drop — within 30% rule
Risk per lot: (₹82 − ₹63) × 65 = ₹1,235
Time SL safety net: 30 minutes (exit at 10:02 if nothing happens)

3 TARGETS (FIXED 1:2 RR):
T1 = Entry + 1R = ₹82 + (₹19 × 1) = ₹101 (book 50%)
T2 = Entry + 2R = ₹82 + (₹19 × 2) = ₹120 (book 25%, trail rest)

4 PLACE THE ORDERS:
Buy 1 lot CE @ ₹82 (limit order) — FILLED
Immediately: Sell 1 lot CE @ ₹63 as SL-M, trigger 24,397 on index
Target 1: Sell 0.5 lot (if split-allowed) @ ₹101 limit

5 TRADE PROGRESSES:
9:45 → Nifty at 24,475, premium ₹98. T1 approaching. Hold.
9:52 → Premium hits ₹101. Book 50% at +1R. MOVE SL TO ENTRY (₹82).
Worst case from here: breakeven on remaining 50%. Best case: continues to T2.
10:08 → Premium hits ₹120. Book another 25%. Trail SL to ₹101.
10:22 → Premium peaks at ₹135, then drops to ₹101. SL hits. Exit remaining 25%.

NET OUTCOME (per lot):
50% booked at ₹101 = +₹19 × 32.5 = +₹617
25% booked at ₹120 = +₹38 × 16.25 = +₹617
25% trail-exit at ₹101 = +₹19 × 16.25 = +₹308
Total: +₹1,542 per lot (28.9% ROI in 50 minutes)

NOTE: You did NOT pick the top (₹135). You let the market take you out. This is the correct outcome. Trying to sell the exact peak is the single most expensive habit in retail trading.

SL & TARGETS CHEAT SHEET

  • Pick ONE SL type per trade (Price / Premium / Time / Volatility) — and pair it with Time SL as a safety net.
  • Always place the SL order in the system immediately after entry. Mental stops are not stops.
  • Minimum 1:2 RR on every option-buying trade. 1:3 is preferred.
  • Book 50% at 1:1, move SL to entry. This single move converts hope-trades into guaranteed non-losers.
  • Use SL-M, not SL-L, for option exits. Certainty beats a few rupees of slippage.
  • Flat by 15:00 for non-expiry, 14:30 for expiry. The last hour is retail's graveyard.
  • Never move SL against you. Never average a loser. Never skip the stop. Three rules. Print them. Read them every morning.
